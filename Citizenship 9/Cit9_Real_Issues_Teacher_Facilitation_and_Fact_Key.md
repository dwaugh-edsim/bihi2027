### The Math Key (Docket §1)

| Task | Work | Answer | Teaching Point |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| (a) | $1,938 ÷ $16.75 | **≈ 116 hours/month** (callout says ~116) | Rent at min wage = ~3 full work-weeks, every month, before food or bus |
| (b) | $1,938 ÷ $2,900 | **≈ 67%** of full-time gross pay | CMHC says 30% is affordable — they're at more than double |
| (c) | $1,938×12 ÷ 0.30 = $77,520/yr ÷ 2,080 hrs | **≈ $37.27/hr** (callout says ~$37) | The "affordable rent wage" is more than 2× minimum wage |
| — | Part-time check: 15 hrs/wk × 4.33 × $16.75 | ≈ $1,088/mo gross — **56% of rent** | A normal teen part-time job cannot come close to a 1-bedroom |

*(Slide 1 gives full-time as "about $2,900/month" = $16.75 × 40 hrs × 4.33 wks, rounded.)*

### The Three Positions — What Each One Really Is (Docket §3)

- **A — Build. Everywhere.** The supply-side / YIMBY position, aligned with where HRM policy has actually been moving (4-unit rule, Dec 2025 planning changes, HAF pressure to hit 2,600 units). Strongest honest version: scarcity is the price-setter (connect to the 2.7% vacancy card); weakest point: "supply" that lands in 5 years doesn't shelter the 1,150 tonight.
- **B — Protect. Plan first.** The position many residents actually took at HRM consultation meetings. Strongest version: infrastructure/sewer/school capacity is real and consultations exist for a reason; weakest point: delay has a body count — the PiT curve went 220 → 586 → 1,132 while plans were made.
- **C — Public land, non-market.** The social-housing position. Strongest version: permanently affordable rents, no neighbourhood upzoning fight; weakest point: governments build slowly and rarely at scale, and "owned land" near transit is limited.

**There is no correct answer.** The formative goal is evidence-cited justification (Exit Q3 tests whether they engaged the *counter*-argument).

## 🎤 Deputation Open Mic — Chair Protocol & What To Listen For

- Teacher = Chair/Mayor. Manage the 90-second timer strictly; the timer is what makes notes-reading feel safe and normal.
- Expect 6–8 speakers in 12 minutes. Use position lottery if one side dominates (one A, one B, one C, repeat).
- Listen-for rubric (mental, formative):
  - **Evidence use:** did they cite a number from the sheet (116 hrs, 1,150, 2.7%, $202, 4-units)? That's the bar.
  - **Aim:** did the "ask" go to the right level of government (§4)? Wrong-level asks are your diagnostic data for the Three Levels block.
  - **Pushback handling:** when a councillor challenges, do they concede anything? Conceding-while-holding is elite citizenship.
- Gallery question rule: attack the *idea*, never the person. Enforce it out loud once, early.

## ✂️ Exit Check Slip Answer Key (§7)

* **Q1 (hours per month's rent):** **≈116 hours** (accept 110–120; $1,938 ÷ $16.75 = 115.7).
* **Q2 (who to contact):** **A city councillor (HRM Council)** — zoning is municipal. Accept "the Mayor of HRM." Do NOT accept MP/MLA without challenge — this is the bridge diagnostic into the Three Levels lesson.
* **Q3 (the counter-argument):** Credit any genuine engagement with the *opposite* position ("Position B made me doubt A because delay hurts the 1,150 now"). An answer that only restates their own side earns re-do at lunch — gently.
* **Q4 (hardest of the three moves + why worth it):** Open. Listen for honesty (speaking was hardest, by far, for most) and any articulation of "because decisions get made without us if we stay quiet."
