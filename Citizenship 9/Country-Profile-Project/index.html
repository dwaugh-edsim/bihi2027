Country Profile Capstone

One country. One global issue. What did they do about it, what happened as a result, and why should Canadians care?

Evaluate the consequences of action and inaction as twenty-first century global citizens.

Focus indicators: Universal Declaration of Human Rights • global climate and economic interconnectedness • Canada's military, peacekeeping, and diplomatic role abroad.

The Task

What you are building

In pairs, you will build a 6-slide profile of one country (not Canada) and how it has responded, or failed to respond, to one global issue. Then you deliver a 5-6 minute presentation. Every claim on every slide needs a source.

Slide 1

Title

Country, issue, both names, class. One strong image and a title that names the issue, not just the country.

Slide 2

Country Background

Where it is, who lives there, how they govern, how people make a living. Three or four sentences maximum. A map earns its place here.

Slide 3

The Global Issue

What the issue is, why it crosses borders, and how it hits your country specifically. Real numbers beat adjectives.

Slide 4

Action or Inaction

The core of the project. What has the government actually DONE? Laws, spending, treaties, enforcement. Promises are not actions; find the follow-through.

Slide 5

Consequences

Intended and unintended, short-term and long-term, and who is affected. Use the consequence map in the Build Guide.

Slide 6

Canada Comparison + Reflection

What has Canada done on the same issue? Same approach or different, and why? Then answer: why should Canadians care?

Worth knowing

You may add up to 2 extra slides after Slide 6 if they earn their place: a timeline, a photo gallery, a key quote with its source. Extra slides of decoration do not earn marks. The sources slide (APA, every source you used) is required and does not count against your six.

    The consequence map (Slide 5's engine)

    For every action or inaction you found, run it through five questions. Two or three strong lines beat five vague ones.

    • Intended: was this the result they wanted?
    • Unintended: what happened that nobody planned for?
    • Short-term: what is happening now or soon?
    • Long-term: will this still matter in 10+ years?
    • Who carries it: citizens? the environment? the economy? a neighbouring country?
    Worked example

    Sea wall in Tuvalu: intended = flood protection, and it worked. Unintended = the wall redirected wave energy, so the unprotected beach next door eroded faster. Short-term = construction jobs, safer houses. Long-term = a wall cannot stop the ocean rising forever; who pays for the next one? Who carries it = families on the eroding side, and the whole country if the money runs out.

    The most common mistake

    Writing an encyclopedia page about the country and the problem, then forgetting the project question: what did the country DO, and what happened because of it? Slide 4 is the heart. If your Slide 4 is thinner than your Slide 2, the project is inside-out.

    What counts as a credible source

    UsableNot usable on its own
    Government sites (canada.ca, gov.uk, un.org, official statistics)Blogs and posts with no named author
    Established newsrooms with named journalists (BBC, Reuters, CBC, The Guardian, APTN)Random videos with no source, AI-generated answer pages
    UN agencies and reports (UNHCR, UNEP, WHO, UNICEF)Wikipedia itself as a final citation
    Research bodies (OECD, World Bank, ILO, IPCC)Advocacy pages used without checking their evidence
    About Wikipedia

    Wikipedia is a fine starting point: read it, then scroll to the bottom, open the citation it used, and cite THAT source. You get a stronger citation and you can see whether the claim holds up.

    Citation rules for this project

    • 2+ APA citations at the bottom of every slide that carries information.
    • Images you did not take need a credit line too (a short "Photo: [creator], Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA" line is enough).
    • Cite as you go, not the night before. The builder below makes it take 30 seconds.
    • Citation generators (MyBib, Scribbr, BibGuru) are allowed and encouraged; check what they produce.

    How it is marked (Level descriptors)

    Five criteria: issue analysis, country response, Canada comparison, reflection depth, citation rigor. Presentations are marked on the same five, plus one question in the conference: can both partners talk to every slide?

    LevelWhat it looks like
    4 • ExcellentSpecific, current, sourced. Slide 4 separates actions from promises. Consequences run the full map. The Canada comparison finds a real difference and explains it. Citations are correct APA on every slide.
    3 • ProficientClear and complete on all six slides. Some depth missing (consequences stay obvious, comparison stays general). Most citations present, small APA flaws.
    2 • DevelopingBackground heavy, response thin. Some slides lack sources or use weak ones. Comparison says "both care about it" without evidence. Bullets read like paragraphs.
    1 • BeginningCountry described, project question not yet answered. Missing slides, missing citations. Fixable at a checkpoint conference: come talk to me.
