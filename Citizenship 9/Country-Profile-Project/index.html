What you are building (6 slides)

Slide 1

Title

Country, issue, both names, class. One strong image and a title that names the issue, not just the country.

Slide 2

Country Background

Where it is, who lives there, government type, how people earn a living. 3-4 short bullets max + a map.

Slide 3

The Global Issue

What the issue is, why it crosses borders, and how it hits your country specifically. Real numbers beat adjectives.

Slide 4Core Slide

Action or Inaction

The heart of the project. What has the government actually DONE? Laws, spending, treaties, enforcement. Promises are not actions.

Slide 5

Consequences

Intended and unintended, short-term and long-term, and who carries the cost (see the 5-lens map in Build Guide).

Slide 6

Canada Comparison + Reflection

What has Canada done on the same issue? Same or different, and why? Then answer: why should Canadians care?

Build Guide

Slide by slide, then the thinking tools

Slide planner (click a slide to inspect)

Slide 1

Title

What belongs on it: Country, global issue, both partner names, your class, and one strong, credited image.

Pro tip: Name the tension in your title. "Tuvalu and Sea Level Rise: Buying Time on Sinking Land" beats "Tuvalu Project".

Starter you can rewrite

"[Country] and [issue]: what they did, and what it cost."

Slide 2

Country Background

What belongs on it: Continent and neighbours, main languages, government type, how people earn a living, and a map. Keep it to 3-4 short bullets total.

Watch out: Do not spend half your talk on fun facts. Background sets up the issue; it is not the main event.

Starter you can rewrite

"[Country] is a [government type] of about [population] people, most of whom..."

Slide 3

The Global Issue

What belongs on it: Define the issue clearly, show why it crosses borders, and show how it hits YOUR country hardest or differently using one or two real, sourced numbers.

Pro tip: If your topic is a human-rights issue, name the exact UDHR article here.

Starter you can rewrite

"This is a global issue because... In [country], it shows up as..."

Slide 4 (Core)

Action or Inaction

What belongs on it: What the government actually DID: laws passed, budgets spent, treaties signed, and whether they were enforced (or ignored).

Watch out: Separate promises from follow-through. A speech or a target for 2040 is not an action unless money or law backs it up.

Starter you can rewrite

"In [year], the government passed / funded / refused to..."

Slide 5

Consequences

What belongs on it: Intended vs unintended results, short-term vs long-term impact, and who carries the cost. Use the 5-lens map below.

Pro tip: Naming one real unintended consequence separates a Level 4 slide from a Level 2 slide.

Starter you can rewrite

"The plan was meant to... Instead, ..."

Slide 6

Canada Comparison + Reflection

What belongs on it: What Canada did on the SAME issue (from your Response Brief). Same or different, and one concrete reason why. Then answer: why should Canadians care?

Pro tip: Connect it to Nova Scotia or a TNC Canadians buy from, in your own words.

Starter you can rewrite

"Canada took a [similar/different] approach because... Canadians should care because..."

Final Slide

APA Sources

What belongs on it: Every source you used, in alphabetical APA format, plus image credits. Required, and does not count against your six slides.

Pro tip: Use the APA builder in the Tools & Rubric tab to stack and copy your alphabetized list.

Quick rule

2+ APA citations at the bottom of every content slide, plus the full list on your Sources slide.

The 5-Lens Consequence Map (Slide 5)

1. Intended

Was this the result they wanted?

Tuvalu exampleFlood protection behind the sea wall, and it worked.
2. Unintended

What happened that nobody planned for?

Tuvalu exampleWave energy bounced sideways, eroding the next beach faster.
3. Short-Term

What is happening right now or soon?

Tuvalu exampleConstruction jobs and safer houses during king tides.
4. Long-Term

Will this still matter in 10+ years?

Tuvalu exampleWalls cannot stop rising oceans forever. Who pays next?
5. Who Carries It

Citizens, environment, economy, or neighbours?

Tuvalu exampleFamilies on the eroding side, and the whole nation if funding runs out.
Rights lens (Slides 3, 4 and 6)

Name the UDHR articles your issue touches, the treaties the country signed, and the Charter section answering the same right in Canada. "Both countries care" becomes "both promised Article X; here is what each actually did."

TNC lens (Transnational Corporations)

Cards tagged TNC tie the national issue to a global company (like Canadian giant Loblaw / Joe Fresh in Bangladesh, or Nestle in Indonesia). Naming the TNC makes Slide 6's "why should Canadians care?" concrete.

The most common mistake

Writing an encyclopedia page about the country and the problem, then forgetting the project question: what did the country DO, and what happened because of it? If your Slide 4 is thinner than Slide 2, the project is inside-out.

What counts as a credible source

✓ Usable sources

  • Government sites (canada.ca, gov.uk, un.org, official statistics bureaus)
  • Established newsrooms with named journalists (BBC, Reuters, CBC, The Guardian, APTN)
  • UN agencies and reports (UNHCR, UNEP, WHO, UNICEF, OCHA)
  • Research bodies (OECD, World Bank, ILO, IPCC, International Crisis Group)

✕ Not usable on its own

  • Blogs and social posts with no named author or editor
  • Random videos with no source, or AI-generated answer pages
  • Wikipedia itself as a final citation (follow its footnotes instead!)
  • Advocacy pages used without checking their underlying evidence

    Five criteria: issue analysis, country response, Canada comparison, reflection depth, and citation rigor. Plus one conference question: can both partners talk to every slide?

    Specific, current, sourced. Slide 4 separates actions from promises. Consequences run the full map. Canada comparison explains a real difference. Correct APA on every slide.

    Clear and complete on all six slides. Some depth missing (consequences stay obvious, comparison stays general). Most citations present with small APA flaws.

    Background heavy, response thin. Some slides lack sources or use weak ones. Comparison says "both care about it" without evidence. Bullets read like paragraphs.

    Country described, project question not yet answered. Missing slides or citations. Fixable at a checkpoint conference: come talk to me early.
