Rights Quick Reference

The UN, the UDHR, and the Charter on one page. Keep it beside your research: every human-rights topic in the catalog runs through these three names.

Outcome (verbatim from the curriculum)

Evaluate the consequences of action and inaction as twenty-first century global citizens.

The three names, in one line each

NameWhat it isIts teeth (enforcement)
United NationsClub of 193 countries, founded 1945 after WWII (Canada is a founding member). General Assembly (1 vote each) + Security Council (15 members; 5 permanent members hold a veto, meaning 1 "no" blocks action).Mostly pressure: public reports, peacekeeping, and sanctions (trade/money penalties). Cannot force a country.
Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR, 1948)30 articles listing what every human is owed. Drafting chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt. Most translated document on Earth.None of its own. A declaration (statement of values), not a legally binding treaty: it is the measuring stick, not a court.
Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms (1982)Part of Canada's Constitution (highest law in Canada). Protects people against government action (laws, police, public schools), not private arguments.Real legal teeth: courts strike down laws that break it. Rights can still be reasonably limited (s. 1) or temporarily overridden (s. 33).

UDHR articles you will actually use

ArticleWhat it says (plain words)
1Born free and equal in dignity and rights.
2No discrimination: the whole list applies to everyone.
3Life, liberty, and personal safety (freedom from harm or unfair arrest).
5No torture or cruel punishment, ever.
14Right to flee danger and seek safety (asylum) in another country.
19Freedom of opinion, expression, and information.
26Right to education, free at least in the early years.

Charter sections you will actually use

SectionWhat it says (plain words)
s. 2Fundamental freedoms: conscience, religion, thought, expression, peaceful protest (assembly), joining groups.
s. 7Life, liberty, and personal safety: government cannot take them away unfairly.
s. 15Equality before and under the law, without discrimination.
s. 1Reasonable limits: government can limit a right if proven fair and necessary in a democracy (e.g., hate-speech laws).
s. 33Notwithstanding clause: a government can temporarily pause s. 2, 7, or 15 for up to 5 years at a time.
CRCBonus treaty: UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989), officially approved (ratified) by every UN member except the US.
Rights lens for your project

For your topic, answer three questions and cite them: 1) Which UDHR articles does this issue touch? 2) What has the country promised (treaties signed)? 3) What has it actually done? Canada check: which Charter section answers the same right here, and what is Canada's record?
