# Knowledge Keeper / Elder Visit — Outreach Pack (Class 21, Nov 5–9 window)

**⚠️ TIME-CRITICAL:** The outline's most time-critical external dependency. The visit lands **Nov 5–9** (Class 21, all sections) and Mi'kmaw organizations ask for **6+ weeks of lead time — outreach emails must go out this week.** Sending in the first week of September was the target; every week of delay shrinks the fallback options (Zoom visit, recorded address + structured response).

**Ask:** A Mi'kmaw Knowledge Keeper, Elder, or community educator to visit three Grade 9 Citizenship 9 classes (~60 min each) to speak about Mi'kmaw citizenship, the treaty relationship, and what reconciliation asks of everyone — with student Q&A prepared in advance.

---

## 1. Who to contact (in order)

| Priority | Organization | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mi'kmaw Native Friendship Centre** (Halifax) — education/community programming | Guest speakers, cultural programming; the outline's recommended first call |
| 2 | **Mi'kmaw Kina'matnewey** (Mi'kmaw education authority) | Mi'kmaw-led education resources; may direct to a Knowledge Keeper or educator |
| 3 | **School board's Indigenous Education lead / supports team** | Board protocol, honorarium process, and whether a board-affiliated speaker exists |
| 4 | **Treaty Education NS (TENS) / Union of Nova Scotia Indians (UNSI)** | Treaty education resources; possible speaker referrals |
| 5 | A local Mi'kmaw community's education office (e.g., Sipekne'katik, Millbrook — nearest to the school) | Direct community invitation — **only via the community's own office, never cold-contacting an individual** |

**Etiquette rule:** invitations go through offices/organizations, not to individuals. If someone declines or doesn't reply in ~10 days, follow up once, then move to the next contact.

## 2. Draft outreach email (edit brackets before sending)

> **Subject:** Grade 9 Citizenship 9 — invitation to speak on Mi'kmaw citizenship & treaties (Nov 5–9)
>
> Wela'lioq / Hello,
>
> I teach Citizenship 9 — a compulsory Grade 9 course at Bicentennial Junior High (HRM). Our Unit 2, "Rights, Treaties & Truth and Reconciliation," opens with Mi'kmaw worldview (Netukulimk, the seven districts of Mi'kma'ki) and the Peace and Friendship Treaties of 1752 and 1760–61, taught with Mi'kmaw-authored sources.
>
> **We would be honoured to host a Mi'kmaw Knowledge Keeper, Elder, or community educator** for one class per section — three sessions of about 60 minutes each — during the week of **November 5–9** (flexible; we can fit your schedule that week or the following one). Students will have prepared respectful questions in advance using visit protocols, and no recording or photography would happen without explicit permission.
>
> We will follow your guidance on honorarium, gifts (tobacco or otherwise, as you direct), room setup, and any opening protocols. An in-person visit is ideal; a Zoom session is very welcome if travel is difficult.
>
> Would this be possible, or could you point us to someone in the community who sometimes visits schools?
>
> Wela'liek / Thank you,
> **Mr. Dave Waugh · Citizenship 9 · Bicentennial Junior High · Room 8**
> [phone] · [school email]

*(If your board has a preferred salutation or protocol wording, defer to it. Confirm honorarium expectations early — typical ranges for school visits exist; the board Indigenous Education team can advise.)*

## 4. Visit-day flow (60 min)

1. Teacher introduces the guest properly (name, community/role as they wish to be introduced); students already know the protocol basics
2. Guest speaks (~25–30 min): Mi'kmaw citizenship, treaty relationship, reconciliation
3. Student Q&A from prepared questions (~20 min) — students ask their prepared questions first, follow-ups only if invited
4. Thanks in the manner the guest indicates; teacher follows up with card + thank-you from the class

## 5. After the visit

- Thank-you notes from the class within the week (Class 22 opening)
- Reflection slip: "One thing I want to remember from the visit" (→ Civic Foundations portfolio)
- Log the visit for next year's planning: who, what worked, lead time actually needed
