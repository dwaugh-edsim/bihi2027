# Citizenship 9 — High-Engagement Debate & Deliberation Formats (Class Size: 28)

**Course:** Citizenship 9  
**Teacher:** Mr. Waugh &bull; Room 8 &bull; Bicentennial Junior High (2026–2027)  
**Challenge:** Standard 1-on-1 debates leave 24–26 students passive and disengaged in a roster of 28. These structures guarantee 100% active engagement, accountability, and differentiated speaking roles.

---

## 1. The Parliamentary Caucus & Rotating Hot Seat (Whole-Class)

### Structure
* **Class Split:** 2 Benches of 14 students (**Government** vs. **Official Opposition**).
* **Physical Layout:** Opposing rows facing each other.
* **Front Bench (Active Debaters):** 3 designated lead debaters (Prime Minister/Opposition Leader, Minister/Critic of Finance/Justice, Lead Whip) + **1 Empty "Hot Seat"** on each side.
* **Backbench (Research & Whip Caucus):** 10 students per side organized into Research Pairs (Evidence Verifiers, Rebuttal Drafters, Question Framers).

### Mechanics
1. **Opening Speeches (90s each):** Frontbench leads present foundational arguments.
2. **Caucus Strategy Time (2 min):** Backbenchers draft "Points of Order," cross-exam questions, and evidence cards, feeding them physically to the front bench.
3. **The Hot Seat Rule:** Any backbencher who wishes to deliver a direct 45-second intervention or cross-examination must step forward into the Hot Seat. Once they deliver their point and receive a response, they must yield the seat and rotate back to the caucus.
4. **Division (Vote):** Backbenchers vote by standing or electronic ballot based on argument quality and evidence, not just party loyalty.

### Why it Works for 28
* Eliminates passive listeners; backbenchers have clear production tasks (writing evidence slips).
* Lower-stakes speaking opportunity (45-second hot seat) for students intimidated by full formal speeches.

## 2. Simultaneous 4-Person Committee Pods (7 Parallel Debates)

### Structure
* **Class Split:** 7 independent pods of 4 students operating at their own tables simultaneously.
* **Pod Roles:**
  1. **Affirmative Advocate (1):** Delivers pro-motion argument with evidence (60s).
  2. **Negative Advocate (2):** Delivers counter-argument and rebuttal (60s).
  3. **Cross-Examiner (3):** Asks 2 probing questions to each side to test inconsistencies (90s).
  4. **Committee Chair / Justice (4):** Keeps strict stopwatch time, tracks evidence credibility, and writes a 1-paragraph ruling/verdict.

### Mechanics
* **Round 1 (8 min):** Motion #1 debated.
* **Rotation (1 min):** Roles shift clockwise within each pod.
* **Round 2 (8 min):** Motion #2 debated with new advocates and chairs.
* **Debrief (5 min):** Committee Chairs report split rulings to the whole class.

### Why it Works for 28
* Zero spectators. Every student speaks and listens closely in an intimate 4-person setting.
* Drastically reduces performance anxiety compared to speaking before the entire room.

## 3. Multi-Stakeholder Public Hearing / Royal Commission (6 Teams of 4–5)

### Structure
* Ideal for complex local and provincial dilemmas (e.g., HRM park encampments, Mi'kmaw fisheries, offshore wind vs. fishing grounds).
* **6 Stakeholder Delegations (4–5 students each):**
  1. **Municipal / Provincial Decision-Makers** (HRM Council / Crown Panel)
  2. **Directly Affected Community Group** (e.g., Unhoused Residents / Working Fishers)
  3. **Economic / Business Sector** (e.g., Downtown Halifax Business Commission / Industry Reps)
  4. **Public Advocacy / Civil Rights** (e.g., Legal Aid, Civil Liberties Observers)
  5. **Environmental / Scientific Experts** (e.g., Ecology Action Centre / Marine Biologists)
  6. **Indigenous / Mi'kmaw Leadership** (e.g., Assembly of NS Mi'kmaw Chiefs)

### Mechanics
1. **Preparation (15 min):** Teams receive stakeholder briefing cards and formulate a 2-minute position brief and 3 non-negotiable policy demands.
2. **Hearing Phase (20 min):** Each group delivers their 2-minute testimony to the Council panel; other delegations submit written questions.
3. **Negotiation & Floor Amendments (10 min):** Delegations caucus to draft compromise clauses.
4. **Final Vote:** The Council panel renders an official policy resolution.

### Why it Works for 28
* Breaks away from false binary ("yes/no") simplifications into realistic governance and policy compromise.
* Teams of 4–5 allow collaborative peer research and distributed speaking roles.

## 4. The Silent / Written Speed-Dialogue Wall (Low-Anxiety, Deep Argumentation)

### Structure
* Paired desks with 1 shared debate canvas and 2 different colored pens.
* 100% silent protocol for the first 10 minutes.

### Mechanics
* **Round 1 (2 min):** Student A writes an affirmative claim and 1 piece of supporting evidence (Blue pen).
* **Round 2 (2 min):** Student B reads, circles assumptions, and writes a direct counter-argument below (Red pen).
* **Round 3 (2 min):** Student A replies to the specific objection and proposes a compromise (Blue pen).
* **Round 4 (2 min):** Student B concludes by evaluating what common ground was reached (Red pen).
* **Gallery Walk (5 min):** Students walk the room with sticky notes leaving peer evaluations on argument logic.

### Why it Works for 28
* Perfect for classes with high conversational volume or uneven participation.
* Trains students to dissect written logic and evidence rather than relying on vocal dominance.
