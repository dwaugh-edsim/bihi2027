Midnight Walk • May 28, 1971

  • Two teenagers: Donald Marshall Jr. (17) meets friend Sandy Seale (17) in Wentworth Park.

The Investigation That Never Happened

  • Donald walked into the station as a reporting witness with a knife wound on his arm.

He went to the police for protection. They put him in handcuffs.

ACT I • THE TRIAL & MAXIMUM SECURITY • 1971–1982
Tunnel Vision, Coerced Lies & 11 Lost Years

Fabricating a Murder Conviction

  • Police Tunnel Vision: Detectives actively ignored evidence pointing to Roy Ebsary.
  • Witness Intimidation: Police coerced and pressured teenage witnesses into signing false statements claiming they saw Donald do it.
  • An all-white jury took just minutes to convict 17-year-old Donald of life imprisonment.
Authorities actively buried evidence that proved Donald Marshall Jr.'s innocence.

11 Years in Maximum Security

  • Sent to maximum-security federal penitentiaries (Springhill and Dorchester).
  • Marshall spent 11 years—his entire youth from age 17 to 28—behind bars for a crime he never committed.
  • The real killer walked the streets of Nova Scotia as a free man for over a decade.
Marshall was only freed in 1982 after a witness confessed police forced them to lie.

ACT II • TRUTH & VINDICATION • 1989
1989: The Royal Commission Exposes Systemic Racism
Exoneration Archive • 1989
Press Conference: Donald Marshall Jr. speaks to national media following his exoneration. His courage forced Canada's first full inquiry into institutional racism.

The Royal Commission Verdict

  • Marshall was acquitted in 1983. In 1989, Nova Scotia completed a landmark Royal Commission inquiry.
  • Official Finding: The Commission concluded the justice system failed Donald Marshall Jr. at every single step due to systemic racism.
  • Proved racism was built into the institutional machinery of the police and courts.
  • Roy Ebsary was finally tried and convicted of manslaughter.
Sparked the creation of the Mi'kmaw Legal Support Network (MLSN) and restorative justice in NS.
SYSTEMIC REVELATION Racism wasn't just individual prejudice; it was built into the institutional procedures of the police and courts.
ACT II • NATIONAL LEGAL REFORM • 1999
Gladue Rights: Reforming Canadian Sentencing

The Gladue Decision (R. v. Gladue, 1999)

  • Following the Marshall Commission, Parliament amended the Criminal Code (Section 718.2(e)).
  • In 1999, the Supreme Court affirmed Gladue Rights as mandatory national law for all Canadian judges.
  • Born directly from the injustice suffered by Donald Marshall Jr.
A permanent transformation in Canadian criminal law born directly from Marshall's struggle.

What Canadian Judges Must Legally Do

  • 1. Consider Systemic Background: Judges *must* evaluate the unique history of colonialism, residential schools, systemic discrimination, and poverty.
  • 2. Seek Restorative Alternatives: Courts are legally required to look for community-based healing and alternatives before sending an Indigenous person to prison.
The law demands restorative healing rather than reflexive imprisonment.

ACT III • POMQUET HARBOUR, NS • AUGUST 1993
463 Pounds of Eels: The Federal Ambush

Fishing for Survival (August 1993)

  • Ten years after prison, Donald Marshall Jr. and his wife go out in a small boat on Pomquet Harbour.
  • They catch 463 pounds of adult eels using traditional nets.
  • Sold the catch to a South Carolina buyer for $787.10.
  • The money was used strictly for family survival: buying groceries and paying rent.
Marshall was not running a corporate fleet; he was feeding his family.

The Federal Ambush (DFO Swoop)

  • Department of Fisheries and Oceans (DFO) officers swoop in, seize his boat, nets, and eels.
  • 3 Federal Criminal Charges:
    • Fishing out of commercial season
    • Fishing without a federal licence
    • Using an illegal net
Under Canada's Fisheries Act, he was 100% guilty. His defence? A 233-year-old treaty.

ACT IV • THE TREATY OF 1760 • NATION-TO-NATION
Peace & Friendship — Not Conquest

What Is NOT in the Treaty

  • Zero Land Surrendered or Sold: There is no deed, purchase, or conquest clause in this treaty.
  • The Mi'kmaq never lost a war against the British Crown.
  • Unceded Territory: Nova Scotia and Mi'kma'ki remain legally unceded land.
This was a nation-to-nation commercial peace agreement, not a surrender.

The Legal Clash of 1993

  • DFO's Argument: The federal *Fisheries Act* applies to everyone in Canada. No one can fish without a federal permit.
  • Marshall's Argument: The 1760 Treaty is a constitutional guarantee that outranks federal ministry regulations.
Does a 233-year-old treaty beat modern federal fisheries law?

ACT V • SUPREME COURT OF CANADA • SEPT 17, 1999
The Acquittal: Treaties Outrank Federal Law

The Supreme Court Ruling (1999)

  • Donald Marshall Jr. is ACQUITTED of all criminal charges!
  • Section 35 of Canada's Constitution (1982) recognizes and affirms Aboriginal and treaty rights as supreme law.
  • The Court rules the 1760 Treaty is alive and legally binding today.
Historic treaty promises outrank federal Fisheries Act regulations.

The "Moderate Livelihood" Right

  • Justice Ian Binnie ruled that the Truckhouse clause protected an ongoing right to catch and trade fish for a “Moderate Livelihood”.
  • Defined as: Securing basic necessities—food, clothing, housing, and amenities.
  • First time in Canadian history that an Indigenous commercial harvesting right was upheld by the highest court.
Donald Marshall Jr. stood up twice against the justice system—and won both times.
CONSTITUTIONAL TRIUMPH The Supreme Court of Canada affirmed: Treaty rights are not historical artifacts; they are supreme constitutional law.
ACT V • THE FATAL FLAW & LIVE TALLY
The Fatal Flaw: The Undefined Dollar Trap

The Undefined Dollar Vacuum

  • The Court confirmed the right to a "moderate livelihood"... but never set a dollar figure, season limit, or boat quota!
  • Is a moderate livelihood $30,000? $50,000? $80,000? $100,000+?
  • Ottawa and DFO stalled and failed to negotiate clear regulations with Mi'kmaw communities.
Leaving the definition blank created a legal vacuum that both sides filled with conflict.

The Clashes: Burnt Church & St. Mary's Bay

  • 1999 Burnt Church (NB): DFO boats rammed Indigenous lobster skiffs; traps seized and destroyed.
  • 2020 St. Mary's Bay (NS): Sipekne'katik launched its own treaty fishery under *Netukulimk*. Non-Indigenous commercial fishers blockaded wharves and burned a lobster pound.
Ambiguity in law produces conflict on the water.

“We Are All Treaty People”

What Citizens Living in Mi'kma'ki Receive

  • The Mi'kmaq never lost a war of conquest and never surrendered land.
  • The Peace Treaties are what allow non-Indigenous citizens to live, build homes, go to school, and own property peacefully in Nova Scotia today.
  • Without the 1760 treaty, non-Indigenous presence has no legal peace agreement.
Treaties are not special privileges for Indigenous people; they are the peace pact for all of us.

What Treaty Partners Owe

  • Holding Government Accountable: Demanding that Ottawa honor Crown treaty promises rather than stalling.
  • Respecting Inherent Rights: Recognizing that treaty harvesting rights are constitutionally protected supreme law.
  • Active Reconciliation: Learning the real history and protecting shared lands, waters, and resources.
Reconciliation is an active civic duty, not an optional gesture.
