BEAT 3 • POMQUET HARBOUR • AUGUST 1993
The Eels: Federal Fisheries Act vs. 1760 Treaty

The Ambush & Federal Charges

  • Donald harvested 463 lbs of eels and sold them for $787.10 to pay rent and buy groceries.
  • Armed DFO officers seized his boat, net, and catch.
  • 3 Federal Criminal Charges: Closed season, unlicensed fishing, illegal net.
  • Crown Argument: "The Fisheries Act applies to all waters and all people equally. No special rights."
Under Canada's Fisheries Act regulations, Donald Marshall Jr. was guilty.

The 1760 Treaty Defence

  • Marshall walked into court holding the 1760 Treaty of Peace and Friendship signed at Fort Edward.
  • Article 4: Promised free hunting & fishing "as usual" and a "Truckhouse" to trade for "necessaries".
  • Section 35 of the 1982 Constitution: Recognizes and affirms treaty rights as supreme constitutional law.

BEAT 4 • SUPREME COURT OF CANADA • SEPT 17, 1999
The 1999 Legal Decision: R. v. Marshall
▶ PLAY NOTEBOOKLM VIDEO 4 (2 MIN)

The Constitutional Acquittal

  • Donald Marshall Jr. is ACQUITTED of all charges!
  • Supreme Court rules the 1760 Treaty is alive and binding under Section 35 of Canada's Constitution.
  • Confirms an inherent constitutional right to harvest and sell catch for a "Moderate Livelihood" (food, clothing, housing).
  • Affirms that Mi'kma'ki is unceded land (zero land was ever surrendered or sold).
Historic treaties outrank federal fisheries regulations.

The Fatal Flaw: The Undefined Dollar Trap

  • The Court never defined an exact dollar figure, boat quota, or calendar season for "moderate livelihood"!
  • Is it $30,000? $60,000? $100,000+?
  • The Court told Ottawa and First Nations to negotiate the rules.
  • Ottawa stalled for 21 years, leaving a dangerous legal vacuum.
Leaving the definition blank was a legal time-bomb waiting to explode.

BEAT 5 • MODERN CONSEQUENCE #1 • SEPTEMBER 2020
The 2020 St. Mary's Bay Lobster Riots

21 Years of Stalling Explodes

  • After 21 years of Ottawa refusing to negotiate, Sipekne'katik First Nation launched their own self-regulated livelihood lobster fishery.
  • Issued community licences outside the federal DFO season in St. Mary's Bay.
  • Commercial fleets blockaded Saulnierville wharf, fired flares at Indigenous boats, and cut hundreds of lobster traps.
  • A lobster holding pound in Middle West Pubnico was burned completely to the ground.
Ambiguity in federal law produced open violence on Nova Scotia waters.

The Legal Clash Today

  • Commercial Fishers: "Conservation! One rule and one season for all Canadians, or stocks collapse."
  • Mi'kmaw Fishers: "Sipekne'katik launched 250 traps; commercial fleets fish 350,000! Our constitutional treaty right cannot be delayed forever."
Traces directly back to the Supreme Court leaving "moderate livelihood" undefined in 1999.
