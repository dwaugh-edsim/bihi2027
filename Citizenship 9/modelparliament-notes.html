Citizenship 9 — Resource Notes

Model Parliament — Executive Summary

Source folder: resources/Unit_05_Governance/Model Parliament/  ·  Reviewed 2026-10-05  ·  Status: Archived — run in previous years
TL;DR: Model Parliament is a complete, previously-run mock-parliament unit: students form a government, two opposition parties, and parliamentary officials, prepare speeches on a Library of Parliament sample bill (Bill C-122, The Gasoline Preservation Act), and run a real parliamentary debate over two sessions with a sitting MP guest-speaking as Speaker. It was not scheduled for the current Cit 9 plan (the Bicentennial Mock Election already covers the Unit 5 simulation slot), but two of its pieces — the decision-making-spaces photo discussion and the LoP sample-bill library — are directly reusable with near-zero prep.

1. What it was

A multi-session project run at the school in previous years (junior-high level, English/ELA + Citizenship outcomes per the deck's speaker notes). The class simulated the House of Commons end-to-end: parties formed, leaders and ministers chosen, a government bill researched and debated, and a final vote — bookended by two dated sessions (January 14: Opening of Parliament; January 21: Government Bill from First Reading to Royal Assent) with Hon. Darren Fisher, MP (Dartmouth—Cole Harbour) attending as guest Speaker of the House. It was run through Google Classroom.

The bill used, Bill C-122 The Gasoline Preservation Act, is one of the Library of Parliament's Model Parliament Unit sample bills — a family of ready-made classroom bills that also includes the Youth Electors Act and the National Essential Services Act. In other words, this kit and the newer Veggie-Bill committee simulation come from the same publisher's catalogue.

3. How it ran (reconstructed from the deck)

Hook — "How do groups decide?"

Nine annotated photos of decision-making spaces, each with speaker-note discussion prompts: the House of Commons, a corporate boardroom, the UK House of Lords, the Nunavut Legislative Assembly (consensus chamber), a school staff room, a library meeting room, the Blue Quills sharing circle (Alberta), Province House (NS House of Assembly), and the Membertou First Nation band council chamber. Guiding questions: What can we learn about a group by looking at how they meet? Is there a right or wrong way to make decisions? Student-question examples are captured in the notes (e.g., "Why isn't there any furniture?" on the sharing circle; "It looks like the other green picture but smaller" on Province House).

Low-stakes practice

Scenario: the administration wants a fifth school rule — groups run a PMI (Plus/Minus/Interesting) analysis, then a whole-class Four Corners debate on the warm-up statements. This rehearses position-taking before the real bill.

The bill and roles

  • Government (11): PM, Ministers of Environment & Climate Change, Natural Resources, Transport, and Innovation/Science/Industry, plus 6 MPs including a Government House Leader.
  • Opposition (10): Official Opposition (leader + 5 MPs, two shadow ministers) and a Third Party (leader + 3 MPs, one critic).
  • Officials (2 + teacher + guest): Speaker, Clerk, Sergeant-at-Arms, Page — with the guest MP presiding over the real sessions.

Preparation and the debate

  • Prep day: party position on C-122, evidence gathering in research groups (shared docs, bullet points), speaker selection.
  • Homework speech assignments, differentiated by role: PM and Environment Minister — 3-minute speeches for; Opposition leader and shadow ministers — 2-minute speeches against; one backbencher per non-government party carries a proposed amendment; every other MP gives a 1-minute statement on an issue of personal interest.
  • Debate prep: anticipate the other side's arguments, prepare counter-evidence; slide guidance on presenting with diagrams, tables, maps, and data.
  • Sessions: Opening of Parliament (Jan 14), then the full bill passage — first reading through Royal Assent (Jan 21).

4. Value for the current Cit 9 course

Not a scheduling candidate. Unit 5's simulation slot is taken by the Bicentennial Mock Election (Classes 53–56), and the Veggie-Bill committee sim (see cit9-veggie-bill-committee-sim.md) covers the legislative-stage experience. Running Model Parliament as-is would duplicate both and cost 3–4 classes we don't have.

But three components are directly salvageable:

  • The decision-making-spaces photo discussion — the strongest single asset. It's a ready-made opener for "how groups and governments make decisions," and it builds cultural breadth the current deck lacks: Westminster confrontation, Nunavut's consensus model, a Mi'kmaw band council chamber, and a Black-footed/ nehiyawak-style sharing circle side by side. It would slot naturally into the Unit 5 worldview→values→policy runway (around Classes 50–52) or as the pre-election framing, with the same care as the rest of the Indigenous content — discuss, don't role-play.
  • The LoP sample-bill catalogue (Youth Electors Act, National Essential Services Act) — pre-written, age-appropriate bills. If the Veggie-Bill committee sim runs and the cohort wants more, a second bill from the same catalogue re-skins the whole activity with zero authoring.
  • The differentiated speech-assignment structure (3-min leadership / 2-min critics / 1-min backbench statements + amendments) — a clean template for the election sim's leader's debate and any future formal debate. The Four Corners sheet is also a fine Day-1-style warm-up as-is.

Guest-speaker precedent: the previous runs show a sitting MP will come twice for this format. Worth remembering as an option for the election sim's vote day or a post-election "what your MP does" session — the deck's guest role (Speaker) made the students' proceedings feel real.

5. Caveats if anything is reused

  • Strip slides 34–61 (template filler) and slide 46 (font/credits guide) before projecting anything.
  • The deck's Google Classroom code (slide 18) and the January session dates are from the previous run — historic, not reusable.
  • Riding name (Dartmouth—Cole Harbour) reflects the previous run; verify the current MP and riding before any guest outreach.
  • The Copy of docx is the better warm-up version (homelessness prompt beats the "globally minded" one); fix the typos if it's ever printed.
