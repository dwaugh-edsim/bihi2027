# Citizenship 9 — Unit 1 Policy Dossiers (Class 6)

**Purpose:** Three one-page teacher dossiers on the top three issues surfaced by the Current Issues Diagnostic (`Student_System/CIT9_Current_Issues_Diagnostic.html`): **Housing, Tariffs, School Cellphone Bans.** Each dossier shows students that every issue has a *level* of government — and an *instrument* (bylaw, directive, statute) — that acts on it.

**How to run (Class 6, 60 min):** Trios get one dossier each (10 min read + annotate) → trios rotate dossiers (or jigsaw) → each trio completes a **"Who Acts?" strip** for each issue: *which level acts, with what instrument, and who feels it?* → whole-class debrief building a master chart on the board.

**Teacher framing rule:** stay analytical (who CAN act, with what tool), never partisan (who SHOULD act). The "should" debate arrives in Class 7's caucus and the Term 2 debates.

---

## DOSSIER 1 — HOUSING: "Why can't I afford to move out?"

**The issue:** Rents and house prices in HRM rose faster than local incomes through the 2020s. Vacancy rates are among the lowest in Canada. Students in the diagnostic flagged it as the issue that will hit them first.

**Who acts, and with what instrument:**

| Level | What they actually do | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Municipal (HRM)** | Decides *what can be built where*: zoning rules that used to limit most residential land to single-unit homes; recent HRM changes opened more land to multi-unit buildings and speed up permits | **Bylaw** (zoning) + permits |
| **Provincial (NS)** | Housing programs and supports; landlord–tenant law; taxes aimed at speculation (e.g., the non-resident property tax); runs Affordable Housing programs | **Statutes, regulations, directives** |
| **Federal (Canada)** | Funds housing through national programs (e.g., the Housing Accelerator Fund, which pays municipalities to reform zoning and build faster); sets immigration targets that shape demand; mortgage rules via banks | **Spending programs, statutes** |

**The takeaway chart:** the *same* problem — a housing shortage — needs all three levels to move, and each level holds a different lever. A mayor cannot set immigration targets; a Prime Minister cannot rezone a Halifax street.

**Discussion prompts:**
1. If HRM rezones your neighbourhood for four-unit buildings tomorrow, who benefits? Who gets angry? *(Preview: Class 64 Power Audit.)*
2. Which lever acts fastest — bylaw, directive, or federal spending? Which acts largest?

## DOSSIER 2 — TARIFFS: "Why did my part-time job lose hours?"

**The issue:** In 2025 the United States imposed new tariffs (border taxes) on Canadian steel, aluminum, automobiles, and other goods; Canada answered with counter-tariffs on U.S. products. Global supply chains repriced overnight — and the effects reach NS kitchens, warehouses, and part-time schedules.

**Who acts, and with what instrument:**

| Level | What they actually do | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal (Canada)** | *Only* Canada can set tariffs — trade and customs are federal powers (Constitution s. 91). Ottawa imposed counter-tariffs and support packages for affected industries | **Statute + regulation (customs tariffs)** |
| **Provincial (NS)** | Cannot set tariffs — but feels them: NS exporters (lumber, tires, seafood) sell into U.S. markets; the province responds with adjustment programs and worker supports | **Spending programs** |
| **Municipal (HRM)** | Cannot touch tariffs — but the Port of Halifax's traffic, and local employers' hours, move with them | **None directly** — consequences land in the community |

**The takeaway chart:** tariffs show the cleanest case of **exclusive federal jurisdiction** — and of *spillover*: a decision made in Ottawa (or Washington) shows up as fewer shifts at a Dartmouth warehouse.

**Discussion prompts:**
1. A tariff is a tax paid at the border. Who ultimately pays it — the importer, the exporter, or the shopper? Defend with a chain of reasoning.
2. Why would a province bother offering "adjustment programs" for a tax it cannot control?

## DOSSIER 3 — CELLPHONES IN SCHOOL: "Who took my phone, legally?"

**The issue:** Since the 2024–25 school year, personal mobile devices must be **off and out of sight during instructional time** in every NS public school — a rule many students in the diagnostic had opinions about. It was announced by the Province in June 2024.

**Who acts, and with what instrument:**

| Level | What they actually do | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **Provincial (NS)** | The Minister of Education and Early Childhood Development issued the directive: devices off and away during class across all public schools, with exceptions (e.g., medical/accessibility needs, teacher-directed instructional use) | **Directive (ministerial)** |
| **Municipal / School (HRM + Bicentennial)** | No power to override it — implements it: school-level expectations for storage and consequences, classroom-by-classroom norms | **Implementation of the directive** |
| **Federal (Canada)** | Not involved — education is provincial jurisdiction (s. 92). (Federal online-harms debates are a separate file.) | **None** |

**The takeaway chart:** the cleanest case of **provincial jurisdiction over education** — and of how a *directive* (not a statute, not a bylaw) actually runs students' daily lives. It is also the rare policy in the dossier set the students live inside *right now*.

**Discussion prompts:**
1. Could a school vote to allow phones anyway? Why not? *(delegated power — Class 5)*
2. If the directive were a statute debated in the Legislature instead of a ministerial directive, what would be different about how it got made? Who gets to speak?

## Debrief master chart (build on the board)

| Issue | Federal acts by… | Provincial acts by… | Municipal acts by… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Housing | funding programs, mortgage rules | housing programs, landlord–tenant law | **zoning bylaws, permits** |
| Tariffs | **customs tariffs (exclusive)** | adjustment programs | — (receives consequences) |
| Cellphones | — (education is provincial) | **ministerial directive** | school-level implementation |

**Assessment tie-in:** the "Who Acts?" strips feed Class 7's caucus briefs and the **Class 8 formative check** (see `cit9-u1-quiz-bank.md`). The jurisdiction content is re-tested in the **Unit 2 Test (Class 24)** only indirectly — it is formally revisited in the **Unit 5 Test (Class 69)**, so this class is the front-loaded foundation, not a one-off.
