# Veggie-Bill-1 — Senate Committee Simulation (how to use it)

> **Source:** Library of Parliament "Parliamentary Simulations" series (learn.parl.ca, © 2026). A complete, print-and-run committee-stage simulation built around the **Vegetable Harmony Act** — a deliberately absurd fictitious bill (Bill 100) that would ban parents from serving spinach, Brussels sprouts, turnip, parsnip, and cauliflower to their children. Lives in `resources/Unit_05_Governance/Veggie-Bill-1/`.

## What's in the folder

| File | What it is |
|------|-----------|
| `Committee-Meeting_Teacher-Guide_EN-TAGGED.pdf` (14 pp) | Full 3-lesson plan: intro class → groupwork prep class → simulation class. Role assignment table, classroom setup diagram, 50-min timing sheet. |
| `Committee-Meeting_Student-Package_EN-TAGGED.pdf` (84 pp) | Character profiles for 30 students (chair, 19 senators in four affiliation groups A–D, Minister of Food, 9 witnesses from three interest groups), role overviews, rules & decorum, prep worksheets, and the **chair's script**. Print by role group, not whole. |
| `VegetableHarmonyAct-EN.pdf` (12 pp) | The mock bill itself — bilingual, formatted like a real Parliament of Canada bill (preamble, definitions, prohibition, penalties, regulations, coming-into-force) **plus 4 pre-written amendment slips** (restaurant exemption, mandatory dessert, adding asparagus/beets to the banned list, reduced fines). |
| `Committee-Meeting_Simulation-Steps_EN-TAGGED.pdf` (2 pp) | Student-facing procedure quick sheet: minister presentation → witnesses → clause-by-clause → amendments → vote to report. |
| `Committee-Meeting_Exit-Slip_EN-TAGGED.pdf` (1 p) | 3-2-1 reflection + "how is this different from a real committee?" |
| `Committee-Meeting_Slide-Deck_EN-TAGGED.pptx` | Intro-class deck (committees, legislative process, the bill). |

Everything is accessible-tagged, bilingual where it matters, and designed for Grades 9–12 with **three 50-minute periods** — no prep beyond printing and cutting character cards. The teacher plays the **committee clerk** (timekeeper, speaker's list, vote recorder, amendment paper handler), so classroom control stays with us while a student chairs.

## Why it earns a slot in Cit 9

- **It fills the exact gap after the Mock Election.** Classes 53–56 (Bicentennial Election) end at vote day — students elect a government and never see what that government does with power. This sim is "what happens *after* you win": the winner's minister must defend their bill clause-by-clause, and the losing parties get to amend and obstruct it. It's the natural Class 57 material.
- **It sets up Class 57–59's protest arc.** Idle No More was sparked by **Bill C-45** — an omnibus bill pushed through with limited debate. Students who have just sat through clause-by-clause examination will understand *viscerally* what "limited committee scrutiny" means as an outrage, instead of needing it explained.
- **It's the low-stakes role-play warm-up** before the Sentencing Circle sim (Class 60): same skills (staying in role, speaking through the chair, decorum rules) with a comedy bill nobody can get hurt by.
- **Curriculum fit:** hits "Investigate the structure, operation, and selection of government" and "Evaluate strategies to meaningfully engage as citizens" directly; witnesses-vs-senators structure is a clean illustration of "working within/outside government."
- **Zero build cost.** The course spine says Cit 9 sims are lighter than HL9's — this one is a genuine published simulation, not something to author. Bilingual, accessible, and free.

## Recommended placement — two options

**Option A (recommended): single "legislature in action" class, right after Vote Day (before current Class 57).**
Skip the 3-class arc; run a compressed 50-min version:
1. *(10 min)* Slide deck excerpt: what committees do + the bill's preamble and clauses 4–10, read aloud for the absurdity.
2. *(25 min)* Assign on the spot: 1 chair (script from student package), ~8 senators, 1 Minister of Food, 2–3 witnesses; everyone else is gallery + reporters who must file one question. Run minister presentation → one witness pair → clause votes on 4, 5, and 7 only.
3. *(10 min)* Force one amendment slip (the restaurant exemption is the cleanest), vote it, then vote to report the bill.
4. *(5 min)* Exit slip (3-2-1 version).
Then hand off to Idle No More the next class with the line: *"Now — what if a government didn't want its bill examined clause-by-clause?"*

**Option B: full 3-class run as published.** Costs two extra classes; would require compressing the protest arc (57–59) or dropping a buffer. Only worth it if the election sim lands well and the cohort is thriving on simulations — the full version has richer character profiles (19 distinct senators with province-based positions, four pre-seeded amendments from named senators).

## Practical notes

- **Class sizes fit.** 30 roles max; our sections run smaller — cut senator seats first, never witnesses (witnesses carry the perspective-taking).
- **Print strategy:** student package is 84 pp but divided by role group — print the character profiles (pp. 3–10, cut into cards), plus the 2-page role section per student. Don't print whole copies.
- **The amendment slips in the Act PDF are the best part** — pre-written, formatted like real committee-stage amendments, each planted with a specific senator character. For the full run, hand each amendment-carrying senator their slip with their profile.
- **Chair is the hardest role** — give it to a confident student and lean on the included script; we stay as clerk, which keeps order-of-play in teacher hands.
- **Reuse beyond Unit 5:** the Vegetable Harmony Act is also a ready-made artifact for Unit 1's jurisdiction review ("which level of government could even pass this?") or the Class 7 caucus norms — file it under `resources/Unit_05_Governance/` and pull it out wherever a "read a real-looking bill" moment is needed.
- **Optional video:** CPAC Senate committee clips (link in teacher guide, p. 7) — worth 5 minutes only if the cohort needs to see real decorum first.
- **Assessment:** keep it formative (exit slip → the 20% ongoing bucket). Do not add a graded item on top of the election sim's 6% + U5 test 4% — this is enrichment that deepens the election post-mortem, not a fifth U5 assessment.
