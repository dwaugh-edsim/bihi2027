THE THREE MAJOR MID-CENTURY GLOBAL CONFLICTS COMMON KNOWLEDGE BASE WITH THEATER MAPS
1. World War I 1914 to 1918
TOTAL: ~20M DEAD (~10M CIVILIANS) CANADA: ~620,000 SERVED • ~66,000 KILLED
What the war was actually about: Rival European empires spent decades building giant armies and secret alliances like a line of dominoes. When a Serbian nationalist assassinated the Austrian Archduke in Sarajevo in 1914, the dominoes fell and dragged dozens of nations into four years of industrial slaughter in the mud. Canada was a British colony with no separate foreign policy, so when Britain declared war, all Canadians were automatically at war too.
Casualties: Total: ~20 million deaths (~9.7M military, ~10M civilian) across all nations. Canada: ~620,000 served, ~66,000 killed, 172,000 wounded.
Where: Western Front trenches across Belgium and northern France (700-km continuous line).
Trench Warfare: Barbed wire, deep mud, poison gas (chlorine used first at Ypres in 1915), and artillery barrages.
Major Battles: Second Ypres (1915), The Somme (1916), Vimy Ridge (April 1917), Passchendaele (autumn 1917).
Enemies: German Empire and Austro-Hungarian Empire (Central Powers).
End: Allied breakthrough in 1918; Armistice took effect November 11 at 11:00 AM.
Local effects: Halifax was the primary troop departure port. On Dec 6, 1917, the munitions ship Mont-Blanc exploded, killing nearly 2,000 civilians.
WESTERN FRONT: FRANCE & BELGIUM
2. World War II 1939 to 1945
TOTAL: 75 TO 80M DEAD (50M+ CIVILIANS) CANADA: ~1.1M SERVED • ~45,000 KILLED
What the war was actually about (Two Global Theatres): WWII was a two-front struggle against the Axis Powers (Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy, and Imperial Japan). In Europe, Adolf Hitler exploited German bitterness over the 1919 Versailles Treaty to seize power, invading neighbours and murdering six million Jewish people in the Holocaust. In the Pacific, Imperial Japan sought an Asian empire for oil and rubber, invading China in 1937. When the Allies cut off trade to halt Japanese conquest, Japan launched surprise attacks across the Pacific on Dec 7, 1941 (Pearl Harbor, Hong Kong, Malaya). Canada's Parliament voted independently to declare war on Germany in 1939 and immediately on Japan on Dec 7, 1941.
Casualties: Total: 75 to 80 million deaths (over 50M civilians, including 6M Jews in the Holocaust and millions in Asia). Canada: ~1.1 million served, ~45,000 killed, 55,000 wounded.
Where: Atlantic Ocean, Western Europe (Italy, Normandy D-Day, Netherlands), Pacific (Hong Kong, Japan).
Battle of the Atlantic: Halifax was the Convoy Capital. Merchant ships assembled in Bedford Basin under constant German U-boat threat in the mid-Atlantic air gap.
Pacific & Hong Kong (1941): Nearly 2,000 Canadians defended Hong Kong. All were killed or captured (290 killed in action); over 1,680 survived into brutal Japanese POW slave labour camps, where 267 died from starvation, disease, and abuse.
Home Front Internment: In 1942, racial panic led Canada to use the War Measures Act to forcibly intern over 22,000 Japanese Canadians (over 75% Canadian citizens) in BC interior camps, confiscating their homes, farms, and businesses.
Atomic Defeat & The United Nations: Germany surrendered May 1945. Japan surrendered in August 1945 after the US dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki (developed with Canadian uranium from Great Bear Lake). The UN was founded in October 1945 to prevent future global wars and avert nuclear extinction.
Local effects: Halifax convoys fed Britain; German U-boats sank ships in the St. Lawrence and Cabot Strait; thousands of Maritimers served in both theatres.
NORTH ATLANTIC CONVOY & EUROPE
3. The Korean War 1950 to 1953
TOTAL: ~3M DEAD (1.5M+ CIVILIANS) CANADA: ~26,000 SERVED • 516 KILLED
What the war was actually about: During the Cold War, the world was split between the Communist bloc (Soviet Union and China) and Western democracies (US, Canada, and allies). Korea was divided along the 38th parallel into a Communist North and a pro-Western South. In June 1950, the North launched a surprise invasion that almost pushed the South into the sea. The United Nations sent troops to restore peace in the first collective military action in UN history. Canadian soldiers fought in brutal sub-zero mountain trenches.
Casualties: Total: ~3 million deaths (over half were Korean civilians: ~1.5M civilian deaths). Canada: ~26,000 served, 516 killed, 1,200 wounded.
Where & Conditions: Korean Peninsula, rugged mountain hills along the 38th Parallel. Sub-zero Siberian winters (-30°C) with frozen rifle bolts.
Battle of Kapyong (Hill 677, April 1951): 2PPCLI surrounded by 10,000 Chinese soldiers; called down friendly artillery onto own lines to hold ridge (US Presidential Unit Citation).
Enemies: North Korean communist forces and Chinese army divisions.
End & The DMZ: Armistice signed July 27, 1953; DMZ still divides North and South today. Known as "The Forgotten War".
KOREAN PENINSULA: 38TH PARALLEL
WHO SERVED IN THE SHADOWS? CITIZENSHIP AND RECOGNITION

No. 2 Construction Battalion (Nova Scotia's Black Battalion)

In 1914, Black volunteers were turned away at recruiting offices by white officers. Black leaders fought back, demanding the right to serve. In 1916, Canada created the No. 2 Construction Battalion, based in Pictou and Halifax. Over 600 men served in France, cutting timber and building railways under artillery fire. At home, Black Nova Scotians faced segregated schools, theatres, and restaurants. In 2022, the Prime Minister apologized in Truro for this anti-Black racism.

Indigenous Veterans: Service Without Equal Rights

Over 7,000 First Nations, Inuit, and Métis soldiers volunteered in WWI and WWII, serving as decorated snipers and scouts (like Francis Pegahmagabow with 378 kills and Tommy Prince with 11 medals). Yet when they returned home, they were still denied the federal vote until 1960, could not enter Legion halls, and were denied equal veterans' benefits and land grants under the federal Indian Act.

THE WAR IN AFGHANISTAN (2001 to 2014) NATO ARTICLE 5 COLLECTIVE DEFENSE & MODERN COMBAT
4. The War in Afghanistan 2001 to 2014
TOTAL: ~176,000 DEAD (70,000+ CIVILIANS) CANADA: ~40,000 SERVED • 158 SOLDIERS KILLED
What the war was actually about: On September 11, 2001, Al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked four airliners and attacked New York and Washington, killing nearly 3,000 civilians, including 24 Canadians. Al-Qaeda was based in Afghanistan under the protection of the Taliban government. For the first time in history, NATO invoked Article 5 (an attack on one is an attack on all). Canada joined international allies to remove the Taliban from power and prevent future terrorist attacks. Canadian troops spent over a decade fighting in the dangerous dust and heat of Kandahar Province.
Casualties: Total: ~176,000 direct deaths (over 70,000 Afghan civilians, ~70,000 Afghan security forces). Canada: ~40,000 served, 158 soldiers and 7 civilians killed, 2,000+ wounded physically, thousands with PTSD.
Where & Environment: Kandahar Province, baking deserts (50°C heat), grape drying walls, and rugged mountain valleys.
Enemies & Tactics: Taliban insurgent fighters and concealed roadside bomb (IED) networks.
End: Combat mission in Kandahar ended 2011; training mission finished 2014.
Local effects: Pte. Nathan Smith of Tatamagouche was killed at Tarnak Farm in April 2002: one of Canada's first combat deaths since Korea.

CANADA AS A PEACEKEEPER FOUR ESSENTIAL MISSIONS WITH LOCATION MAPS

1. Suez Crisis (1956): Birth of Peacekeeping

Casualties: ~3,000 dead (mostly Egyptian civilians/military). Canada: ~1,000 deployed, 0 combat deaths.

The Crisis: Britain, France, and Israel invaded Egypt to seize the canal. Soviets threatened nuclear war.

The Move: Diplomat Lester B. Pearson proposed neutral Blue Helmets to separate armies so invaders could withdraw. Pearson won the 1957 Nobel Peace Prize.

SUEZ CANAL & SINAI

2. Cyprus (1964 to Present): The Green Line

Casualties: ~4,000 to 5,000 dead, 200,000 displaced. Canada: 28 peacekeepers died holding the line.

The Crisis: Fighting exploded between Greek and Turkish Cypriots on the Mediterranean island of Cyprus.

The Reality: Canadians patrolled the buffer zone. When Turkey invaded in 1974, Canadians defended Nicosia Airport under fire.

CYPRUS: THE GREEN LINE

3. The Balkans (1992 to 1995): Medak Pocket

Casualties: ~100,000 dead (over 40,000 civilians; Srebrenica). Canada: 23 peacekeepers died in service.

The Crisis: Yugoslavia collapsed into ethnic civil war. Canadian peacekeepers protected civilians from snipers and shells.

Medak Pocket (Croatia, 1993): 2nd Battalion PPCLI fought a 15-hour battle against Croatian forces to stop ethnic cleansing: Canada's biggest battle since Korea.

CROATIA & MEDAK POCKET

4. Rwanda (1994): The Limit of Peacekeeping

Casualties: ~800,000 civilians murdered in 100 days of genocide. 2 million refugees displaced.

The Crisis: Extremist militias launched an extermination campaign against the Tutsi minority.

The Moral Stand: Gen. Roméo Dallaire begged for troops to stop it. The UN refused and ordered him to leave. Dallaire stayed with a small force and saved roughly 32,000 people.

HOME FRONT NOVA SCOTIA WHEN THE WAR REACHED OUR DOORSTEP
HALIFAX HARBOUR & BASIN
Halifax Explosion (Dec 6, 1917) Munitions ship Mont-Blanc (2,900 tonnes explosives) collided with Imo in the Narrows. Wiped out the North End, killed 2,000, injured 9,000. Vince Coleman's telegraph sacrifice saved ~300 train passengers. Boston sent emergency blizzard relief trains.
Battle of the Atlantic (1939 to 1945) Halifax was the lifeline convoy capital feeding Britain. Merchant ships assembled in Bedford Basin under constant U-boat threat. German U-boats sank passenger ferry SS Caribou in Cabot Strait (137 killed). Mandatory city blackouts.
The VE Day Riots (May 7 to 8, 1945) Overcrowded city, rationing, and closed liquor stores caused 10,000 sailors and citizens to riot upon victory in Europe. Downtown storefronts smashed and looted along Barrington and Gottingen streets.
Bedford Magazine Explosion (July 1945) Just 10 weeks after VE Day, a fire at Magazine Hill jetty detonated depth charges and torpedoes for 24 hours. City evacuated; heroic fire crews saved the main bunker from leveling the harbour.
