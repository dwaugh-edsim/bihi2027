Citizenship 9 · Remembrance Day · Personal Narratives

The Soldier Report: one real person, told properly.

You pick one real person whose life was shaped by war. You research them, you ground their story in the history from your Canada at War Field Guide, and you write 300 words in three paragraphs. The strongest stories, letters, and photos go into Room 8's school-wide Remembrance broadcast.

01 · WHO
Pick a real person
A veteran, a nurse, a home-front factory worker, a refugee. Two ways to pick: next slide.
02 · GROUND IT
Place them in the history
Which war, which years, which map theater, and why Canada went. Your field guide has all of it.
03 · WRITE IT
300 words, 3 paragraphs
Who they were. Where they served and why Canada was there. What it cost them and why their story matters.
Delivered in Google Classroom as a template: the paragraphs are chunked into three 100-word boxes with guiding questions, so nobody stares at a blank page. An adapted version with sentence starters is assigned separately, quietly. It counts as a marked report: it is your Remembrance-grade writing.
The Pick · Two Ways In

Part 1 or Part 2. Both are real people.

Part 1 · Option A
Pick someone from your own family
  • Ask at home: a veteran, a home-front factory worker, a ration volunteer, or a relative displaced by war.
  • Nothing military in the family? That counts too: the home front built the war.
  • Gather: full name, hometown, their wartime job, a photo or letter if one exists, and what the war did to their life after.
START THIS TONIGHT: one conversation at dinner is your whole research step.
