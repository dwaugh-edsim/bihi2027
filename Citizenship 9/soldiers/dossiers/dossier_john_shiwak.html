John Shiwak (Sikoak)

Lance Corporal · 1st Newfoundland Regiment

First World War (1915-1917) Newfoundland Rigolet (Cul-de-Sac), Labrador

An Inuk hunter and trapper from Rigolet, Labrador, who persuaded other men from his region to enlist and became one of the best snipers in the British army. His comrade remembered a shy, lonely man who said sniping was like swatching seals. He was killed in the Battle of Cambrai in November 1917.

Where This Came From: This dossier is built from the Dictionary of Canadian Biography entry by John C. Kennedy (which quotes the unpublished memoir of his comrade Howard Morry), The Canadian Encyclopedia, and Veterans Affairs Canada. It is not a canadianletters.ca collection.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born in February or March 1889 in Rigolet, Labrador, the eldest son of John Shiwak and Sarah Susanna. His family name was changed from Sikoak, which means 'relating to thin ice'.
  • Pre-War Life: A hunter, fisherman and trapper who also worked summers for a doctor (most likely Harry Paddon of the Grenfell Mission). A novelist who met him in 1911 described him as a natural artist and writer.
  • Enlistment: He enlisted in St. John's on July 24, 1915, 5 feet 5 inches tall and 132 pounds, and persuaded two other Inuit and a white man to join him.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War. Newfoundland was a separate dominion and sent its own regiment, which was devastated at Beaumont-Hamel on July 1, 1916.
  • Arrival: Shiwak reached the front in France on July 24, 1916, three weeks after Beaumont-Hamel.
  • Sniper: Over about 15 months of trench warfare he was seen as an exceptional scout and marksman. One officer called him the best sniper in the British army. He was promoted to lance corporal on April 16, 1917.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: In his letters he said he could not understand the steady killing. Comrades remembered him as shy and lonely, and his last months were marked by despondency after a close friend died.
  • Death: He and six others were killed by an exploding shell during an attack on Masnieres in the Battle of Cambrai on November 20 or 21, 1917. He was buried in the village.
  • Legacy: A commemorative plaque was unveiled at Masnieres on June 30, 2023. More than 60 Labrador men served in the war and five from the Lake Melville area were killed.

1916-1917 Comrade's Memoir Excerpt

From: Howard L. Morry (about John Shiwak)
Howard L. Morry, a Newfoundlander who served with Shiwak in France, remembered him in an unpublished memoir written in 1957: "a sniper and a good one. He was shy and lonely but I got to be quite friendly with him by talking of seal hunting, etc. We'd talk for hours and often he'd say will it ever be over. He sure was a great shot and had a lot of notches on his rifle. He said sniping was like swatching seals." Swatching means shooting seals in open water as they briefly lift their heads to breathe, which the Dictionary of Canadian Biography calls unparalleled training for a future sharpshooter.
Source Verification: View at Dictionary of Canadian Biography (John C. Kennedy)

1915-1917 Service Summary

Archival Transcription
Slight of build, Shiwak enlisted on 24 July 1915 in St. John's in the Newfoundland Regiment. After training in Scotland he reached the front in France on 24 July 1916, three weeks after the regiment's devastating losses at Beaumont-Hamel. During the next 15 months of hellish trench warfare he impressed all as an exceptional scout and marksman. One officer would call him the best sniper in the British army, and his actions earned him promotion to lance corporal on 16 April 1917. In his letters he confessed that he could not understand the steady killing. At least one card was sent from France to "My Dear Louisa", of whom he thought "every Day even when I am in the line".
Source Verification: View at Dictionary of Canadian Biography (John C. Kennedy)

November 20th 1917 Death at Masnieres

Archival Transcription
On 20 November 1917 (one witness later recorded the 21st) Shiwak and six others were killed by an exploding shell during an attack on the village of Masnieres, in the battle of Cambrai. He was buried in the village and received the British War and Victory medals. His final months had been shrouded in despondency and loneliness, which grew after the death of a close friend and former trapping companion. Shiwak was one of five men from the Lake Melville area killed in the war. A commemorative plaque was unveiled in Masnieres, France on June 30, 2023.
