Michael Francis Murphy

Private · Royal Newfoundland Regiment, 'C' Company (Regimental #754)

First World War (1914-1918) Newfoundland St. John's, Dominion of Newfoundland

Born in St. John's, Newfoundland, in February 1894, Michael Murphy enlisted on December 15, 1914 (Regimental #754) in 'C' Company of the Newfoundland Regiment. Fighting when Newfoundland was still a separate Dominion, he endured the trenches of Gallipoli, service in Egypt, and combat in France before returning home. His dossier holds wartime hospital and uniform photos, postcards, personal items, and printed records.

Where This Came From: All primary-source transcriptions, scans, and archival artifacts in this dossier come from the Michael Francis Murphy Collection at The Canadian Letters & Images Project (canadianletters.ca), Vancouver Island University.
View Source Collection on CanadianLetters.ca
Archival Collection Note (Canadian Letters & Images Project)

Michael Francis Murphy was born February 27, 1894, in St. John's, Newfoundland. Murphy enlisted on December 15, 1914, with the Royal Newfoundland Regiment (sevice #754), part of the original "C" coy. Murphy served in Gallipoli, Egypt, and France, and returned to Newfoundland at the end of the war. The collection currently consists of two photographs of Murphy and one letter to his daughter. Murphy's service file is available online through the Provincial Archives of Newfoundland and Labrador (The Rooms).

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born February 27, 1894, in St. John's in the then-Dominion of Newfoundland.
  • Service Role: Early December 1914 volunteer (Regimental #754) in 'C' Company of the Royal Newfoundland Regiment.
  • Distinct Identity: During WWI Newfoundland was a self-governing Dominion separate from Canada, raising its own national regiment.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War (1914-1918). Attached to Britain's famous 29th Division, the Newfoundland Regiment earned the rare title 'Royal' in 1917 for battlefield valour.
  • Theater: Suvla Bay at Gallipoli (1915), Suez/Egypt (early 1916), and the Western Front in France and Belgium (1916-1918).
  • Conditions: Endured dysentery, heat, and frostbite at Gallipoli before transferring to the mud and artillery of France; photographed convalescing in hospital in 1918.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Very few December 1914 Newfoundland volunteers survived both Gallipoli and the Western Front unscathed.
  • Outcome: Survived hospitalization in 1918, returned to Newfoundland, and later wrote to his daughter reflecting on his service.
  • Remembrance: Murphy's photographs and artifacts honour Newfoundland's distinct path into Atlantic Canadian remembrance.

April 16th 1918 Letter

Archival Transcription
47 General Hospital B.E.F. 16.4.18 Dear Mrs Murphy Your husband has been sent down to us having been wounded in his thigh. & is considered to be seriously ill. I am sorry to say. He is having every possible care & attention & you must hope for the best. He is doing as well as can be expected. I will write again if any change occurs. News will be so stale when it reaches you. but I hope he will able to write you himself again soon- Yours truly E.H. Lang Matron

June 18th 1918 Letter

Archival Transcription
47 General Hospital B.E.F. France 18.6.18. Dear Mrs Murphy Your letter received this morning. I am glad to be able to tell you that your husband has made very good progress & his leg is almost well. He will probably soon be transferred to England. no doubt he has written to himself, given you all the news about himself. Miss Lang, who wrote to you before, has gone to another hospital. December 2nd 1918 Letter

From: E. Johnson · To: Mrs Murphy
47. Fenton Lee New Herrington Fence Houses Durham 2.12.18 Dear Mrs Murphy You will no doubt be suprised to hear from me a complete stranger to you but we have just had your husband staying along with my son with us for a week & he begged of me to write to you. My son & he are stationed in the same hospital & seem to be very dear pals. The hospital where they are seem to be a good one & I am sure you need'nt worry of your husband as he is coming on fine. His wound seems to be healing alright & I should think you will be having [?]ome with you again when the year turns. We were so glad to have him with us because when the hes been in the army as long & wounded a change is always welcome to break the monotony. The weather has been great while they have been here & they have been able to get out all the week. It is a very quiet place we live in. It is a colliery village but there is a town just five miles away & they were able to take the car there whenever they wished. We are 130 miles from the hospital but it only takes about 8 hrs to go. They started away this morning at ten & would arrive in London about 6pm this evening. I thought it was an awful long time only seeing my son once a year but it must have been a lifetime to you but still it is over at last & the boys will all get settled at home again Mike often talked of you & your darling little girl & I should long for a photo of you both I think your husband can hardly [?] your little girl to be anything but a baby still when they are away from home they cannot picture their kiddies growing up. My baby is 20 years of age & is fighting with the forces in France. He has been lucky & not had any accident since he went out & I hope it wont be long before we have him home with us. The factories & works are all applying to get their men & boys back again to work. We are just waiting now of a wire to say that Stan & Mike have arrived at Hospital. We are looking forward to them coming again at Xmas we will welcome your husband to our home any time now we have enjoyed his company so much. He asked me to tell you he had a glorious time with us & it is the first taste of home life he has had since he left your home. I am enclosing my son photograph that is with your husband also one of my husband & myself now dear friend I must close sending my best wishes to you & baby & hope you will soon have your dear one with you I remain yours sincerely E. Johnson

July 5th 1919 Letter

Archival Transcription
July 5, 1919. M. Murphy, 754, 14 Barter's Hill, CITY. Dear Sir, Your application was considered at a Meeting of the Civil Re-Establishment Committee held on Friday evening last and I have been asked to inform you that after the Committee had given careful and sympathetic attention to the study of your case it concluded that your disabilities were not of such character and extent as to render It impossible for you to resume your former class of work. If there is any other way, however, in which this Committee can be of assistance to you, I hope you will not fail to let us know. Yours faithfully, WW.[?] Vocational Officer.

September 1st 1924 Letter

Archival Transcription
September 1st, 1924. Mrs. Michael Murphy, 17 Patrick Street, C I T Y. DEAR MRS. MURPHY,- We have not received the decision from the Govern-ment regarding discretionary powers to the Board of Pension Commissioners under which authority your claim for considera-tion on your pension would be given.
