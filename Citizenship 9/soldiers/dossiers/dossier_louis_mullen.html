Louis Wilfred Mullen

Gunner / Driver · No. 11 Overseas Field Artillery Ammunition Column & 43rd Battery, CFA

First World War (1914-1918) Prince Edward Island Covehead, Queens County, Prince Edward Island

Born in Covehead on Prince Edward Island's North Shore in April 1896, Louis Mullen travelled to Halifax in February 1916 to enlist in the Canadian Field Artillery, serving at the front with the 43rd Battery until the Armistice. He returned home with a remarkable wartime album of over one hundred photographs, personal items, embroidered postcards, and letters.

Where This Came From: All primary-source transcriptions, scans, and archival artifacts in this dossier come from the Louis Wilfred Mullen Collection at The Canadian Letters & Images Project (canadianletters.ca), Vancouver Island University.
View Source Collection on CanadianLetters.ca
Archival Collection Note (Canadian Letters & Images Project)

Louis Wilfred Mullen was born in Cove Head, Prince Edward Island, in April 1896. Mullen previously was a member of the militia before he enlisted in February 1916 in Halifax, Nova Scotia , with the No. 11 Overseas Field Artillery Brigade Ammunition Column. He served overseas with the 43rd Battery until his return to Canada at the end of the war. The collection currently consists of his photograph album containing over one hundred photographs, a letter, postcards, and other miscellaneous personal items.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born April 1896 in the farming and fishing community of Covehead, Prince Edward Island.
  • Service Role: Served in the pre-war militia before enlisting in Halifax in February 1916 with the Canadian Field Artillery (43rd Battery).
  • Visual Record: Compiled an extraordinary personal wartime album of photographs, postcards, printed passes, and keepsakes.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War (1914-1918). Field artillery batteries fired creeping barrages that protected Canadian infantry assaults from Vimy to Mons.
  • Theater: Western Front artillery positions in France and Belgium from 1916 to 1918.
  • Conditions: Artillery ammunition columns hauled high-explosive shells up shelled muddy tracks at night using horse teams and lorries.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Survived years of counter-battery shelling, gas attacks, and bitter winters in the field.
  • Outcome: Returned safely to Canada at demobilization with his wartime camera prints and personal artifacts intact.
  • Remembrance: Mullen's artifacts show what Maritime soldiers chose to carry in their pockets and preserve when peace finally arrived.

Primary Source Transcriptions (1 Curated Item)

Verbatim Archival Text

1916 Letter

Archival Transcription
Gr. LW Mullen #314273 11th Field Howitzer Brigade Ammunition Column Army Postoffice London - Eng Dear Mother :- I got here feeling fine and thats the address. I spent $1.00 for Mothersills sea sick remedy and I wish I had it back as I wasn't the least bit sick. We had a great trip over. Went aboard the Olympia on saturday afternoon and left Halifax Wednesday morning 9.30 met two days fog off Newfoundland and landed at Liverpool Thursday morning and left there at 5.30 p.m. for our present headquarters Bramshot Camp and got here 3 am & walked 3 miles to camp. (we are going to be shifted to Witley Camp tomorrow). At present we are at Bramshot about 50 miles from London. Witley is 8 miles from here. But the address on the front gets me anywhere. The mail leaves here for Canada just once a week and I didn't know until an hour ago. We are attached to the 11 Brigade of 4 batteries - 29th, 35th, 36th, 43rd I know a lot of fellows in 36th from Sydney. We expect a furlough in London within the next two week of 6 days. That sure is some place. I'm feeling fine. We'll have to march to our new camp tomorrow. thats 8 miles away. Its an artillery training camp. Believe me this is some neat country. Every inch of the country is cultivated and as clean as a whistle and no wooden houses at all. All lights out at night, London is right dark at night and if you use a flash light in streets you go to jail. Machine guns on every high building. I'll send a detailed letter next week. Well Mother I'll close for now hoping you and Papa Annie and Viola are as well as I am. Love from Lou Better forward this to girls as I wont have time to write them until next week. We are in Hants county about 37 miles from London and believe me we have lots of company in the soldiers line there's quite a few of them here,
