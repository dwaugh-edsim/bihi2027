Reginald John Paul

Private · 1st Battalion, Newfoundland Regiment

First World War (1914-1918) Newfoundland Burin, Burin Peninsula, Dominion of Newfoundland

Born on December 14, 1895, in the fishing outport of Burin, Newfoundland, Reginald Paul enlisted just after his nineteenth birthday in December 1914. Having survived the Gallipoli campaign, he went over the top at Beaumont-Hamel on the morning of July 1, 1916, where over 80 percent of the Newfoundland Regiment fell in half an hour. Two letters from the regimental chaplain break the news to his family.

Where This Came From: All primary-source transcriptions, scans, and archival artifacts in this dossier come from the Reginald John Paul Collection at The Canadian Letters & Images Project (canadianletters.ca), Vancouver Island University.
View Source Collection on CanadianLetters.ca
Archival Collection Note (Canadian Letters & Images Project)

Reginald John Paul was born on December 14, 1895, in Burin, Newfoundland. He enlisted on December 21, 1914, and served with the Royal Newfoundland Regiment. Paul served both at Gallipoli and on the Somme and was killed on the first day of the Somme, July 1, 1916 . The collection consists of two letters from the chaplain to his family, and one photograph. Paul's service file is available online through the Provincial Archives of Newfoundland and Labrador (The Rooms).

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born December 14, 1895, in the coastal outport of Burin on Newfoundland's Burin Peninsula.
  • Service Role: Private in the 1st Battalion, Newfoundland Regiment, enlisting December 21, 1914, at age 19.
  • Outport Volunteer: Left a tight-knit south-coast maritime community in the first winter of the war.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War (1914-1918). On July 1, 1916, British and Newfoundland troops launched the opening offensive of the Battle of the Somme in northern France.
  • Theater: Gallipoli (1915) followed by Beaumont-Hamel on the Somme, July 1, 1916.
  • Conditions: Ordered to advance down an exposed slope through uncut barbed wire into interlocking German machine-gun fire; of ~800 Newfoundlanders who attacked, only 68 answered roll call the next morning.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Killed in action on July 1, 1916, at age 20; the collection preserves his portrait and two poignant letters sent by the chaplain to his parents in Burin.
  • Outcome: One of hundreds of young Newfoundlanders lost in thirty minutes on a single morning.
  • Remembrance: Explains why July 1 is still observed in Newfoundland and Labrador as Memorial Day alongside November 11.

Verbatim Archival Text

July 12th 1917 Letter

From: Chaplain · To: Father
44 Casualty Clearing Station B.E.F. France July 12, 1917 Dear Sir, I have recently recovered the remains of Pte Reginald Paul, which have been lying out since he was killed about a year ago. I have buried the body in one of our military cemeteries quite near to where he fell. The War Office will be writing to give you full particulars when they have received our list. I have found on the body the letters I now enclose. These are awfully sad days and I am very sorry you have been called upon to give up the one who has died the Hero's death for King and Country & in support of Right and Honour. Believe me, Yours in truest sympathy, Leonard T. Pearson Chaplain to the Forces

November 16th 1917 Letter

From: Chaplain · To: Father
44 Casualty Clearing Station B.E.F. France 16.11.17 Dear Mr. Paul, Your letter has reached me today & many thanks for it. Your Son's poor Body was one of over 6,000 that I helped to recover as soon as the Enemy retreated & the ground safe to work over. I have looked up my Records & find I buried his Remains in Hawthorne Ridge, No 2, B Row, Grave 8. It is quite close to Beaumont Hamel, between the wires in the original "No Man's Land" & within a few yards of where he fell. The Record book has to be carefully gone through by the War Office as 95% of the bodies I recovered are cases of "Missing". It is only within the last few weeks the official news has been sent to some relatives and probably you will be hearing quite soon again. These are awfully sad days and we must put our trust in the Lord Jesus and in His Words in John 11:25:6. Please accept my heartfelt sympathy & also express it for me to your son's wife. Believe me, Yours sincerely Leonard T. Pearson
