Edward James ('Joe Dowey') Power

Private · Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI), Service #SF14075

Korean War (1950-1953) Nova Scotia North Sydney, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia

Born in North Sydney, Cape Breton, on July 10, 1931, Edward James Power - known to friends and family as 'Joe Dowey' - enlisted in Halifax on January 4, 1951, at age 19 to serve with the United Nations forces in Korea. Deployed with the PPCLI on the fortified ridge known as 'The Hook' along the 38th Parallel, he was killed by shrapnel on Christmas Eve, December 24, 1952, and laid to rest at the UN Cemetery in Busan.

Archival Collection Note (Canadian Letters & Images Project)

Private Edward James Power, also known as “Joe Dowey,” was born in North Sydney, Nova Scotia, on July 10, 1931, to parents Edward and Jean (née MacDonald) Power.

Enlisting in Halifax, N.S., on January 4, 1951, he served with the Princess Patricia’s Canadian Light Infantry, Royal Canadian Infantry Corps, in Korea. Power was killed when hit by shrapnel while deployed at the “Hook” (located near the Samichon River, along the border region of North and South Korea), on December 24, 1952, and was buried in the United Nations Cemetery, Busan, South Korea.

Content notes: Photos only.

External links: Pte. Edward James Power’s service record (Serv/Reg# SF14075) is not open to public access at this time. A memorial page honouring Power can be visited online at the Canadian Virtual War Memorial.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born July 10, 1931, in North Sydney, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia, to Edward and Jean (MacDonald) Power; nicknamed 'Joe Dowey'.
  • Service Role: Private (#SF14075) in Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI), enlisting in Halifax on January 4, 1951.
  • Post-WWII Generation: Too young for WWII, he answered Canada's 1951 call for a Special Force to defend South Korea.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: Korean War (1950-1953). Unlike WWI or WWII, Canada fought under a collective-security mandate of the United Nations Security Council after North Korea invaded South Korea.
  • Theater: Static mountain trench line at 'The Hook' near the Samichon River along the 38th Parallel, winter 1952.
  • Conditions: Sub-zero Korean mountain winters, sandbagged bunkers, night patrols, and heavy artillery and mortar duels across no-man's-land.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Killed by enemy shrapnel on Christmas Eve, December 24, 1952, at age 21 - one of 516 Canadians who died in the Korean War.
  • Outcome: Buried thousands of kilometres from Cape Breton in the United Nations Memorial Cemetery in Busan, South Korea; his collection preserves rare wartime photographs from Korea.
  • Remembrance: Ensures Cape Breton and Maritime sacrifices in the 'Forgotten War' of Korea stand beside WWI and WWII.

Korean War Photographic & Memorial Record Archival Note

Service Number SF14075 · Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry

Because Korean War military personnel files remain governed by federal post-1945 privacy schedules, Private Power's archival collection at The Canadian Letters & Images Project preserves his three wartime photographs from Korea alongside his official biographical and burial record at the United Nations Memorial Cemetery in Busan, South Korea.

When writing your 3-paragraph report on Private Power, cite visual details directly from his Korean War photographs above (such as his winter field kit in Korea with fellow PPCLI soldiers and his dress uniform portrait) together with the strategic context of the December 1952 fighting at the “Hook” near the Samichon River.
