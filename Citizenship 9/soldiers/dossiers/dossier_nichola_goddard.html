Nichola Goddard

Captain · 1st Regiment, Royal Canadian Horse Artillery (Forward Observation Officer attached to 1 PPCLI)

Afghanistan War (2006) Nova Scotia Antigonish, Nova Scotia (high school years); born in Papua New Guinea

Born in Papua New Guinea, Nichola Goddard went to high school in Antigonish, Nova Scotia, and chose the army in her final year. As a forward observation officer in Afghanistan she directed artillery fire from a light armoured vehicle. She was killed on May 17, 2006, the first Canadian woman to die in a combat role.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born May 2, 1980 in Madang, Papua New Guinea, the eldest of three children of teachers Tim and Sally Goddard. By age 18 she had lived in seven communities across Canada, including Black Lake, Saskatchewan.
  • Nova Scotia Years: She attended high school in Antigonish, Nova Scotia. A strong cross-country skier, she earned the nickname 'Care Bear' from her ski team.
  • Choosing the Army: She chose the military in her senior year in 1997, then trained at the Royal Military College in Kingston, Ontario.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: Canada's mission in Afghanistan (2001-2014). In January 2006 Goddard deployed to Kandahar province.
  • Her Job: As a forward observation officer attached to the Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry she rode in a Light Armoured Vehicle and called in artillery fire and airstrikes.
  • The Battle: On May 17, 2006, more than 200 Canadian and Afghan soldiers swept an area south of Kandahar. Her vehicle was ambushed as it moved toward a mosque.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Standing in the turret with her head and shoulders exposed, she was hit by shrapnel after rocket-propelled grenades struck the vehicle. She died instantly at age 26.
  • Firsts: She was the first Canadian woman killed in a combat role, the highest-ranking soldier killed in Afghanistan up to that time, and the 16th Canadian to die there.
  • Legacy: Awarded the Meritorious Service Medal in 2006. A school in Calgary, a Canadian Coast Guard ship, a lake in Saskatchewan and a fund supporting servicewomen carry her name.

1980-1997 Biographical Source Note

Archival Transcription
Nichola Kathleen Sarah Goddard was the first of three children of British and Canadian schoolteachers Tim and Sally Goddard. She spent the first three and a half years of her life in remote villages of Papua New Guinea, where she ate local food and learned the local language. By 1984 the family was in Black Lake, Saskatchewan, a small fly-in Indigenous community, where she learned Dene, the language of her friends on the playground. By age 18 she had lived in seven communities across Canada and had attended high school in Antigonish, Nova Scotia. She chose the military in her senior year of high school in 1997. Her mother Sally recalls that "free education and a job for five years after graduation" factored heavily in the decision.
Source Verification: View at The Canadian Encyclopedia (Robert Foreman, 2020)

May 17th 2006 Battle Account

Archival Transcription
On 17 May 2006, Nichola Goddard and over 200 Canadian and Afghan soldiers were tasked to sweep an area south of Kandahar in the Panjwayi district to root out Taliban fighters gathering there. Goddard and her team were ambushed by militants as they moved toward a mosque. As the commanding officer, Goddard was standing in the turret of the light armoured vehicle with her head and shoulders exposed. Numerous rocket-propelled grenades hit the vehicle. Shrapnel struck Goddard in the back of the head, killing her instantly. She was the first female Canadian soldier to die in a combat role and the highest-ranking soldier to die since Canada arrived in Afghanistan. She was the 16th Canadian to die in that conflict.
Source Verification: View at The Canadian Encyclopedia (Robert Foreman, 2020)

2006-2020 Legacy and Remembrance

Archival Transcription
Goddard's family arranged a public funeral at St. Barnabas Anglican Church in Calgary on 26 May 2006, and she is buried in the National Military Cemetery at Beechwood in Ottawa. She was posthumously awarded the Meritorious Service Medal on 27 October 2006. Her death shocked the country. Many Canadians at the time believed combat was a job for men, and her life and death received more media attention than any of the other soldiers killed in Afghanistan. According to her family and friends she despised gender stereotypes and viewed herself as an equal among her male counterparts. Goddard Lake in Saskatchewan was named for her in 2014, as were the Canadian Coast Guard ship Captain Goddard M.S.M. and Captain Nichola Goddard School in Calgary.
