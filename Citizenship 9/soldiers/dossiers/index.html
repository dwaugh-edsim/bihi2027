WWI Private

Archie Keat

Tatamagouche, Colchester County, Nova Scotia

Enlisted from rural Tatamagouche, Nova Scotia. Served through frontline trench warfare with the 25th Battalion. On November 11, 1918, he marched into liberated Belgian territory as the guns fell silent at 11:00 AM, penning a historic letter home to his mother describing the euphoric relief, cheering crowds, and civilian gratitude.

Branch: Canadian Army (Infantry)
Unit: 25th Battalion (Nova Scotia Rifles), CEF

WWI Lieutenant / Officer (Given name 'Major')

Major Hudgins

Aylesford, Kings County, Nova Scotia (Annapolis Valley)

Born in the Annapolis Valley (his parents gave him the first name 'Major'). Trained in Nova Scotia, commanded harbour defense patrols around Halifax, and deployed overseas as a company officer in the renowned 85th Nova Scotia Highlanders. Wrote extensive letters home to his family before being killed in action on the Western Front.

Branch: Canadian Army (Highland Infantry)
Unit: 85th Battalion (Nova Scotia Highlanders), CEF

WWI Sergeant

Daniel Spencer Reid

Middle Musquodoboit, Halifax County, Nova Scotia

A rural Halifax County farm boy who enlisted at age 19 in October 1915 with the 85th Battalion (Nova Scotia Highlanders). Rising to the rank of Sergeant, his 43 preserved letters home describe the realities of camp life, overseas transit, and front-line artillery bombardments before he was killed in the mud of Passchendaele on October 30, 1917.

Branch: Canadian Army (Highland Infantry)
Unit: 85th Battalion (Nova Scotia Highlanders), CEF (Service #222676)

WWII Leading Aircraftman (LAC) / Flight Mechanic

Lewis G. Billard

Glace Bay, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia

Born in the mining community of Glace Bay, Cape Breton, Lewis Billard joined the RCAF in 1943. He worked around the clock maintaining the powerful Rolls-Royce Merlin engines in Lancaster heavy bombers in England and Mosquito nightfighters in continental Europe. His 112 letters capture technical ingenuity, airbase life, and post-war Europe.

Branch: Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF)
Unit: RCAF Ground Crew (Aircraft Maintenance & Engine Fitter)

WWII Captain / Nursing Sister

Nurse Frances Charman

Joggins, Cumberland County, Nova Scotia

Born in the fossil-cliffs town of Joggins, Nova Scotia, Frances Charman graduated from Aberdeen Hospital in New Glasgow and nursed in Truro before enlisting in 1942. Rising to the rank of Captain, she treated critically wounded soldiers in mobile field hospitals across desert sandstorms in North Africa and the brutal mountain fighting in Italy.

Branch: Military Nursing Corps (Army Medical Corps)
Unit: Mobile Field Hospitals (Mediterranean Theater)

WWII Able Seaman

Kenneth Francis Henderson

Saint John, New Brunswick (Married in Halifax, NS)

A Maritime sailor who married Elsie Goodwin in Halifax in February 1944 before sailing for England. Aboard the Bangor-class minesweeper HMCS Thunder, Henderson took part in the dangerous opening hours of D-Day, clearing naval mines just two miles off the Normandy coast so Allied assault transports could land. Wrote the famous letter: 'It's the real thing this time darling. We are on our way in now...'

Branch: Royal Canadian Navy (RCN)
Unit: HMCS Thunder (Bangor-class Minesweeper), 31st Canadian Minesweeping Flotilla

WWII Flying Officer (Bomber Pilot)

Flying Officer Edwin Frederick ('Eddie') Patzer

Yorkton, Saskatchewan (Junior hockey star for Yorkton Terriers)

A star defenseman for the Yorkton Terriers junior hockey team, Eddie Patzer earned his wings in 1943 and captained a four-engine Halifax heavy bomber. Flew perilous nighttime bombing operations into the heavily fortified German Ruhr Valley. Tragically, on what was scheduled to be his 30th and final combat tour operation before returning home to Canada, his bomber was hit by anti-aircraft flak over Germany in February 1945.

Branch: Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF)
Unit: No. 432 'Leaside' Squadron, No. 6 (RCAF) Bomber Group (Service #J87362)

WWII Gunner

Ernest Albert Underwood

Born in Winnipeg, MB; Enlisted in Victoria, British Columbia

Enlisted in Victoria with his brother Leslie in 1939. His gun crew was credited as the first Canadians to shoot down an enemy aircraft (a Junkers-88) in August 1941. Deployed into the disastrous Dieppe Raid on August 19, 1942; wounded on the pebble beach, captured by German troops, and survived three years as a prisoner of war in German camps before liberation in 1945.

Branch: Royal Canadian Artillery (RCA)
Unit: 3rd Light Anti-Aircraft (LAA) Battery, RCA
