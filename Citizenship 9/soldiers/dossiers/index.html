Daniel Spencer Reid

Sergeant · Middle Musquodoboit, Halifax County, Nova Scotia

First World War (1914-1918) Nova Scotia Canadian Expeditionary Force (Highland Infantry)

A 19-year-old volunteer from Middle Musquodoboit in rural Halifax County who enlisted in October 1915 with the 85th Nova Scotia Highlanders. Rising to Sergeant, he wrote home steadily about Halifax camp routines, ocean convoys, and frontline mud before falling in the assault at Passchendaele on October 30, 1917 - a war that also claimed his brother Donald Calderwood Reid.

Unit: 85th Battalion (Nova Scotia Highlanders), Regimental #222676
Theater: Western Front: Vimy Ridge and Passchendaele, Belgium
Outcome: Killed in action at Passchendaele, October 30, 1917 (age 21)

Jeannette ('Nettie') Drysdale Bridges

V.A.D. Nursing Volunteer · Saint John, New Brunswick

First World War (1914-1919) New Brunswick Voluntary Aid Detachment (Military Hospital Service)

Born in Saint John, New Brunswick, in August 1889, 'Nettie' Drysdale Bridges crossed the Atlantic to serve as a Voluntary Aid Detachment (V.A.D.) nurse in British military hospitals throughout 1918 and 1919. Her rich archive preserves over thirty letters, an extensive wartime photograph album, telegrams, postcards, newspaper articles, and personal nursing artifacts.

Unit: V.A.D. Military Hospitals, England
Theater: Wartime & Pandemic Convalescent Hospitals, Great Britain (1918-1919)
Outcome: Survived wartime nursing and the 1918-1919 influenza crisis; returned to New Brunswick

Vincent Sanford MacCausland

Flying Officer · Tyne Valley, Prince County, Prince Edward Island

Second World War (1939-1945) Prince Edward Island Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF)

Born in Tyne Valley, Prince Edward Island, on February 1, 1913, Vincent MacCausland enlisted in the RCAF in March 1940 and was hand-picked for the ultra-secret No. 617 Squadron RAF. On the night of May 16-17, 1943, flying at treetop height into Germany's Ruhr Valley on the famous Dambusters Raid (Operation Chastise), his Lancaster bomber was shot down.

Unit: RCAF attached to No. 617 Squadron RAF ('The Dambusters'), Service #J15309
Theater: Bomber Command Night Operations over Germany (Operation Chastise)
Outcome: Killed in action during the Dambusters Raid, May 17, 1943 (age 30)

Michael Francis Murphy

Private · St. John's, Dominion of Newfoundland

First World War (1914-1918) Newfoundland Newfoundland Forces (Infantry)

Born in St. John's, Newfoundland, in February 1894, Michael Murphy enlisted on December 15, 1914 (Regimental #754) in 'C' Company of the Newfoundland Regiment. Fighting when Newfoundland was still a separate Dominion, he endured the trenches of Gallipoli, service in Egypt, and combat in France before returning home. His dossier holds wartime hospital and uniform photos, postcards, personal items, and printed records.

Unit: Royal Newfoundland Regiment, 'C' Company (Regimental #754)
Theater: Gallipoli (Ottoman Empire), Egypt, and Western Front (France)
Outcome: Wounded/hospitalized in 1918; survived Gallipoli and France and returned to St. John's

Reginald John Paul

Private · Burin, Burin Peninsula, Dominion of Newfoundland

First World War (1914-1918) Newfoundland Newfoundland Forces (Infantry)

Born on December 14, 1895, in the fishing outport of Burin, Newfoundland, Reginald Paul enlisted just after his nineteenth birthday in December 1914. Having survived the Gallipoli campaign, he went over the top at Beaumont-Hamel on the morning of July 1, 1916, where over 80 percent of the Newfoundland Regiment fell in half an hour. Two letters from the regimental chaplain break the news to his family.

Unit: 1st Battalion, Newfoundland Regiment
Theater: Gallipoli Peninsula & Beaumont-Hamel (Somme, France)
Outcome: Killed in action at Beaumont-Hamel on the first day of the Somme, July 1, 1916 (age 20)

Karl Vincent Butler

Lance Corporal · Sydney, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia

Second World War (1939-1945) Nova Scotia Canadian Army (Infantry)

Born in Sydney, Cape Breton, on May 6, 1912, Karl Butler served overseas as a Lance Corporal in the West Nova Scotia Regiment. After years of training in Britain writing over seventy warm letters home, he landed in the July 1943 invasion of Sicily and was killed in action during the mountain fighting near Regalbuto/Agira on August 2, 1943. His dossier includes telegrams, personal items, photos, clippings, and letters.

Unit: West Nova Scotia Regiment, R.C.I.C. (Service #F54612)
Theater: Britain & Allied Invasion of Sicily (Operation Husky), Italy
Outcome: Killed in action in Sicily, August 2, 1943 (age 31)

Edward James ('Joe Dowey') Power

Private · North Sydney, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia

Korean War (1950-1953) Nova Scotia Canadian Army (United Nations Command)

Born in North Sydney, Cape Breton, on July 10, 1931, Edward James Power - known to friends and family as 'Joe Dowey' - enlisted in Halifax on January 4, 1951, at age 19 to serve with the United Nations forces in Korea. Deployed with the PPCLI on the fortified ridge known as 'The Hook' along the 38th Parallel, he was killed by shrapnel on Christmas Eve, December 24, 1952, and laid to rest at the UN Cemetery in Busan.

Unit: Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI), Service #SF14075
Theater: 'The Hook' near the Samichon River, Korean Peninsula (38th Parallel)
Outcome: Killed in action by shrapnel on Christmas Eve, December 24, 1952 (age 21)

Clarence Reginald Gass

Corporal · Bayfield, Antigonish County, Nova Scotia

First World War (1914-1918) Nova Scotia Canadian Expeditionary Force (Highland Infantry)

Born in coastal Bayfield, Antigonish County, in April 1894, Clarence Gass enlisted in Halifax in November 1915 with the 85th Nova Scotia Highlanders and served right through to the end of the war. His family archive includes Western Union night telegrams, uniform photographs, field postcards, regimental printed matter, and over forty wartime letters.

Unit: 85th Battalion (Nova Scotia Highlanders), CEF
Theater: Halifax, Britain, France, and Belgium
Outcome: Survived three years of overseas service and returned home in 1919

Gunner · Truro, Colchester County, Nova Scotia

First World War (1914-1918) Nova Scotia Canadian Garrison / Siege Artillery

Born in Truro, Nova Scotia, in September 1899, Ludlow Weeks tried to enlist underage and was turned away before successfully joining the No. 10 Halifax Siege Battery in May 1917 at age 17. Serving overseas in 1918, he sent home more than forty letters along with postcards, photographs, newspaper clippings, and personal keepsakes.

Unit: No. 10 Halifax Siege Battery, CEF

Outcome: Survived overseas artillery service and returned to Nova Scotia

George Roderick Chisholm

Private · Pictou, Pictou County, Nova Scotia

First World War (1914-1918) Nova Scotia Canadian Expeditionary Force (Infantry)

Born in Pictou, Nova Scotia, on March 15, 1897, George Chisholm enlisted in April 1916 and deployed to France with the 78th Battalion. Three days before the Easter Monday assault on Vimy Ridge, he penned a short note from the forward assembly area; he was killed in action storming the ridge on April 9, 1917, just weeks past his twentieth birthday.

Unit: 78th Battalion (Winnipeg Grenadiers), CEF
Theater: Vimy Ridge, Northern France

Archie Keat

Private · Tatamagouche, Colchester County, Nova Scotia

First World War (1914-1918) Nova Scotia Canadian Expeditionary Force (Infantry)

Enlisted from rural Tatamagouche on Nova Scotia's North Shore and fought through frontline trench warfare with the 25th Battalion. On November 11, 1918, he marched into a newly liberated Belgian town as the guns fell silent, writing home that evening to his mother about cheering civilians tearing maple leaf badges off Canadian uniforms as souvenirs.

Unit: 25th Battalion (Nova Scotia Rifles), CEF
Theater: Western Front: Belgium and Northern France

Ralph Clement Gale

Private · Youngs Cove, Queens County, New Brunswick (Enlisted Amherst, NS)

First World War (1914-1918) New Brunswick Canadian Expeditionary Force (Mounted Rifles)

A 19-year-old schoolteacher from Youngs Cove, New Brunswick, who enlisted at Amherst, Nova Scotia, in April 1915. Captured during the ferocious Battle of Mount Sorrel near Ypres on June 2, 1916, he spent over two years as a Prisoner of War in Germany, writing home and appearing in camp photographs before dying of illness in Dortmund just months before the Armistice.

Unit: 6th & 4th Battalions, Canadian Mounted Rifles (Service #111184)
Theater: Mount Sorrel (Ypres, Belgium) & German POW Camps (Friedrichsfeld & Munster II)
Outcome: Captured June 2, 1916; died in a German POW hospital in Dortmund, July 29, 1918 (age 23)

John Teaton Gray

Able Seaman · Central Kingsclear (near Fredericton), New Brunswick

Second World War (1939-1945) New Brunswick Royal Canadian Naval Volunteer Reserve (RCNVR)

Born on a family farm in Central Kingsclear near Fredericton, New Brunswick, in November 1924, John Gray enlisted in the RCNVR shortly before his eighteenth birthday. Training at HMCS Brunswicker (Saint John), HMCS Cornwallis (Annapolis Basin), and HMCS Stadacona (Halifax) before going to sea aboard HMS Caldwell and the Tribal-class destroyer HMCS Huron, he sent letters, postcards, photos, and naval keepsakes home.

Unit: HMCS Brunswicker, HMCS Cornwallis, HMCS Stadacona & Destroyer HMCS Huron (#V50367)

Louis Wilfred Mullen

Gunner / Driver · Covehead, Queens County, Prince Edward Island

First World War (1914-1918) Prince Edward Island Canadian Field Artillery

Born in Covehead on Prince Edward Island's North Shore in April 1896, Louis Mullen travelled to Halifax in February 1916 to enlist in the Canadian Field Artillery, serving at the front with the 43rd Battery until the Armistice. He returned home with a remarkable wartime album of over one hundred photographs, personal items, embroidered postcards, and letters.

Unit: No. 11 Overseas Field Artillery Ammunition Column & 43rd Battery, CFA

Daniel Serrick

Staff Sergeant · Jollimore (Halifax), Nova Scotia

Second World War (1939-1945) Nova Scotia Airborne & Joint Special Operations

Born in Jollimore on Halifax's Northwest Arm in September 1920, Daniel Serrick lived one of the most extraordinary combat arcs of any Maritimer in WWII: evacuated off the beaches of Dunkirk with the British Army in June 1940, he volunteered successively for the British Commandos, the 1st Canadian Parachute Battalion, and the elite US-Canadian First Special Service Force ('Devil's Brigade'), falling in battle breaking out of Anzio toward Rome in May 1944.

Unit: British Commandos, 1st Canadian Parachute Bn & First Special Service Force (#U1805)
Theater: France (Dunkirk Evacuation, 1940) & Anzio Beachhead, Italy (1944)
Outcome: Evacuated at Dunkirk (1940); killed in action in Italy, May 29, 1944 (age 23)

Patricia Mary Jones Carter

Corporal · Halifax, Nova Scotia

Second World War (1939-1945) Nova Scotia Canadian Women's Army Corps (CWAC)

Living with her family in wartime Halifax when the Canadian Women's Auxiliary Corps (later CWAC) was created in August 1941, Patricia Carter enlisted almost immediately on October 17, 1941. Inspired in part by her father - WWI artillery veteran Gunner Albert Edward Carter - she recorded a detailed memoir of the opening six months of women's army service inside congested, blacked-out Halifax.

Unit: District Depot & Military District No. 6 Headquarters, Halifax
Theater: Wartime Fortress & Convoy Port of Halifax, Nova Scotia (1941-1943)
Outcome: Served in the pioneering 1941 cohort of the CWAC; authored a firsthand memoir

Frances Charman

Captain / Nursing Sister · Joggins, Cumberland County, Nova Scotia (Trained in New Glasgow & Truro)

Born in the Bay of Fundy coal town of Joggins, Nova Scotia, Frances Charman graduated as a nurse from New Glasgow's Aberdeen Hospital in 1926 and worked in Truro and Boston before enlisting in the Royal Canadian Army Medical Corps. Lewis G. Billard

Leading Aircraftman (LAC) · Glace Bay, Cape Breton, Nova Scotia

Second World War (1939-1945) Nova Scotia Royal Canadian Air Force (Ground Crew)

Born in 1923 in the coal-mining town of Glace Bay, Cape Breton, Lewis Billard joined the RCAF in 1943 as an aero-engine mechanic. Working night and day in freezing hangars and dispersal pads, he tuned the Rolls-Royce Merlin V-12 engines powering four-engine Lancaster heavy bombers in England and twin-engine Mosquito nightfighters in liberated Europe.

Unit: RCAF Aero-Engine Mechanic (Lancaster & Mosquito Squadrons)
Theater: Bomber & Fighter Airbases in England and Northwest Europe
Outcome: Survived WWII airbase and continental service; returned to Nova Scotia

Harry Austin McCleave

Lieutenant · Stewiacke, Colchester County, Nova Scotia

First World War (1914-1918) Nova Scotia Canadian Expeditionary Force (Infantry Officer)

Born in Stewiacke, Nova Scotia, in February 1892, Harry McCleave won Nova Scotia's prestigious 1915 Rhodes Scholarship to Balliol College, Oxford University. Putting Oxford on hold because of the war, he enlisted in Halifax in September 1915, kept a crisp daily wartime diary starting aboard RMS Adriatic in March 1916, and was killed in action in October 1916.

Unit: 64th Overseas Battalion & 13th Battalion (Royal Highlanders), CEF
Theater: Halifax, Atlantic Crossing (RMS Adriatic), and the Somme, France
Outcome: Killed in action in France, October 1916 (age 24)

Kenneth Francis Henderson

Able Seaman · Saint John, New Brunswick (Married in Halifax, NS)

Second World War (1939-1945) New Brunswick Royal Canadian Navy (RCN)

Born in Saint John, New Brunswick, in 1922 and married to Elsie Goodwin in Halifax in February 1944, Able Seaman Kenneth Henderson sailed aboard the minesweeper HMCS Thunder in the vanguard of D-Day. Sweeping German sea mines under coastal guns ahead of the landing craft on June 5-6, 1944, he kept a running letter to Elsie: 'It's the real thing this time darling. We are on our way in now...'

Unit: HMCS Thunder (Bangor-Class Minesweeper), 31st Canadian Minesweeping Flotilla
Theater: English Channel & Normandy Invasion Coast (D-Day, June 6, 1944)
Outcome: Survived D-Day minesweeping operations and postwar naval career

Norvin Smith Crawford

Trooper · New Brunswick

Second World War (1939-1945) New Brunswick Royal Canadian Armoured Corps (RCAC)

Enlisting early in New Brunswick (Regimental #G117), Trooper Norvin Crawford served as an armoured tank driver with New Brunswick's storied 8th Princess Louise's Hussars in the 5th Canadian Armoured Division. On September 1, 1944, during the fierce tank assault that cracked the German Gothic Line on Italy's Adriatic coast, Crawford was killed in action at age 28. His commanding officer wrote a deeply moving letter to his fiancee Grace Fulton.

Unit: 8th Princess Louise's (New Brunswick) Hussars, 5th Canadian Armoured Division (#G117)
Theater: Gothic Line Offensive, Italian Campaign (1944)
Outcome: Killed in action in Italy, September 1, 1944 (age 28)

Edwin Frederick ('Eddie') Patzer

Flying Officer · Yorkton, Saskatchewan

Second World War (1939-1945) Saskatchewan (National) Royal Canadian Air Force (Bomber Command)

A star junior hockey defenseman for the Yorkton Terriers, Eddie Patzer earned his RCAF pilot's wings in 1943 and captained a four-engine Halifax heavy bomber in No. 6 Canadian Bomber Group. On what was scheduled to be his 30th and final combat mission before rotating home to Canada in February 1945, his aircraft was struck by anti-aircraft flak over Germany.

Unit: No. 432 'Leaside' Squadron, No. 6 (RCAF) Group (Service #J87362)
Theater: Night Bomber Operations over Germany's Ruhr Valley
Outcome: Killed in action over Germany on his 30th and final tour mission, February 1945

Ernest Albert Underwood

Gunner · Born Winnipeg, MB; Enlisted Victoria, British Columbia

Second World War (1939-1945) British Columbia (National) Royal Canadian Artillery (RCA)

Enlisting in Victoria with his brother in 1939, Gunner Ernest Underwood crewed an anti-aircraft gun in Britain before landing in the disastrous Dieppe Raid on August 19, 1942. Wounded on the chert-stone beach and taken prisoner, he survived nearly three years of forced marches and captivity in German stalags, sending censored POW postcards and letters home until liberation in 1945.

Unit: 3rd Light Anti-Aircraft (LAA) Battery, RCA
Theater: Britain, Dieppe Raid (August 19, 1942), and German POW Camps (Stalag VIII-B)
Outcome: Wounded and captured at Dieppe (1942); survived nearly three years as a POW until 1945
