Allan Hamm

Private · CEF Overseas Forces (Recruited Charlottetown, 1917)

First World War (1914-1918) Prince Edward Island Bunbury (near Charlottetown), Prince Edward Island

Born in Bunbury across the Hillsborough River from Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island, in July 1897, Allan Hamm worked as a young Island carpenter before entering the army at Charlottetown in 1917. His family preserved over seventy wartime letters alongside Western Union telegrams, personal items, printed military documents, postcards, and photographs.

Where This Came From: All primary-source transcriptions, scans, and archival artifacts in this dossier come from the Allan Hamm Collection at The Canadian Letters & Images Project (canadianletters.ca), Vancouver Island University.

Archival Collection Note (Canadian Letters & Images Project)

Allan Hamm was born in Bunbury, Prince Edward Island in July, 1897. He worked as a carpenter prior to his recruitment in Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island in 1917 . The collection consists of more than seventy letters, as well as photographs, postcards, telegrams, and other items.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born July 1897 in Bunbury, Queens County, Prince Edward Island, where he worked as a carpenter.
  • Service Role: Entered the Canadian Expeditionary Force in Charlottetown in 1917, following his older brother Charles Bovyer Hamm, MM (enlisted 1915).
  • Mobilization: Reflects the 1917 wartime phase when Island tradesmen and farm sons were called up to replace heavy 1916-1917 casualties.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War (1914-1918). By 1917 Canada faced a severe manpower crisis overseas, drawing recruits from every PEI parish.
  • Theater: Training camps in Maritime Canada and Great Britain, supporting the Canadian Corps overseas.
  • Conditions: Constant correspondence back to Bunbury tracks camp life, wartime travel, mail delays, and news of wounded Island neighbours.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: His older brother Charles had already been wounded in September 1916, placing two sons of the same Bunbury household in harm's way.
  • Outcome: Both Hamm brothers survived the war, leaving behind more than 110 combined letters and artifacts.
  • Remembrance: Allan Hamm's personal items, postcards, and letters offer a vivid window into working-class Prince Edward Island life during the Great War.

January 22nd 1917 Letter

From: Mary Plummer · To: Mrs. Hamm
Canadian Field Comforts Commission, Moore Barracks, Shorncliffe. January 22 1917 To: Mrs. Hamm Charlottetown. P.E.I. Dear Madam, We have received many cards of thanks for the Christmas presents we sent to the men in France. As you so kindly subscribed towards these presents we thought you might like to have some of the answers. The Xmas card was made so that the back would tear off and it is on this that the men have written. The full amount of Â£2000 which we asked for was subscribed and this gave every man in France a present and we now send you all our sincere thanks for your help. Yours faithfully, (Mary Plummer) Capt. C.F.C.C.

March 6th 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst No. S. 6-3-18 Dear Mother:- Just a line to let you know we arrived in Amherst all sound. I am very sorry I did not get home to see you all but I did not know we were going till I was going down to barracks at ten o'clock so I couldn't get out then. Charlie came all the way with us too. I can tell you the bunch came in all right. Well as I have not seen the place yet I have nothing more to say. Good Bye Allan P.S. No3204020 Pte Allan Hamm H. Corp 1st Depot Battn N.S. Regt. Amherst N.S.

March 13th 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst N.S. 13-3-18 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I am well and having a good time. We all have been quarantined since last Saturday but I don't know what it is for a lot of the boys have a bad cold and some of them have gone to the hospital. There are about 800 hundred German prisoners here I was out to see them last Saturday afternoon. There is lots of snow over here it snowed about 6 inches every night since we came over but its nice and warm in the day time. How did Charlie get along at Halifax. Well as we don't get out I haven't any news this time. this is the second time I wrote and got no answer. Write Soon. Good Bye. Allan. No3204020 Pte H. Corp 1st Depot Bn N.S. Regt. Amherst N.S. P.S. I am sending you one of our Depot badges

March 21st 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst N.S. 21-3-18. Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I got your letter and am feeling fine only I am tired of this place already. We were only out two nights since we came over here but we get about a six mile rout march every day so that's all we get out now. A lot of the boys had spinal menigitis that's what we were quarantined for but there isn't anyone taking it now so I think it will be lifted Saturday. What is the matter with Herb Mason now that he had to go under the operation? Did Charlie get home yet? And how did he get along? I think you had better send me a telegram some time about the middle of the week saying some of you are very sick as I would like to get home for a week and that's the only condition they will give a pass on so be sure and send it or get the doctor to send one if you don't get a letter from me before 27-3-18. Well I guess I will close as that's all I ant you to do for me this time. Hoping you are all well. Good Bye Allan P.S. Tell Frank I will write to him after a while

March 27th 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst N.S. 27-3-18 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I am well received the message from G. T. Dewar today but can't get away on account of quarantine it was to be lifted Tuesday but Measles broke out and that's why I can't go. I never new till tonight why we were quarantined for it was spinal menigitis. They are carting them out there for four every morning now for measles. Did you get the money I signed home yet we never got a cent since we left the Island. I guess only for this quarantine we would be in England by now as we were to get no more pay in Canada but I think we are to get signed on the pay roll in Canada again for a while. Well I guess I will close for this time. Hoping you are all well. Good Bye. Allan P.S. Is Charlie home yet. Write soon and give me all the news tell dad I have no news or I would write to him. I heard Fred made another move

March 31st 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst N.S. 31-3-18 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I am well hoping you are all the same. We are having great weather over here now. The wagons are going around town now but the is plenty of snow in the country yet we were away out the road the other day for a route march and I guess there is more snow than on P.E.I. and its nothing but hills what we saw anyway was. We got out of quarantine yet and no word of it either. Elliott Jones was taken to the hospital the other day with Measles there is a great dose of them here now but they are very mild. You people must be very busy you can't write I only had two letters from you since I came here and I think this is the fourth one I wrote so. It is an awful hole over here the sooner we get out the better. Well I guess I will close for this time hoping to hear from you soon. Good Bye Allan P.S. You can send along something to eat as soon as you like I think I could manage it now

April 2nd 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst. N.S. 2-4-18 Dear Mother:- Received your most welcome letter to-day and was very glad to hear from you for I was looking for letters all week from home. We are having great weather here now and the streets are getting pretty bare now they were using wagons around the town two weeks ago. You talk about streets been in a mess but its here you will see them some of them are concrete and the water has no chance to soak away. We got paid to-day for the first time since we left the Island. If you don't soon get that money you want to look after it. don't leave it too long either. Well I guess I will close for this time hoping you are all well as it leaves me the same. Allan.

April 7th 1918 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Amherst, N.S. 7-4-18 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I am well & I hope you are are the same. We are having lovely weather over here now the streets are near all bare and the dust is flying on some of them. By they are concrete. Well its only 28 days since we were out at night and I haven't very much news. Well I guess you needn't answer this letter till you get another from me but it won't be from here I guess likely it will be on the transport I will write next. January 4th 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Barn House. Whitstable, Kent 4-1-19 Dear Mother:- Received your welcome letter today dated Dec. 20th and was glad to hear from you. We are having awful weather here now raining every day. All the Canadians that are able to leave hospital are going to a Convalscent camp pretty soon, and I don't know what they will do with us from there. Yes I got the two parcels with the chicken in them. I didn't have a parcel from you since about the 12th Dec. so I guess I didn't get the one for X-mas, but I had all I wanted and am getting lots of grub. now so don't send any more till I get settled down again as I am not likely to be a Convalscent very long. Well you needn't worry about my leg it is allright now its as good as ever it was. As I have no more news I must close hoping you are all well as it leaves me at present. Good Bye. Allan Hamm.

January 11th 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Hut 7. A Division. Can. Conv. Hosp. Wootcote Park Epsom. Surrey. 11-1-19 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know that I am well and hoping this letter finds you all the same. Well you will see that I have been moved again but don't expect to be here any longer than a couple of weeks and then I will be sent back to Bramshott again. Then we are sent from there away up to Wales to a demobalization camp and then we are sent home but it will take about six months to get through all that at the rate they are doing it now. We are having awful wet weather here and if we don't soon get away we will all be web toed, like ducks. I suppose you think I am going to have a big time on that Â£10 I wired for but it don't last long when a fellow has to pay for his feed at the rate they charge in this country for instance if you have a fried egg it only costs eleven cents. That's pretty hard luck for poor Vince having to loose his leg, I wrote to him a couple of times but got any answer, do they hear from him at home. Well I must close as news is scarce hoping to hear from you soon, Good Bye Allan.

January 21st 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Ward E. Manor War Hospital Epsom. Surrey. 21-1-19 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I was sent back to hospital again on the 17th as my leg went bad on me again it all swoll up and broke so it is open again but it isn't sore now at all and is going on allright again so don't worry about me. This is a much better hospital than the one I was in before of course there isn't near as many patients for to look after. We are having lovely weather here there has been about two fine days in over a month. I got a letter from the bank at Herne Bay saying my money was there, and I sent after it but didn't get it yet. Had a letter from El the other day they all had the flu is there very much around there and did any of youse have it yet. Is there any good chocolate there now? How is Vince Duffy getting along and send me his add. The last letter I wrote I think I told you to send my mail to Bramshott but you better send it here instead as I won't get there as quick as expected. I must close hoping you are all well Good Bye AHamm P.S. Tell Addie I got her letter and will answer when I get some news. ByeBye.

January 26th 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Epsom Surrey, 26-1-19 Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know that I am still in bed and getting along allright - again. I think its pretty near time that I was out of bed for to stay now as I have done ten weeks so far. Well we are having pretty good weather here now. Are there many soldiers coming home now? or have they got Victoria Park filled up training soldiers for the next war. How is Charlie getting along? Well as I have writers cramp along with not having any news I must close hoping you are all well. Write soon and give me all the news of the settlement. Good Bye Pte, AHamm-

January 31st 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Ward E. Manor War Hosp. Epsom. Surrey. 31-1-19 Dear Mother: Just a few lines to let you know I am getting along allright now. I got a parcel from you Wednesday that you mailed on Jan 6th. We are having rotten weather here it either rains or snows every day and is awful foggy along with it. I don't know where your letters are going because I haven't had any for quite awhile. Is it very cold there this winter and is there very much snow? I guess you will think I must be having a hard time when I am back in hospital again. I was only in bed for a week this time so it isn't anything for to worry over, it was a piece of bone that was working out so I guess it will be allright this time. Well as I have no news I must close hoping you are all well. Write soon and give me all the news. Good Bye AHamm

February 10th 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
Ward E. Manor War Hosp Epsom Surrey Dear Mother:- Received two of your welcome letters yesterday dated Jan 2nd and 14th and was glad to hear from you I thought you must have all been dead it was over three weeks since I had any letters from home. I guess I had better answer all you questions. I aint so very thin, I sleep well and could eat a donkey in a fit so how will that do. We are having nice weather here lately fine and cold. You needn't be looking for me to be home before spring anyway when it gets good and warm, there. but I wont refuse going before that if I get a chance. I am getting along farelly good but it will be quite awhile before my leg is healed up. Tell Addie all the news I got is in this letter so there is no use in me writing the same to her. I must close for now Write soon. Good Bye Allan

February 22nd 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
J2 Ward 15th Can. Gen. Hosp Taplow. Berks. Dear Mother:- Just a few lines to let you know I am well hoping this letter finds you all the same my leg is all healed again and I expect to be out of hospital in a couple of weeks. You will see that I have been moved again they are moving us every where around except home. We are having another spell of wet weather here now. Do you ever take time to write a letter I haven't had any for quite awhile. Is any of the boys from around the settlement - home yet? Well I think I will have to close as this is such a lively place there is now news. Write Soon. Good Bye Allan.

March 2nd 1919 Letter

From: Allan · To: Mother
J2 Ward 12th Can. Gen. Hosp. Taplow Berkes. 2-3-19 Dear Mother:- Received two of your welcome letters the other day dated Jan 24 and Feb 6th and was glad to hear from you. Well I might as well tell you for to give up expecting me home for a couple of months by what I hear all A.1. men go back to the reserve and wait for their division till it comes from France so the one I am in doesen't leave France till some time in April. Did Meadie Beaton marry a Widower like the rest of them did. I am going on leave tomorrow morning for a few days. I must close. Good Bye. Allan. P.S. type this add on my letters until I get back to the reserve 85th Battn 17th Can. Reserve Ripon, Yorkshire England.
