Thomas (Tommy) Ricketts, VC

Sergeant (Private when awarded the VC) · 1st Battalion, Royal Newfoundland Regiment

First World War (1916-1919) Newfoundland St. John's, Newfoundland (born at Middle Arm, White Bay)

A teenage soldier from Newfoundland who joined the army at 15 and won the Victoria Cross at 17 when he ran back for ammunition under fire, then used his Lewis gun to stop the enemy getting their field guns away. He is the youngest Victoria Cross recipient in a combat role in any army, and he spent his life as a quiet St. John's pharmacist.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born April 15, 1901 at Middle Arm, White Bay, Newfoundland. He later lived in St. John's and attended Bishop Feild School.
  • Enlistment: He joined the Newfoundland Regiment in 1916 when he was only 15 years old.
  • Newfoundland: Newfoundland was a separate dominion until 1949, so Ricketts was a Newfoundland soldier, not a Canadian one.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War, final Hundred Days. On October 14, 1918 the advance from Ledeghem in Belgium was held up by fire from a battery of field guns.
  • The Action: Private Ricketts and his section commander took a Lewis gun forward. When their ammunition ran out and the enemy began to bring up gun teams, he ran back 100 yards for more and returned to drive the enemy off.
  • The Result: His platoon then advanced without casualties and captured four field guns, four machine guns and eight prisoners.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: He survived the war and went home to Newfoundland. One biography cited by Wikipedia is titled 'Newfoundland's Reluctant War Hero'.
  • Honours: The King presented his Victoria Cross on January 19, 1919, reportedly saying: 'This is the youngest VC in my army.' France awarded him the Croix de Guerre with Gold Star.
  • Legacy: He studied pharmacy, ran a drugstore on Water Street in St. John's, and was given a state funeral when he died on February 10, 1967. His medals are displayed at The Rooms.

October 14th 1918 (published January 4th 1919) Official Citation

From: Victoria Cross citation, London Gazette
"During the advance from Ledeghem (Belgium) the attack was temporarily held up by heavy hostile fire, and the platoon to which he belonged suffered severe casualties from the fire of a battery at point blank range. Private Ricketts at once volunteered to go forward with his Section Commander and a Lewis gun to attempt to outflank the battery. They advanced by short rushes while subject to severe fire from enemy machine guns. When 300 yards away, their ammunition gave out. The enemy, seeing an opportunity to get their field guns away, began to bring up their gun teams. Private Ricketts at once realized the situation. He doubled back 100 yards, procured some ammunition and dashed back to the Lewis gun, and by very accurate fire drove the enemy and their gun teams into a farm. His platoon then advanced without casualties, and captured four field guns, four machine guns and eight prisoners. A fifth field gun was subsequently intercepted by fire and captured. By his presence of mind in anticipating the enemy intention and his utter disregard for personal safety, Private Ricketts secured the further supplies of ammunition which directly resulted in these important captures and undoubtedly saved many lives"
Source Verification: View at The London Gazette, Supplement 31108 (January 4, 1919), p. 309

January 19th 1919 Investiture

Archival Transcription
King George V presented Ricketts with his Victoria Cross at York Cottage on the Sandringham Estate on January 19, 1919. At the ceremony the King reportedly said: "This is the youngest VC in my army." General Dighton Probyn, one of the oldest living VC holders at the time, was also present. Ricketts, at 17, is the youngest VC recipient enrolled in an army in a combatant role. He is not the youngest recipient of all: two boys aged 15 received the VC in non-combatant roles, and Jack Cornwell of the Royal Navy, aged 16, was honoured after his death at Jutland.
Source Verification: View at Wikipedia: Thomas Ricketts (VC)

1919-1967 Later Life

Archival Transcription
After the war Ricketts studied pharmacy and opened a business on Water Street in St. John's. He was the youngest living VC recipient from the time of his award until 1940. He was given a state funeral when he died in 1967 and is buried in the Anglican Cemetery on Forest Road. His medals are on display at The Rooms in St. John's. Wikipedia notes that Ricketts is sometimes mistakenly called a Canadian soldier. Newfoundland was a self-governing dominion at the time and did not become a province of Canada until 1949. In 2018 the Tommy Ricketts Memorial Peace Park opened in Conception Bay South, and a monument stands at Sint-Eloois-Winkel near Ledeghem in Belgium.
