Rev. William A. White and No. 2 Construction Battalion

Honorary Captain (Chaplain) · No. 2 Construction Battalion, CEF (attached to the Canadian Forestry Corps)

First World War (1916-1919) Nova Scotia Truro and Halifax, Nova Scotia (born in Virginia)

Born to formerly enslaved parents in Virginia, William Andrew White became a Baptist minister in Truro and then chaplain to No. 2 Construction Battalion, the segregated Black unit raised in Nova Scotia after Black volunteers were turned away as 'a white man's war'. He was one of the very few Black officers in the Canadian army, and his diary became the basis for the film Honour Before Glory.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born June 16, 1874 in Virginia to formerly enslaved parents. A teacher in Washington, D.C. encouraged him to study at Acadia University in Wolfville, Nova Scotia, where he graduated in 1903.
  • Minister: He became a Baptist minister, worked as a travelling missionary, and in 1905 became pastor of Zion United Baptist Church in Truro. He and his wife Izie had 13 children, including the singer Portia White.
  • The Unit: No. 2 Construction Battalion was authorized on July 5, 1916 after Black Canadians who tried to enlist were told it was a 'white man's war'.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War. The battalion was a support unit, not a combat one, and was headquartered first in Pictou and then in Truro.
  • Overseas: It sailed from Halifax on March 28, 1917 on the SS Southland. Because it was understrength it became No. 2 Construction Company, attached to the Canadian Forestry Corps in the Jura Mountains of France.
  • The Work: The men cut and milled lumber for trenches and duckboards, ran railways and water systems, and once milled 95,000 board feet in ten hours.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Even in uniform, Black soldiers were treated as second-class. White reported that nearby units refused to accept him as their chaplain.
  • Aftermath: The battalion returned to Halifax in January 1919 and was disbanded in 1919-1920. Its service faded from public memory.
  • Legacy: Canada recognised the battalion as an event of national historic significance in 1992, awarded it a battle honour on June 1, 2022, and apologised for its treatment in July 2022.

1914-1916 Historical Source Note

Archival Transcription
With the outbreak of the First World War, hundreds of Black Canadians eager to serve went to recruiting stations. At the time few Black people were serving in the Canadian military because of the racial attitudes then prevalent, and most who tried to enlist were rejected and told it was a "White man's war." The Department of Militia and Defence left the decision to each commanding officer, and since many held deeply ingrained beliefs about the inferiority of Black people, very few were accepted. On May 11, 1916 the War Office approved forming a Black unit, and on July 5, 1916 No. 2 Construction Battalion was authorized.

1916-1919 Unit History

Archival Transcription
The battalion's headquarters was first in Pictou, Nova Scotia, and moved to Truro in September 1916. It was commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Daniel Hugh Sutherland. Its strength reached just over 729 men in March 1917. Although it was a Black unit, all but one of its 19 officers were white; the exception was the chaplain, William A. White. The unit left Halifax on the SS Southland on March 28, 1917. By the fall of 1917 it was working in the Jura Mountains of France, headquartered at La Joux, where the men felled trees, hauled the logs to mills and milled lumber for the front. They also cared for 70 to 100 horses, drove trucks, maintained roads, built a railroad and ran camp water pumps and a power plant. Working with No. 21 Company of the Canadian Forestry Corps they once set a record of 95,000 board measures of wood in ten hours.
Source Verification: View at Wikipedia: No. 2 Construction Battalion (and The Canadian Encyclopedia)

1916-1936 Biographical Source Note

Archival Transcription
William Andrew White was a leading member of the African Nova Scotian community. The Canadian Encyclopedia points out that although the battalion's other officers were white, White served as its chaplain with the rank of honorary captain, which made him one of the very few Black officers in the Canadian Expeditionary Force and the British Empire. He worked hard and was always concerned about his soldiers' well-being, but reported that nearby units refused to accept him as a chaplain even when they had none. After the war he became pastor of Cornwallis Street Baptist Church in Halifax, pioneered monthly radio broadcasts of his services, and in 1936 became the first Black person honoured with a Doctor of Divinity by Acadia University. He died on September 9, 1936. The 2001 documentary Honour Before Glory, produced by his great-nephew Anthony Sherwood, is based on White's wartime diary, which records hardships such as the lack of proper clothing and medical care.
Source Verification: View at The Canadian Encyclopedia (John Boileau, 2019)

1982-2022 Recognition Timeline

Archival Transcription
In 1992 the Government of Canada designated No. 2 Construction Battalion an event of national historic significance, and a plaque was placed in Pictou the following year. On June 1, 2022 the battalion was awarded the theatre of war honour "France and Flanders, 1917-18". In July 2022 Prime Minister Justin Trudeau apologised for the "blatant anti-Black hate and systemic racism" endured by the battalion's soldiers. The Nova Scotia Archives hosts a searchable nominal roll of the men who served, so students can look up individual soldiers from the battalion.
