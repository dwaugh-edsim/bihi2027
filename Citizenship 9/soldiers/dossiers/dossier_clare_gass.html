Clare Gass

Nursing Sister (Lieutenant) · No. 3 Canadian General Hospital; No. 2 Canadian Casualty Clearing Station

First World War (1915-1919) Nova Scotia Shubenacadie, Hants County, Nova Scotia

A nursing sister from Shubenacadie, Nova Scotia, who kept three secret diaries through the First World War. One of them recorded John McCrae's poem 'In Flanders Fields' six weeks before it was published. Her diaries are now a valuable record of life in wartime hospitals.

Where This Came From: This dossier is built from the Wikipedia article on Clare Gass, which cites the published diary (The War Diary of Clare Gass, 1915-1918, edited by Susan Mann, McGill-Queen's University Press, 2000), the East Hants Historical Society and CBC News. It is not a canadianletters.ca collection.

Three-Paragraph Remembrance Report Notes

Target Length: ~300 Words Total (90-110 Words Per Paragraph)
Paragraph 1
Identity & Origins
  • Hometown: Born March 18, 1887 in Shubenacadie, Nova Scotia, the eldest child and only daughter of a general store and lumber mill owner.
  • Training: After four years of private schooling in Windsor, she trained as a nurse at the Montreal General Hospital from 1909 to 1912.
  • Enlistment: In April 1915 she joined the Canadian Army Medical Corps as a nursing sister with No. 3 Canadian General Hospital.
Paragraph 2
Conflict & Strategic Context
  • Conflict & Mandate: First World War. Nursing sisters ran the hospitals that received wounded soldiers from the front.
  • Service: She helped set up the first tented hospital at Camiers, France in the summer of 1915, then in November 1917 moved to No. 2 Canadian Casualty Clearing Station at Remy Siding, Belgium, closer to the front.
  • Under Fire: During the German spring offensive of 1918 she endured air raids and evacuations.
Paragraph 3
Human Cost & Legacy
  • Human Cost: Her brother Blanchard Victor and a second cousin were both killed at Vimy Ridge in April 1917.
  • The Diary: Against military rules she kept three detailed diaries. One copied out 'In Flanders Fields' six weeks before it was first published, after John McCrae shared it with her and she urged him to send it to Punch.
  • Legacy: After the war she led the social service department at the Montreal General Hospital for 28 years and helped develop medical social work in Canada. She died in Halifax on August 5, 1968.

1915 (diary) Diary Source Note

Archival Transcription
Gass kept three detailed diaries throughout the war, against the military rules of the time. One of them recorded John McCrae's poem "In Flanders Fields" six weeks before it was first published. After McCrae wrote the poem he showed it to Gass and asked for her feedback; she encouraged him to send it to the magazine Punch and copied it into her diary. The diaries were edited by Susan Mann and published as The War Diary of Clare Gass, 1915-1918 by McGill-Queen's University Press in 2000.
Source Verification: View at Wikipedia: Clare Gass (citing S. Mann, CBC News)

1915-1919 Service Record

Archival Transcription
In April 1915 Gass joined the Canadian Army Medical Corps as a nursing sister at No. 3 Canadian General Hospital and served mostly in France, helping to set up the first tented hospital at Camiers in the summer of 1915. Her brother Blanchard Victor and second cousin Laurence Henderson were both killed at Vimy Ridge in April 1917. In November 1917 she was posted to No. 2 Canadian Casualty Clearing Station at Remy Siding in Flanders, Belgium, closer to the front lines, where she worked seven months on day and night duty in several wards. As a member of the military and a relative of soldiers she was allowed to vote in the 1917 federal election. During the German spring offensive of 1918 she endured air raids and evacuations. She later escorted sick and wounded soldiers back to Canada and was demobilized in November 1919.
Source Verification: View at Wikipedia: Clare Gass

1920-1968 After the War

Archival Transcription
After the war Gass studied social service at McGill University and worked for the Family Welfare Association of Montreal. In 1924 she became director of the social service department at the Western Division of the Montreal General Hospital, a post she held for 28 years, and she helped develop professional medical social work in Canada. She retired in 1952 and returned to Shubenacadie, where she took part in local history, drama, photography, poetry and music, and was a charter member of the East Hants Historical Society in 1967. She never married and died on August 5, 1968 at Camp Hill Hospital in Halifax at age 81.
