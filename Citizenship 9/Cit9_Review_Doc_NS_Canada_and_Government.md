# SECTION 1: Nova Scotia Regional Geography (The 8 Places from the Worksheet)

In Class 04/05, we completed the **Nova Scotia Regional Navigator**. Nova Scotia is not just one uniform place—it is made up of distinct geographic regions, each with its own industry, cultural heritage, and geographic identity.

### 🗺️ The 8 Core Nova Scotia Locations & Geographic Anchors

| # | Region / Town | Geographic Anchor & Location | Key Identity, Industry & Historic Significance | Worksheet Riddle Clue |
|:---:|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **1** | **Amherst** | Cumberland County &bull; Sits on the narrow **Chignecto Isthmus** | **"The Land Bridge to Canada":** Border gateway physically connecting peninsular Nova Scotia to New Brunswick and the rest of North America. | *CLUE #4: Land bridge border town on the Chignecto Isthmus.* |
| **2** | **Truro** | Central Nova Scotia &bull; At the head of Cobequid Bay & Salmon River | **"The Hub of Nova Scotia":** The geographic crossroads where Highway 102 (from Halifax) and Highway 104 (Trans-Canada) meet, and where all major CN rail lines intersect. | *CLUE #2: The Hub where major highways and railways cross.* |
| **3** | **Annapolis Valley** *(Kentville / Wolfville)* | Sheltered along the Bay of Fundy & Minas Basin, protected by the North and South Mountains | **"The Agricultural Valley":** The farming and orchard heartland of NS; famous for Honeycrisp apples, cider, vineyards, rich dykelands, and extreme Fundy tides. | *CLUE #1: Rich valley famous for apples, agriculture, and cider.* |
| **4** | **Halifax & Dartmouth** *(HRM)* | Central Atlantic Coast on Halifax Harbour | **"Capital & Navy Fleet":** Provincial capital, economic hub, home to Province House (oldest legislature in Canada, 1819), and CFB Halifax (major Canadian naval base). | *CLUE #6: Capital city on an ice-free harbour with naval fleet.* |
| **5** | **Lunenburg** | South Shore along the Atlantic Ocean | **"The Bluenose Shipyard":** UNESCO World Heritage waterfront town famous for fisheries and shipbuilding; built the iconic racing schooner *Bluenose* (found on the Canadian 10¢ dime). | *CLUE #3: Historic South Shore shipyard that built the Bluenose.* |
| **6** | **Yarmouth** | Far Southwestern tip of peninsular Nova Scotia | **"Southwest Gateway & Lobster Port":** Leading commercial lobster fishing port, Acadian French cultural coast, and international ferry terminal connecting NS to Bar Harbor, Maine. | *CLUE #7: Southwest tip with major lobster fleet and ferry to Maine.* |
| **7** | **New Glasgow & Pictou County** | Industrial North Shore along the Northumberland Strait | **"Birthplace of New Scotland & Sobeys":** Where the Scottish immigrant ship *Hector* landed in 1773; industrial coal/foundry history; founding home of Sobeys grocery empire (1907). | *CLUE #8: Northumberland shore town where the Hector landed & Sobeys began.* |
| **8** | **Cape Breton** *(Unama'kik)* | Island forming the northeastern part of Nova Scotia (separated from mainland by Strait of Canso) | **"Island of Steel, Coal & Culture":** Connected to mainland via the **Canso Causeway**; famous for Gaelic/Celtic culture, Mi'kmaw heritage, coal and steel heritage, the Bras d'Or Lake, and the Cabot Trail. | *CLUE #5: Historic island of steel, coal, Gaelic culture, and Cabot Trail.* |

### 🌊 4 Waterways & Physical Features Every Grade 9 Citizen Must Know:
1. **Bay of Fundy:** World's highest tides; borders the Annapolis Valley and northern shore of mainland NS.
2. **Northumberland Strait:** Shallow, warm strait separating mainland Nova Scotia and Pictou from Prince Edward Island.
3. **Chignecto Isthmus:** The narrow 24-kilometer strip of land connecting Nova Scotia to New Brunswick (near Amherst).
4. **Canso Causeway:** The rock-fill causeway constructed in 1955 across the Strait of Canso that connects mainland Nova Scotia to Cape Breton Island.

# SECTION 2: Places in Canada (10 Provinces, 3 Territories & Capitals)

Canadian citizens must know the political geography of our federation. Below is the complete West-to-East breakdown of Canada’s 10 provinces and 3 northern territories, along with the **common traps** that trip students up on tests.

### 🍁 The 10 Canadian Provinces (West to East)

| # | Province | Postal Code | Capital City | ⚠️ Common Student Trap / Important Note |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | **British Columbia** | `BC` | **Victoria** | **TRAP:** Vancouver is NOT the capital! Victoria is located on Vancouver Island. |
| **2** | **Alberta** | `AB` | **Edmonton** | **TRAP:** Calgary is NOT the capital! Edmonton is the provincial capital. |
| **3** | **Saskatchewan** | `SK` | **Regina** | **TRAP:** Saskatoon is NOT the capital! Regina is the provincial capital. |
| **4** | **Manitoba** | `MB` | **Winnipeg** | Home to the Canadian Museum for Human Rights and the Forks. |
| **5** | **Ontario** | `ON` | **Toronto** | Canada's most populous province and largest metropolitan city. |
| **6** | **Quebec** | `QC` | **Quebec City** | **TRAP:** Montreal is NOT the capital! Quebec City (*Ville de Québec*) is the capital. |
| **7** | **New Brunswick** | `NB` | **Fredericton** | **TRAP:** Moncton and Saint John are NOT capitals! NB is Canada's **only officially bilingual province**. |
| **8** | **Prince Edward Island** | `PE` | **Charlottetown** | **"Cradle of Confederation":** Site of the historic September 1864 Conference. |
| **9** | **Nova Scotia** | `NS` | **Halifax** | Province House is the oldest sitting legislative assembly in Canada (1819). |
| **10** | **Newfoundland and Labrador** | `NL` | **St. John's** | Easternmost city in North America; joined Canadian Confederation in 1949. |

| # | Territory / Nation | Code | Capital City | Geographic / Civic Anchor |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **11** | **Yukon** | `YT` | **Whitehorse** | Borders Alaska; historic Klondike Gold Rush country. |

### 🧠 The 5 "Big City" Traps to Avoid on the Quiz:
* **British Columbia:** The capital is **Victoria**, NOT Vancouver.
* **Alberta:** The capital is **Edmonton**, NOT Calgary.
* **Saskatchewan:** The capital is **Regina**, NOT Saskatoon.
* **Quebec:** The capital is **Quebec City**, NOT Montreal.
* **New Brunswick:** The capital is **Fredericton**, NOT Moncton or Saint John.
* **Canada:** The national capital is **Ottawa**, NOT Toronto.

# SECTION 3: The 3 Levels of Government

Canada is a federal system. Government is divided into **three levels** so that different sizes of problems are solved by the people closest to them.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        FEDERAL GOVERNMENT                              │
│  Location: Ottawa (Parliament Hill) • Leader: Prime Minister           │
│  Scope: Whole country issues (Defence, Passports, Currency, Criminal)  │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
┌───────────────────────────────────▼────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       PROVINCIAL GOVERNMENT                            │
│  Location: Halifax (Province House) • Leader: Premier                  │
│  Scope: Province-wide public services (Health, Schools, 100-Highways)  │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │ (Delegated Authority)
┌───────────────────────────────────▼────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       MUNICIPAL GOVERNMENT                             │
│  Location: City Hall (HRM) • Leader: Mayor                             │
│  Scope: Daily neighbourhood services (Transit, Snow Plowing, Garbage)  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Federal Government (National)
* **Where It Meets:** Parliament Buildings in **Ottawa** (House of Commons & Senate).
* **Constitutional Basis:** **Section 91** of the *Constitution Act, 1867* (enumerated powers for the whole country).
* **Leader:** **Prime Minister** (Head of Government; leader of the party holding the most seats in Parliament).
* **Elected Representative:** **Member of Parliament (MP)** (represents a federal riding, e.g., *Dartmouth—Cole Harbour*).
* **Law Made:** **Statutes / Acts** (e.g., *Criminal Code of Canada*, *Youth Criminal Justice Act*).
* **Key Responsibilities:**
  * National Defence & the Canadian Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force)
  * Criminal Law & Justice (setting crimes and consequences that apply everywhere in Canada)
  * Passports, Immigration & Border Security (entering Canada)
  * Money, Currency & Minting (Bank of Canada, $20 bills, coins)
  * Foreign Affairs, International Treaties & Trade/Tariffs (taxes on imported goods)
  * Canada Post & Postal Service
  * Fisheries & Marine Navigation

### 2. Provincial Government (Province-Wide)
* **Where It Meets:** Province House in **Halifax** (Nova Scotia House of Assembly).
* **Constitutional Basis:** **Section 92** of the *Constitution Act, 1867* (powers of a provincial or local nature).
* **Leader:** **Premier** (Leader of the provincial government and cabinet in Nova Scotia).
* **Elected Representative:** **Member of the Legislative Assembly (MLA)** (represents a provincial district, e.g., *Dartmouth South*, *Dartmouth North*).
* **Law Made:** **Provincial Statutes & Ministerial Directives** (e.g., the NS public school student cellphone directive).
* **Key Responsibilities:**
  * Healthcare & Hospitals (Emergency rooms, clinics, doctors, Nova Scotia Health)
  * Public Education (Grade P-12 schools, Dartmouth High/Bicentennial, curriculum, school rules)
  * Major Highways & Transportation (100-series highways like Hwy 102, Cobequid Pass tolls)
  * Driver's Licenses & Motor Vehicles (Access Nova Scotia)
  * Provincial Parks & Natural Resources (forestry, provincial Crown land, mining)
  * Provincial Labour Laws & Minimum Wage ($16.75/hr in April 2026, rising to $17.00/hr in Oct 2026)

### 3. Municipal Government (Local / City)
* **Where It Meets:** City Hall (Halifax Regional Municipality / HRM).
* **Constitutional Basis:** **Delegated Powers** — Municipalities are **not** in the Constitution. They exist only because the provincial government creates them under the *Municipal Government Act*. The province gives them power; a city cannot invent powers the province hasn't delegated!
* **Leader:** **Mayor** (Elected across the whole municipality to lead Regional Council).
* **Elected Representative:** **City Councillor** (represents a local city district, e.g., *District 5: Dartmouth Centre*).
* **Law Made:** **Bylaws** (local rules passed by City Council that only apply within municipal boundaries).
* **Key Responsibilities:**
  * Public Transit (Halifax Transit buses and Alderney/Woodside ferries)
  * Local Streets, Potholes & Winter Snow Clearing (plowing side streets and sidewalks in Dartmouth)
  * Garbage, Green Cart Composting & Blue Bag Recycling Collection
  * Tap Water, Reservoirs & Sewage Systems (Halifax Water)
  * City Parks, Playgrounds, Skateparks & Recreation Facilities (e.g., Zatzman Sportsplex)
  * Local Land-Use & Residential Zoning (deciding what kind of housing or stores can be built on a street)
  * Local Policing (Halifax Regional Police) & Municipal Fire Services

# SECTION 4: Key Positions & Elected Representatives

A major skill of an active citizen is knowing **who has the power to solve your problem**. When citizens complain to the wrong person, nothing gets fixed.

### 🏛️ Leadership & Representative Matrix

| Position | Level | Building / Chamber | How They Are Chosen | Who They Represent | What They Handle |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Prime Minister** | **Federal** | House of Commons, Ottawa | Leader of the political party winning the most seats in Parliament | All of Canada | National government direction, federal cabinet, national defence, national laws. |
| **Premier** | **Provincial** | Province House, Halifax | Leader of the political party winning the most seats in the provincial election | The Province of Nova Scotia | Provincial government direction, healthcare system, public education, provincial budget. |
| **Mayor** | **Municipal** | HRM City Hall, Halifax | Elected by a majority vote of all residents across the entire municipality | Halifax Regional Municipality | Leads Regional Council, chairs council debates, acts as the public face of the city. |
| **Member of Parliament (MP)** | **Federal** | Parliament Hill, Ottawa | Elected by voters in one federal **riding / constituency** | Local riding residents (~90,000 citizens) | Passports, federal taxes, immigration/visas, Canadian military issues, federal laws. |
| **Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA)** | **Provincial** | Nova Scotia Legislature, Halifax | Elected by voters in one provincial **electoral district** | Local district residents (~15,000–20,000 citizens) | Hospital and ER wait times, school resources, provincial roads/highways, driver licensing. |
| **City Councillor** | **Municipal** | HRM Council Chambers, City Hall | Elected by voters in one municipal **district** | Local neighbourhood residents | Transit routes/schedules, street snow plowing, neighbourhood potholes, parks, city bylaws. |

### 👑 The Crown in Canada (Constitutional Monarchy)
* **Head of State:** **King Charles III** is Canada’s constitutional monarch. He reigns but does not rule; elected officials run the day-to-day government.
* **Federal Crown Representative:** The **Governor General** (represents the King federally in Ottawa).
* **Provincial Crown Representative:** The **Lieutenant Governor of Nova Scotia** (represents the King provincially in Halifax).

## 🎯 "Who Do I Contact?" — Citizen Problem Triage

If you run into these everyday issues in Dartmouth or Nova Scotia, which representative should you call?

| Real-World Problem / Scenario | Correct Level | Who You Contact | Why This Official? |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1. Your family's passport applications are delayed 3 weeks before an international trip.** | **Federal** | **MP** *(Member of Parliament)* | Passports and international border travel are exclusively federal. |
| **2. Emergency Room wait times at Dartmouth General Hospital hit 12 hours.** | **Provincial** | **MLA** *(Member of Legislative Assembly)* | Healthcare, hospitals, and medical staffing are managed by the provincial government. |
| **3. A massive pothole on Victoria Road bends your car wheel rim.** | **Municipal** | **City Councillor / 311** | Local street maintenance, paving, and pothole repairs are run by HRM. |
| **4. The provincial government changes the grade school curriculum and cellphone rules.** | **Provincial** | **MLA** *(Member of Legislative Assembly)* | Education, curriculum, and school directives are provincial jurisdiction. |
| **5. The Halifax Transit #54 bus route is repeatedly cancelled with no notice.** | **Municipal** | **City Councillor** | Public transit (buses and ferries) is operated and funded by the municipal city council. |
| **6. The federal government passes new updates to the Youth Criminal Justice Act.** | **Federal** | **MP** *(Member of Parliament)* | Criminal law and youth justice are federal statutes enacted in Ottawa. |
| **7. A neighbouring apartment plays booming music at 3:30 AM every weeknight.** | **Municipal** | **City Bylaw Officer / HRM** | Noise curfews are municipal bylaws passed and enforced by the city. |
| **8. Provincial income tax rates or minimum wage rules are changed in Nova Scotia.** | **Provincial** | **MLA** *(Member of Legislative Assembly)* | Provincial taxation and labour standards are provincial laws. |
