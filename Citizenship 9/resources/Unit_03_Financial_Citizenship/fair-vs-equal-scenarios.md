# Fair vs. Equal — Scenario Cards (Class 29 Gallery Walk)

**Protocol:** 10 scenarios posted around the room. Students walk silently (5 min), write Agree/Disagree + one-line justification on each sheet. Then pick a side of the room on the **fair / not fair spectrum** for the two flagged scenarios and defend. Every card is deliberately contestable — there is no answer key on purpose.

**Frame vocabulary (on the board):** *equality* = everyone gets the same · *equity* = people get what they need to reach a fair outcome · *fairness* = a judgment about whether the outcome is just.

---

1. Two cashiers at the same grocery store do the same job. One has been there 6 years and earns $1.50/hr more. **That's fair if…** (finish the sentence)
2. A 19-year-old inherits $400,000 and pays no tax on it. A 19-year-old working full-time pays tax on every paycheque.
3. Reserves in Canada have less access to clean drinking water than most Canadian cities.
4. A carbon tax raises fuel prices. Rural low-income drivers pay more of their income than city high-income drivers.
5. A scholarship goes to the student with the highest marks. A different scholarship goes to the student with the greatest financial need.
6. The government gives every household the same $500 cost-of-living cheque.
7. A landlord raises the rent on a unit by 5% because "the market allows it."
8.EI benefits replace 55% of a lost paycheque — but only up to a yearly maximum.
9. Two restaurants pay differently: one tips staff (customers decide), one pays a flat higher wage.
10. **⭐ spectrum scenario:** Mi'kmaw communities hold treaty rights to fish for a **moderate livelihood** outside the commercial season — a right other fishers do not have. *(Is treating rights-holders differently "unfair" — or is it what a treaty promises? Links to Classes 10, 20, 58.)*

**Small-group debate (15 min):** split on the two scenarios with the widest disagreement. Sentence stems on the wall: "I see why you'd say ___ because ___, but have you considered ___?"

**Exit ticket (→ formative):** *Name one scenario where your position shifted during the walk, and what moved it.*

# Follow the Tax Dollar — Flow Map Template + Seminar (Class 32)

## Tax Dollar Flow Map

**Start:** "$5 of my income tax" (or pick another stream: HST on a $100 jacket, $5 of gas tax).

```
MY $5 ──► [LEVEL: federal / provincial / municipal]
              │
              ├──► Program 1: ______  ($__)
              │        └── who benefits: ______
              ├──► Program 2: ______  ($__)
              └──► debt servicing / transfers: ______
WHERE I THOUGHT IT WENT: ______
```

Sources for the research half (teacher bookmark set): federal budget highlights (canada.ca), NS budget documents (novascotia.ca/budget), HRM budget (halifax.ca). Simplified charts are fine — the point is the *flow*, not the exact figure.

## Socratic seminar prompts (after the maps)

1. Where did your dollar go that surprised you?
2. Which programs have you personally benefited from that you didn't realize were publicly funded?
3. What's the difference between "I don't want to pay taxes" and "I want my taxes to go somewhere different"?
4. (Bridge to U5) When parties argue about taxes in an election, what are they *actually* arguing about?
