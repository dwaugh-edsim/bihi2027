# Netukulimk & the Seven Districts of Mi'kma'ki — Class 9 Teaching Pack

**Purpose:** Unit 2's launch resource (Class 9, `cit9-outline.md`). Students meet the Mi'kmaw worldview **before** the treaty documents, so the treaties of Classes 10+ are read through a living legal relationship, not as dusty history. Every student's WHERE StoryMap place (Unit 1) sits inside Mi'kma'ki — this class names that land.

---

## 1. Netukulimk — the core concept

**Definition (use this wording — it is Mi'kmaw-authored):**

> *"Netukulimk is the use, protection and stewardship of the land and resources provided to all by the Creator for the sustenance and well-being of the Mi'kmaq."*
> — Assembly of Nova Scotia Mi'kmaw Chiefs (mikmaqrights.com)

**Teaching points (keep these precise):**
- Netukulimk is a **worldview and a system of reciprocal obligation**, not a synonym for "environmentalism." It guides *how* the Mi'kmaq take from the land — only what is needed, with respect — and *why*: sustenance and well-being, not maximum yield.
- It sits inside **msit no'kmaq** — "all my relations" — the understanding that people, animals, plants, water, and land are related and carry mutual obligations.
- Netukulimk is **living law**, not folklore: it underpins modern Mi'kmaw positions on harvesting, fisheries, and the "moderate livelihood" right confirmed in *R. v. Marshall* (1999) — the thread students will follow in Classes 10, 20, and 58.
- Frame respectfully: we are studying a **Mi'kmaw concept using Mi'kmaw-authored definitions** — we are not teaching Mi'kmaw spirituality, and we do not perform ceremony without invitation. When in doubt, quote the source.

**The comparison students will actually use (Class 9 discussion):**

| | Netukulimk frame | Extraction frame |
|---|---|---|
| The land is… | a relative that provides | a resource that pays |
| Take… | what you need | the maximum you can |
| Success is… | the relationship continues for generations | this year's yield |
| Built into… | Mi'kmaw law ways & treaty positions | market & state policy |

The point is **not** "one side is evil" — it is that *worldviews produce different policies*, and Canada's history (Units 2 and 5) is the story of what happened when the second frame ran without the first.

## 2. The Seven Districts of Mi'kma'ki

Mi'kma'ki — the traditional Mi'kmaw territory — is organized into **seven traditional districts**, connected by rivers and waterways. Nova Scotia sits entirely inside Mi'kma'ki; so do PEI, much of New Brunswick, part of Quebec's Gaspé, and part of Newfoundland.

| District | Approximate meaning | Approximate modern location |
|---|---|---|
| **Unama'kik** | "Land of Fog" | Cape Breton Island |
| **Sipekne'katik** | "Area of wild roots / ripe berries" | Central mainland NS (Shubenacadie river system) |
| **Kespukwitk** | "Land's End" | Southwestern NS |
| **Eskikewa'kik** | "Skin-dressed country" | Eastern shore NS |
| **Epekwitk** | "Lying in the water" | Prince Edward Island (Pictou area associated with Piktuk) |
| **Siknikt** | "Drainage area" | Chignecto isthmus (NB/NS border region) |
| **Kespek** | "Last land" | Gaspé Peninsula, Quebec |

**Classroom activity (10 min):** Students open their WHERE StoryMaps (Unit 1) and label each of their five places with its district (most of Dartmouth/Halifax sits in **Sipekne'katik**; Cape Breton places in **Unama'kik**). Exit question: *"Your five places now have Mi'kmaw names for their territory. What does it change to know the land was named before the street names?"*

**Precision note:** spellings vary across sources (Unama'kik/Unama'ki, Epekwitk/Abegweit); use the forms above consistently and defer to Mi'kmaw sources and guest speakers. The districts are the basis of the traditional **Santé Mawio'mi (Grand Council)** structure — each district with its Keptin (district chief) — which Class 22 revisits under governance.

## 3. Sensitivity & protocol notes (read before teaching)

1. **Sources matter:** use Mi'kmaw-authored definitions (mikmaqrights.com — Assembly of NS Mi'kmaw Chiefs; Mi'kmawey Debert Cultural Centre; MKI / Mi'kmawey Debert; heritage.nf.ca for district maps). Avoid generic "First Nations culture" websites.
2. **Do not simulate ceremony.** No smudging, prayer, or offering re-enactments without an invited Knowledge Keeper leading it (Class 21 is the invitation slot).
3. **Orange Shirt Day bridge:** this class lands Sept 28–29, the teaching day before Sept 30 (no school). Name it plainly: "Wednesday is Orange Shirt Day — tomorrow we begin why the schools matter to this story." Do not front-load residential school detail here; Class 16 carries it with full content-warning protocol.
4. **If a student pushes back** ("why do we start with this?"): validate, redirect, don't argue — the framework in `cit9-study-content.md` §Unit 2 sensitivity notes.
5. **Assessment caution:** test the *concept* (Netukulimk as stewardship worldview; the districts as territory framework), never ceremonial or spiritual detail.
