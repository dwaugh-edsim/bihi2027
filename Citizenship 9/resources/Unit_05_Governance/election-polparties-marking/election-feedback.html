About & Grading

About This Feedback

These are your outcome grades for the Bicentennial 2026 Election Simulation. The unit ran for three weeks in April and May 2026, and covered three outcomes from Unit 5: Governance.

Your grades are based on the evidence hierarchy below. Teacher observation is the primary artifact — who showed up, who drove the work, who was present and engaged day after day. The Town Hall questions, your reflection responses, and your party's platform provided additional evidence.

Vote counts were not used as a grading factor. Many students noted that junior-high social dynamics, not just platform quality, affected vote outcomes.

How to Read This

Each student has three outcomes, graded 1 to 4 with plus signs in between (so the scale is 1, 1+, 2+, 3, 3+, 4):

Outcome 5.1 Issue Valuation — how issues become valued in government and society. Did you choose meaningful issues? Can you explain why they matter?

Outcome 5.2 Government Structure — the structure, operation, and selection of government in Canada. Does your work reflect realistic understanding of how government works?

Outcome 5.3 Democratic Engagement — strategies to meaningfully engage in a democratic process. Did you take a meaningful role? Can you describe specific contributions?

Each outcome shows a short title that captures the headline of your work, a longer comment that explains the grade, and (where available) a direct quote from your own reflection or Town Hall question. The closing paragraph at the bottom of each feedback page is a personalized summary.

Marking Scheme

GradeMeaning
4Fully committed. Completed role, contributed meaningfully, thoughtful reflection.
3+Strong engagement with standout moments.
3Meaningful engagement and satisfactory understanding that met outcomes.

Evidence hierarchy: (1) Teacher observation, (2) Work submissions and response to ongoing feedback, (3) Town Hall question data, (4) Reflection responses, (5) Party platform quality, (6) Voting data (engagement only — not a grading factor).

One Last Thing

This feedback reflects what you did, what you wrote, and what you asked during the election unit. If something here doesn't match what you remember, or if there's context I missed, talk to me. I'm happy to walk through your grade with you one-on-one.

Three takeaways from the whole class:

1. Showing up matters more than winning. The election was decided by one vote, and several strong platforms lost while weaker ones won. Your grade is based on the work you did, not on the vote count.

2. Reflection is where most of the learning showed up. The students who took 10 minutes to think about what they did, why they did it, and what they'd do differently next time wrote the most insightful feedback. The reflection is the single biggest factor in 5.1 and 5.2.

3. Democracy is a skill, not a personality trait. Asking questions, defending your platform, compromising with opponents, building coalitions — these are learned behaviours. The students who practised them in this unit, regardless of party success, are ahead of the students who didn't.

Thanks for taking the simulation seriously. The work you produced was, in many cases, better than what first-year university students do in similar exercises. You should be proud of that.

— Mr. Waugh
