# Sipekne'katik 2020 — Moderate Livelihood Fishery: Media Packet (Class 58)

**Purpose:** The teacher-curated media packet for the Sipekne'katik 2020 case study (Class 58, `cit9-outline.md`). Students read sources representing **four perspectives**, then write a 1-page news article + 1-page perspective analysis (per `cit9-simulation-concepts.md` Option C).

**Teaching frame (non-negotiable):** this is a **jurisdictional and treaty-rights dispute** — Mi'kmaw self-determination vs. federal regulation vs. provincial pressure — NOT "Mi'kmaw fishers vs. non-Indigenous fishers." Students never "take a side"; they analyse whose authority each actor claims.

**Verify before use:** news URLs drift — confirm each link (or find the archived equivalent) the week before Class 58. Items marked 🔗 have stable canonical URLs; items marked 📰 name the exact article to locate via the outlet's search.

## The case in brief (verified timeline)

- **Sept 17, 2020** — the 21st anniversary of *R. v. Marshall* — Sipekne'katik First Nation launches a self-regulated "moderate livelihood" lobster fishery outside the DFO season, issuing its own licences and tags. 📰 CBC ("Sipekne'katik First Nation launched their moderate livelihood fishery on Thursday," Sept 19, 2020 coverage) · Cultural Survival statement (🔗 below)
- **Sept 17–20, 2020** — confrontations at wharves in southwest NS as commercial fishers (and supporters) dispute the fishery;FLIR-quality tensions at Saulnierville and Middle West Pubnico; a Sept 20 incident at St. Mary's Bay leads to charges against a Sipekne'katik fisher.
- **Oct 13, 2020** — a Sipekne'katik lobster storage facility at Middle West Pubnico is raided and its catch destroyed; vehicles damaged.
- **Oct–Nov 2020** — federal ministers engage; RCMP charged Mi'kmaw and non-Indigenous actors alike; national debate over treaty rights, policing, and the undefined "moderate livelihood."
- **Ongoing** — negotiated fisheries agreements between Mi'kmaw communities and DFO continue; Sipekne'katik's own court case about the 2020 seizures was dropped in 2025 (📰 APTN, Jun 9, 2025) as negotiations replaced litigation.

## The legal spine (why the truckhouse clause matters)

*R. v. Marshall* (No. 1), [1999] 3 S.C.R. 456 (Sept 17, 1999): the Supreme Court acquitted Donald Marshall Jr. of fishing offences, affirming a **communal treaty right** to hunt, fish, and gather and to trade for "necessaries" — a **moderate livelihood** — rooted in the **1760–61 Peace and Friendship Treaties' truckhouse clause** (the Crown's promise of trading posts where Mi'kmaq could sell goods). *R. v. Marshall* (No. 2) (Nov 17, 1999) clarified the right is **not unlimited**: it can be regulated for conservation, and it is not open-ended commercial fishing. The Court never defined "moderate livelihood" — which is precisely why the dispute recurs.

## How to run (Class 58, per the outline)

1. **Setup (10 min):** name the frame — jurisdictional dispute, four claimed authorities. Review the legal spine above (truckhouse → Marshall → "moderate livelihood" undefined).
2. **Perspective groups (25 min):** four groups each read their perspective's sources and complete the perspective card: *whose authority does this actor claim? what do they want? what do they fear? what language do they use?*
3. **Article writing (20 min):** each student writes a 1-page news article (byline + date) fairly representing all four perspectives — the CBC "Mi'kmaw journalist" piece is the model.
4. **Debrief (5 min):** whose voices were easiest to find in 2020 coverage — and whose were hardest? (Bridge: Class 64 Power Audit.)

**Sensitivity notes:** recent real event; some students may have family ties to the fishery on any side. Frame as jurisdictional analysis, never adjudication. Use precise names — **Sipekne'katik First Nation**, Mi'kmaw (adj.), Mi'kmaq (noun). Avoid showing violent footage unedited; describe actions in plain language.
