1. Deep Time, Ancient Migrations & Archaeology Module 1 : Geological Evidence & Indigenous Presence

Beringia Land Plain Last Ice Age
  • Ocean Levels Dropped: Glaciers locked up ocean water; global sea levels dropped ~100m, exposing a habitable tundra plain up to 1,600 km wide between Siberia and Alaska.
  • Migration Motive: Hunter-gatherers followed herds of megafauna (woolly mammoths, steppe bison, caribou) across Beringia into North America.
The "Clovis First" Flaw Old Textbook Model
  • The Theory: Held that humans first arrived ~13,000 to 13,500 years ago via an inland ice-free corridor between continental glaciers.
  • Why Incomplete: Newer discoveries prove humans were established thousands of miles south long before the northern ice-free corridor was open.
Eva of Naharon & Kelp Highway Coastal Routes
  • Eva of Naharon: 13,600+ year-old skeletal remains found in a flooded Yucatán cave (Mexico), shattering the Clovis timeline.
  • Kelp Highway: Seafarers migrated southward along Pacific kelp forests. Melting ice raised seas ~100m, drowning ancient coastal sites.
Two Ways of Knowing: Scientific archaeology (excavation, radiocarbon dating, physical hypotheses) operates alongside Indigenous Knowledge (oral traditions, genealogies, presence "since time immemorial" / Wskitqamu). Neither system invalidates the other.

2. Mi'kma'ki & The Peace and Friendship Treaties (1725 : 1779) Module 2 : Unceded Territory & Living Covenants

Mi'kma'ki & "Unceded Land" Foundational Status
  • Mi'kma'ki: Ancestral homeland of the Mi'kmaw nation (Nova Scotia, PEI, eastern New Brunswick, Gaspé, western Newfoundland).
  • Unceded Territory: Nova Scotia land was never sold, ceded, or surrendered by treaty, nor extinguished by conquest.
  • Peace & Friendship: Unlike Western Numbered Treaties, these treaties did NOT surrender land. They were government-to-government peace pacts and military alliances.
  • Royal Proclamation of 1763: King George III decreed Indigenous land could only be acquired through formal Crown treaties, banning private land seizures.
The 1752 & 1760 Treaties Core Guarantees
  • 1752 Treaty (Hopson & Cope): Guaranteed the Mi'kmaq the "free liberty of hunting and fishing as usual" without government interference.
  • 1760-1761 Truckhouse Clause: Crown promised government trading posts ("truckhouses") where Mi'kmaq could trade furs, fish, and goods for European commodities.
  • Meaning of "Necessaries": In 1760, meant essentials of life (food, clothing, shelter, tools, ammunition) required to sustain families.
  • "We Are All Treaty People": Non-Indigenous Canadians' legal right to settle and govern in Nova Scotia derives directly from promises made in these Crown treaties.

3. Donald Marshall Jr. & The 1971 Wrongful Conviction Module 3 : Systemic Injustice & Hickman Inquiry

Wentworth Park & The Frame-Up May 28, 1971
  • The Crime: In Sydney, NS, 17-year-old Donald Marshall Jr. and Sandy Seale met Roy Ebsary in a park. Ebsary fatally stabbed Seale; Marshall was slashed trying to intervene and ran for help.
  • Police Bias: Sydney police immediately targeted Marshall due to anti-Indigenous racism, ignoring Ebsary and fabricating a case.
  • Coerced Perjury: Police intimidated teenage witnesses with criminal charges until they signed false statements accusing Marshall.
  • 11 Years Wrongfully Imprisoned: An all-white jury convicted Marshall of murder; he served 11 years in Dorchester and Springhill maximum-security prisons before exoneration in 1982-83.
1989 Hickman Royal Commission Structural Justice Reforms
  • The Finding: Chief Justice Hickman concluded systemic racism infected every level of the NS justice system: police, prosecutors, defense, and judges.
  • True Killer: Roy Ebsary was finally convicted of manslaughter in the 1980s.
  • Public Prosecution Service (PPS): Created in 1990 as an independent agency, stripping police of the power to lay and control charges.
  • Indigenous Court Workers & Judicial Training: Programs established to assist Indigenous accused and educate judges on systemic bias, paving the way for national criminal justice reform.

4. Gladue Rights & Restorative Justice Module 4 : Sentencing Principles & Alternative Healing

Criminal Code Section 718.2(e) & Gladue (1999) The Law
  • The Legal Mandate: Judges must consider all reasonable alternatives to prison for all offenders, with particular attention to Indigenous circumstances.
  • Systemic Factors Examined: Residential school trauma, Sixties Scoop, child welfare displacement, poverty, language loss, and racism.
  • The Gladue Report: Detailed life biography presented at sentencing proposing community-based restorative healing programs.
Restorative Justice vs Retributive Justice Community Healing
  • Core Distinction: Focuses on repairing harm, healing relationships, and reintegration rather than warehousing offenders in prison.
  • Not a "Get-Out-of-Jail Card": Gladue does not exempt Indigenous offenders from accountability; it addresses root causes of crime through culturally responsive rehabilitation.

5. The 1993 Eel Catch R. v. Marshall
  • Pomquet Harbour (Aug 1993): Donald Marshall Jr. caught 463 lbs of adult eels, selling them for $787.10 to support himself and his partner Jane McMillan.
  • DFO Charges: Charged under federal Fisheries Act with fishing out of season, without a commercial licence, and selling without a licence.
  • Treaty Defense: Marshall argued he was exercising his constitutional treaty rights under the 1760 Treaty.
  • Section 35 (Constitution Act, 1982): Recognizes and affirms treaty rights as supreme constitutional law, outranking ordinary federal fisheries regulations.
6. Supreme Court Ruling Moderate Livelihood
  • Sept 17, 1999 Acquittal: Supreme Court overturned convictions, ruling the 1760 Truckhouse Clause protects commercial fishing rights today.
  • "Moderate Livelihood" Defined: Securing modern "necessaries" (food, clothing, housing, basic amenities), NOT corporate wealth accumulation.
  • The "Undefined Trap": Court never set exact dollar caps, trap quotas, or seasons, creating a 25-year regulatory vacuum.
  • "Marshall 2" Clarification: DFO can regulate treaty fisheries ONLY for genuine conservation after meaningful consultation.
7. Modern Disputes & Civic Duty Active Citizenship
  • 2020 St. Mary's Bay Dispute: Sipekne'katik launched self-regulated lobster fishery outside DFO season; wharves blockaded and traps destroyed.
  • Sovereign Trading Posts: Mi'kmaw enterprises on reserves invoke 1760 Truckhouse Clause, sparking jurisdictional disputes with RCMP.
  • Grade 9 Civic Duty: (1) Understand treaties as supreme law; (2) Respect Indigenous harvesting as legal rights, not poaching; (3) Challenge racist stereotypes; (4) Honor unceded Mi'kma'ki.
