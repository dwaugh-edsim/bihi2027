# Lesson Plan & Curriculum Intent: The Price of Principles (Activism, Neutrality & Consequences)

**Course:** Citizenship 9 (Nova Scotia)  
**Unit:** Unit 5 — Governance & Democratic Engagement (with Unit 4 Media Literacy integration)  
**Lesson Title:** The Price of Principles: Activism, Neutrality, and the Costs of Taking a Stand  
**Target Sequence:** Follows the *Map Sprint (Canada/NS)*, *Three Levels of Government*, and *Real Issues #1: Cost of Living / Housing* assignments. (Preceded by a quick diagnostic quiz on Map / 3 Levels / Cost of Living).

## 2. Pedagogical Intent & Boundaries

### What We Want Students to Take Away
1. **The Physics of Activism (Action + Friction):** Public action and standing up for personal morals is not free—it comes with leverage (ability to influence) and friction (costs, consequences, backlash).
2. **Neutrality as a Choice:** Choosing *not* to speak up (or remaining neutral) is also a strategic choice. It can stem from brand protection, commercial interest, or an ethical obligation to protect others (e.g., tour crew, employees, family).
3. **Evaluating Platform & Power:** Students learn to analyze *who* holds power in public disputes (artists/citizens holding microphones vs. stadium owners, sponsors, record labels, radio networks, or governments holding leases and funds).

### Boundary Guardrails (Teacher Guidelines)
* **NOT an Israel-Palestine Debate:** This lesson explicitly avoids deep-diving into Middle Eastern geopolitics. Modern examples (like Macklemore / Ed Sheeran) are strictly used as structural case studies on **venue contracts, platform management, and tour consequences**.
* **NO Moralizing or Preachiness:** The goal is not to tell 14-year-olds "you must be an activist" or "silence is wrong." The goal is analytical civic realism—understanding why people choose to speak, why people choose to stay quiet, and what each path costs.
* **Reserved Topics Kept Out:** Mi'kmaw Treaty rights / Marshall Decision and Nova Scotian Civil Rights (Viola Desmond) are intentionally excluded from this case set, as they are reserved for dedicated deep-dives later in Units 2 and 5.

## 3. Lesson Sequence & Architecture

```
[ Quick Diagnostic Quiz ]  -->  [ Concept Intro: Action vs Friction ]  -->  [ Case Study Dossiers ]  -->  [ Comparison Matrix & Exit Reflection ]
(Maps + 3 Levels + Cost of Living)  (The Price of the Podium)            (4 Stand vs 4 Neutrality)       (Personal Civic Calculus)
```

### Phase 1: Diagnostic Warm-Up Quiz (10 mins)
Assesses retained knowledge prior to starting the lesson:
1. **Map Foundations:** Locating NS, Atlantic provinces, key water bodies, and Canadian capital regions.
2. **Three Levels of Government:** Identifying federal (defence, immigration, CRTC), provincial (healthcare, education, highways), and municipal (snow clearing, transit, local parks) responsibilities.
3. **Cost of Living / Housing Recall:** Basic concepts from the housing dossier (inflation, rent control, fixed income vs. rising expenses).

### Phase 2: Teacher Facilitation & Framing (15 mins)
* Introduce the term **"Civic Friction"**: The pushback or price exacted when an individual's personal morals clash with powerful institutions (employers, sponsors, radio networks, stadium owners, venue fans).
* Introduce the **Two Paths**:
  * **Path A (The Stand):** Speaking out publicly and accepting the friction/cost.
  * **Path B (Strategic Neutrality / Calculation):** Protecting the platform, keeping a broad audience, or safeguarding the livelihoods of dependents/crew.

### Phase 3: Student Case Study Investigation (25 mins)
Students work individually or in pairs using the online digital dossier (`19_Cit9_Price_of_Principles_Dossier.html`). They select **one case from Category A** and **one case from Category B** to research and compare.

### Category A: Taking a Stand & Paying the Price (Public Advocacy & Friction)

1. **Don Cherry (*Hockey Night in Canada* — 2019)**
   * **The Principle:** Traditional patriotism, military honor, and Remembrance Day observance.
   * **The Cost:** Fired live from *Hockey Night in Canada* after 38 years; immediate end of broadcasting career; national polarization.

2. **k.d. lang ("Meat Stinks" Campaign — 1990)**
   * **The Principle:** Animal rights and vegetarianism.
   * **The Cost:** Radio bans across Alberta and the US Midwest, public sign-burnings, intense hostility from her home province beef community.

5. **Michael Jordan ("Republicans Buy Sneakers Too" — 1990)**
   * **The Calculation:** Commercial neutrality, protecting shoe endorsements, separating athletic performance from partisan politics.
   * **The Trade-off / Critique:** Built an unprecedented global empire, but faced decades of criticism for withholding public support from civil rights candidates.

6. **Dolly Parton (Radical Neutrality / Unified Audience)**
   * **The Calculation:** Unifying a divided public through music and direct philanthropy (Imagination Library, health research) rather than taking political sides.
   * **The Trade-off / Critique:** Beloved across all political demographics; occasionally criticized by activists demanding explicit statements on social issues.
   * **Curated Links:**

7. **Taylor Swift (Strategic Silence to Calculated Voice — 2018)**
   * **The Calculation:** Maintained complete political silence for a decade to protect her career/fans, before strategically stepping out in 2018.
   * **The Trade-off / Critique:** Faced immense pressure from both sides—critics calling early silence cowardice vs. conservative fans reacting to her later statements.
   * **Curated Links:**

### Category C: The Legacy Activist (Expending Unassailable Privilege & Immunity)

9. **Gord Downie (The Tragically Hip Kingston Concert & *Secret Path* — 2016)**
   * **The Calculation / Context:** Facing terminal brain cancer, Downie possessed 100% public goodwill, maximum cultural capital, and zero career risk remaining. He chose to spend his final, massive national platform (11 million viewers watching Kingston concert on CBC) to directly address Prime Minister Justin Trudeau from the stage about Indigenous reconciliation and northern communities.
   * **The Trade-off / Critique:** Didn't pay a career or financial cost (he was already a beloved icon), but demonstrated how maximum privilege/platform can be used for civic responsibility.
   * **Curated Video Links:**
     * [CBC / YouTube: Gord Downie addresses Prime Minister Justin Trudeau on stage in Kingston (August 20, 2016)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=13-6LhD_kHQ)

## 5. Assessment & Student Task Matrix

### Student Task Requirements (Completed in Digital Dossier)
1. **Case Analysis (Category A):** Identify the figure, the principle/value, the action taken, and the exact financial/career/social cost paid.
2. **Case Analysis (Category B):** Identify the figure, their calculation/reasoning for neutrality or silence, and what they gained vs. what they were criticized for.
3. **Comparative Evaluation:** Compare the two cases side-by-side using the 4 core criteria:
   * *Who held the real power (the speaker or the institution)?*
   * *Did the person's action (or silence) achieve their desired goal?*
   * *Who else was affected by their choice (fans, crew, employees, family)?*
   * *How does public opinion view their choice today versus when it happened?*
4. **Personal Civic Reflection & Real-World Dilemma:**
   > **Part A (Principles & Friction):** *"If you believed strongly in a cause at school or in your community, what is one factor that would inspire you to speak out, and what is one factor that would make you hesitate? What cost are you willing to accept for your principles?"*
   > 
   > **Part B (Institutional Role vs. Personal Loyalty):** *"Imagine you work for CBC News as a journalist. An election is coming up. A close childhood friend is running for a local office and asks to put her campaign sign on your front lawn. What do you need to consider before making your decision? (Think about personal loyalty vs. professional neutrality, public perception, institutional trust, and employer codes of ethics)."*
