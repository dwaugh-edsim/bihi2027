Before we start

Four Ground Rules

1
There are no right answers
Reasoning is the skill.
“I don’t know yet” is a real answer, if you can say why you’re still thinking.
2
No loopholes
“Why not just untie them?” “Why not shout?” Clever. And the answer: you get one choice, the scenario as written.
Notice the loophole. Then decide anyway.
3
Attack the argument, not the person
Only two sentences are allowed across this room:
“I see it differently because…”
“That’s a fair point, but…”
4
I will not tell you what I think
My job: hand you the labels for what you’re already arguing.
Your job: find out which label you’re using.

The Switch

Hands up. What do you do, and can you say why?
Scenario 1 · 4 minutes

Turn to your partner

  • Tell your partner what you just chose.
  • Then make them ask you the one hard question: “so what makes this the right call?”
Write the reason, not the answer. “Fewer people die” is a reason. “It’s my job to protect everyone” is a reason. “Pull” is not.
📝 On your sheet
  • Scenario 1: your choice, and your reason in one sentence.
  • Keep it. You will compare it to Scenario 2 in a few minutes.

Here is the point of the whole lesson

Look at what this room just did

  • Same lives. Same math. Same people.
  • Three scenarios. Three different answers from this room.
  • That is not randomness. Something consistent is doing work in your heads.
So what exactly is doing the counting?
  • You argued that how many matters.
  • Then you stopped counting the moment a hand went up.
The labels for what you were already arguing

Three Lenses

The outcome is what matters. Count the results.
The Switch: yes.
The Footbridge: no.
Identical outcomes. But one kills by hand.
Some acts are wrong no matter how good the outcome.
The Footbridge: no.
Not because of the numbers.
Killing an innocent is the thing you don’t do. Ever. Even when it works.
Ask who you are and who is in front of you. Not totals.
Who is this person to me?
What would I want someone to do?
Am I the kind of person who pulls levers?
Which lens is your lens? Be ready to defend it against the other two.
