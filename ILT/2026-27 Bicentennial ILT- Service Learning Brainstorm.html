Service Learning Proposal
A Year of Student-Led Contribution

A proposal for colleagues: Grade 9 ILT students run four service streams through the year — wet-weather gear rehoming, a warmth drive for local shelters, a pre-primary partnership, and a structured lunchtime sports program for elementary students — as the service-learning backbone of Citizenship 9 and a visible, student-run contribution to the school community.

Draft proposal — for staff review Last touched: Oct 2, 2026 Mr. Waugh · ILT

1The Four W's at a Glance

Who Grade 9 ILT students (902 / 903) as project leads, tied to Citizenship 9. Partners we're recruiting: pre-primary and elementary staff (including the PE lead), homeroom teachers, admin, caretaking, families, and a local shelter.
What Four service streams: A — Wet-Weather Outdoor Gear Drive (rehoming, family-first asks); B — Warmth Collection for Local Shelters; C — Pre-Primary Partnership (teachers speak to the 9s; students respond to the needs they hear); D — Structured Lunchtime Sports (the 9s run a scheduled, role-based program for elementary students).

Why Citizenship 9 service learning — authentic take-action work with real audiences — plus a durable school contribution: gear that keeps BiHi students outside, support for people in need in our community, and a bridge between our 9s and our youngest learners.

2Why — Citizenship 9 & School Contribution

This isn't a fundraiser bolted onto a class. It's the take-action component of Citizenship 9 running through ILT: students identify needs, plan responses, persuade real audiences, and answer for the results — the full citizenship arc, four times over, in four different registers of community.

Curriculum fit

  • Citizenship 9: service learning and community contribution; students research needs, take action, reflect.
  • ELA: the ask adapts to three audiences (note home, text, paste-blurb) — audience awareness and persuasive register, assessed authentically.
  • Math / data: recess tallies, drive counts, before/after participation — one real dataset the whole year points at.
  • NS competencies: citizenship, communication, critical thinking, collaboration.

School contribution

  • More BiHi students outside on wet days — fewer indoors, better recesses.
  • Warmth items to local shelters, collected by our students, not purchased.
  • A working relationship between Grade 9s and pre-primary — the building's oldest and youngest learners knowing each other by name.
  • A structured lunchtime sports program for elementary students — organized, active, and led by the big kids they look up to.
  • Lost-and-found redemption: unclaimed serviceable gear re-enters circulation.
The core reframe The asks are rehoming, not fundraising: "check your closet — those rain pants worn twice before the growth spurt are exactly what we need." Free for donors, sustainable, and it teaches students that most community needs are solved by redistribution, not purchase.

4What — Three Service Streams

Stream A · Wet-Weather Outdoor Gear Drive Fall — launches first

Too many students default to indoors on wet days because their gear gave up in October. Students write one shared why-paragraph by consensus, cap a manifest of 8 named items with condition standards, and adapt the ask to three channels. Donations are inspected, logged, cleaned, and shelved by the intake crew. (Full brainstorm mechanics in the appendix.)

Sample why (kid voice, for revision): "On rainy days, a lot of us stay inside because our gear can't keep up. We think that's backwards — puddles, forts, and fresh air are the best part of junior high outside. We're collecting good-condition rain gear so nobody has to sit out because their jacket gave up in October."

Stream B · Warmth Collection for Local Shelters Heading into winter

The same ask-machine, run a second time for people beyond the school: warm hats, mitts, socks, blankets, toiletries. The authentic move is asking the shelter for its current wishlist first — students learn that shelters often need new socks and toiletries but accept gently used outerwear, and that needs shift week to week. The collection window lands where the need is coldest.

Stream C · Pre-Primary Partnership New year onward

Pre-primary teachers are invited to speak to the 9s about their program — how four-year-olds learn outside daily ("there's no bad weather, only bad clothing"), and what the program needs. The 9s prepare interview questions in advance, then design a response project from what they hear. Likely shapes, chosen by the students:

  • Little-kid gear drive: mittens (they vanish daily), splash pants, boots, spare-clothes bins — sized for pre-primary ages, powered by the same channels.
  • Play-prop build: loose parts, fort kits, mud-kitchen tools made or rehomed by the 9s.
  • Spring play day: 9s run wet-weather outdoor stations for pre-primary — the year's evidence, in motion, with the building's youngest learners.
Why Stream C matters most Streams A and B teach students to run a drive. Stream C teaches them to listen before they act — the needs come from a real partner, not a brainstorm. That's the difference between charity and citizenship, and it's the piece most service projects skip.

Stream D · Structured Lunchtime Sports Program Pilot spring · then year-round

Grade 9s design and run a structured sports program for our elementary students at lunch — not supervised free play, but a program: a published schedule, a sport rotation, and fixed student jobs the 9s rotate through.

  • Roles for the 9s: commissioners (schedule and promotion), referees and game leaders, scorekeepers, equipment managers, and a welcome crew for hesitant joiners — every student gets a job that fits.
  • What makes it structured: a sport-of-the-week rotation (soccer, dodgeball variants, basketball skills, cooperative mixed-age games), fair-play rules the 9s explain and enforce, and a rhythm elementary kids can count on — same days, same space, same energy.
  • Listen-first design: before anything launches, the 9s interview elementary teachers about which grades want in and where unstructured lunch play breaks down — the same discipline as Stream C, and a natural extension of it.
The non-negotiable Grade 9s lead every session, but an adult is always present. The 9s run the games, the boundaries, and the encouragement; supervision and safety decisions stay with staff. Built in from day one, this is what makes the program admin-approvable and sustainable past June.

6Guardrails

  • No cash asks — goods only, in both streams, keeps the drive clean.
  • Condition standards stated up front; donations that fail inspection are respectfully declined — students script the decline themselves.
  • Admin nod before public-facing or business-facing asks; family-private channels run without.
  • Dignity rule: no student is ever identified as a gear recipient — drives serve "the schoolyard" and "the community," full stop.
  • Thank-yous are mandatory and specific — they close the loop and are their own assessed writing task.
  • Stream D supervision: the 9s lead every session but never supervise alone — an adult is present at all times.
