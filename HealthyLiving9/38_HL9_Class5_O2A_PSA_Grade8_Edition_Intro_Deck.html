Slide Anatomy · What Goes on the Card

Building a clear fact slide

THE MYTH“Can't you just hold it in?” Small, struck through, at the very top. The myth never gets to be the headline.
THE TRUTHThe main headline. Bold, direct, and declarative. State what is physically true before explaining the details.
THE CONDITIONFor DEPENDS cards, name the condition in one line: which bumps, which type, or how long it's been there.
THE CLINIC811 · Youth Health Centre · Sexual Health Clinic. Confidential, free, and no parental permission required in Nova Scotia.
WHY IT MATTERSWhat actually changes for a Grade 8 who reads this? If the honest answer is nothing, put the card back and pick a better one.

The Grade 8 Test

  • An 8th grader must be able to read it alone and completely understand it.
  • Keep the clinical anatomical terms — just place the plain everyday words right beside them.
  • Lead with the truth. No shock value, no scare tactics, and no moralizing.
  • Need-to-know, not trivia. Give them information that kills unnecessary panic and prevents harm.

Worked Model · From Wall Card to Finished PSA

How the wall card turns into a PSA

Original Wall Card · Walk 1 (Myth Trials) · Card 03
“You can pick up an STI from a toilet seat.”
Claim origin: Overheard in the junior high washroom
“Watch out for public toilet seats.”
Toilet seats do not transmit STIs.
Every sexually transmitted infection requires direct sexual contact — skin-to-skin or fluid-to-fluid. STIs cannot survive or reproduce on cold, non-porous surfaces. Wipe the seat down if you want, sit down if you don't: the seat was never the risk.
WHERE TO GO: 811 · School Youth Health Centre · Local Clinic
PRIMARY SOURCE: KidsHealth.org (Nemours Pediatric Health System)
WHY IT MATTERS: Kills a persistent junior high fear without false reassurance.
Notice the rule in action: The headline is the medical truth. The myth is small and struck through at the top so it never gets anchored in memory.
