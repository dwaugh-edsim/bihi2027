O3: Learners will investigate ways to access health resources that support health and well-being. Focus: navigating the system means knowing that “the ER” is not one wait - it is many, and the drive can be worth it.

How to use this (the actual skill) 1. These are predictions, not promises. Check the live numbers first: yourhealthns.ca → ED wait times (also edwaittimes-app.nshealth.ca, erstat.ca/hospitals/ns).
2. Check the hours column. “Limited hours” means the ER may be closed when you arrive - verify before you drive.
3. Emergencies beat convenience: chest pain, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, head injury, overdose → nearest ER or 911, every time.
4. Stable but urgent? Call 811 first. Sometimes phone advice or a walk-in avoids the ER entirely. If you do need an ER, a shorter predicted wait an hour away can be the better choice.
5. Never drive yourself if you are impaired, in severe pain, or on new medication. Get a ride. Call 811 for options. 211 can help with transport.

Class discussion - fictional scenario

It’s 8 p.m. Ari (14) sprained an ankle at practice. It is swollen and painful, but not deformed (see Scenario 3). Walk-ins are closed. Live waits show Dartmouth General at 4+ hours. Valley Regional in Kentville (24/7, about a 40-minute drive) shows under 1 hour. What are Ari’s best options, in order? Write 2-3 steps and your reason.
