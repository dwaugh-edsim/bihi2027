📐 Testable Mathematical Framework
The Sleep Debt & Athletic Penalty Formulas
These exact mathematical rules appear on Quiz 1 and Midterms.

Formula 1: Sleep Debt

Debt = 9.0 hrs - Actual Sleep

Teens biologically require 8.5 to 9.5 hours for neurodevelopment. Sleeping 5.5 hours = 3.5 hours daily deficit.

Formula 2: The Sleep Debt Rule

20%–25% Slower

3 consecutive nights of <6 hours sleep slows neurological reaction time by 20%–25% (equivalent to 0.05% BAC).

Clinical note: Attention lapses (microsleeps where you miss a stimulus >500 ms) surge by ~300% after chronic sleep restriction.

Formula 3: Social Jetlag

Shift > 2.0 hrs = Jetlag

Sleeping until 1:00 PM on Sunday shifts your circadian clock, ensuring insomnia Sunday night and severe fog Monday.

🧠 Core Biology & Test Bank
The 5 Core Biological Mechanisms
Study Anchor: Definitions guaranteed to be assessed on your Term 1 Quizzes.

1. Circadian Phase Delay

Biological shift in teens where the pineal gland delays melatonin secretion by ~2 hours (shifting sleepiness to 10:30–11:00 PM).

2. Melatonin Suppression (SCN)

Blue light (~460nm) strikes retinal melanopsin receptors, signaling the Suprachiasmatic Nucleus to block melatonin release.

3. Sleep Inertia

The 30–90 minute physiological state of grogginess and slow cognition after waking up out of deep Slow-Wave sleep.

4. Stage 3 Slow-Wave Sleep & HGH

The deep NREM stage where Human Growth Hormone (HGH) is released to repair muscle tissue and rebuild physical energy.

5. REM Sleep & Memory Consolidation

The dream stage where motor skills, athletic tactics, and academic study are cemented into long-term memory.

🔒 Takeaway: Cutting 9 hrs → 5.5 hrs cuts off the final 2 REM cycles where learning is locked in!
