Seven Things To Know IF YOU ONLY REMEMBER SEVEN
  1. Silence is the trick. Chlamydia shows nothing in up to 7 in 10 women. Feeling fine was never a test — and testing is a pee cup and a blood draw, quick, free, confidential.
  2. A sore that vanishes is not an infection that left. Syphilis imitates and marches for years. Cold sores shed even when healed. Blood on a shared razor survives about a week.
  3. HIV's fluid list is short. Blood, semen, vaginal and rectal fluids, breast milk: yes. Saliva, sweat, tears: no.
  4. Protection has fine print. A wallet condom is a dead condom: body heat and friction kill latex. Fresh, in date, every time.
  5. First times are not on a schedule. Half crosses near 18–19, not 16. It should not hurt: time, arousal, lubrication. Bodies return to normal — anatomy is not graded by partner count.
  6. Pain has a rule. "Blue balls" settles on its own and damages nothing. Exercise and heat genuinely ease period pain. Pain that keeps happening gets described to a doctor.
  7. Where to go, no family needed. The mature-minor rule keeps it confidential: Sexual Health Centre (the pill low-cost or free — no questions asked), school Youth Health Centre, any pharmacy (free consult with MSI), 811. And the law runs on birthdays: consent is 16; at 14–15 a partner less than five years older is inside the close-in-age rule.
When Is It Actually "Depends"?
01A real condition
"Depends" counts only when you can name it: which fluid, how many days between birthdays, which infection, is it actually clean.
02Looks are not tests
A clean-looking razor, a healthy-looking partner, a fine-feeling body: none of them are checks. The test is the test.
03Precision beats vibes
The close-in-age rule runs on exact birthdays, not grade labels. Four years and eleven months is not five years.
04Not a hedge
"Depends" is not a safe middle answer. No nameable condition means it's the wrong tick.
