Scenario 1 · 2:15 PM in Dartmouth
You have a sore throat and no family doctor. You show up at a Dartmouth walk-in clinic after school.
What actually happens at the front desk?

FULL FOR THE DAY.
Dartmouth walk-in clinics are overrun and often fill up before or right after opening. Need a doctor long-term? Register at needafamilypractice.nshealth.ca or call 811.
Scenario 2 · 9:15 PM
38.6°C fever for two days. Drinking fluids, talking fine, no rash. ER tonight, or wait?
Which door do you pick?
1) Go to ER tonight 2) Call 811 3) Wait til morning
Click to reveal →
CALL 811 FIRST.
Free Registered Nurse, 24/7. Going to Dartmouth General ER for a mild fever can mean sitting in the waiting room until 3 a.m. Save 911 / ER for real warning signs: chest pain, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, or stiff neck + non-fading rash.
Scenario 3 · Common Infections
You have a red, crusted eye infection (pink eye) or a painful urinary infection. Walk-ins are full and you have no family doctor.
What is your best move?
1) Walk-in tomorrow 2) Ask a pharmacist 3) Go to ER
Click to reveal →
ASK THE PHARMACIST.
Most people think infections always need a doctor, but NS pharmacists can assess and prescribe for pink eye, UTIs, tick bites, cold sores, and strep throat (at Pharmacy Primary Care Clinics), plus renew regular meds. Assessment = $0 (MSI). Medication = you pay.
Scenario 4 · 8:00 PM Sprain
You twisted your ankle at 8:00 PM and might need an X-ray. Dartmouth General shows a 4.5-hour wait, while Kentville (40 minutes away) shows 30 minutes.
Before you decide where to go, ask three questions:
1) Is it life or death? 2) Did 811 say go to the ER? 3) Do you have a car and a safe driver?
Click to reveal →
CHECK LIVE WAITS & HOURS FIRST.
If it is life or death, go to the nearest ER or call 911. If it is stable, 811 says you need an ER, and you have a car, checking live waits and 24/7 hours on yourhealthns.ca can save hours. Forgot your health card? Urgent care will still see you.

“Wait... Isn’t Health Care Free?”
What Nova Scotia MSI pays (and doesn’t)

MSI Pays ($0 at the door)

  • Medically necessary doctor, ER & hospital visits
  • Pharmacist minor-ailment assessments
  • Basic dental until the end of the month you turn 15
  • Public mental health clinics (NS Health / IWK, though waitlists can be long)

MSI Does NOT Pay

  • Prescriptions picked up at a pharmacy
  • Dental after your 15th birthday month & braces
  • Glasses, routine eye exams, and physiotherapy
  • Private therapists & psychologists (faster, ~$150-$220/session) & some forms
Private Insurance in Canada
Next year, your free MSI dental coverage ends.
Even in Canada, a single prescription, pair of glasses, cavity filling at 16, or physio visit adds up fast.
THAT’S WHY JOBS ADVERTISE “BENEFITS.”
Most Canadian families get private health & dental insurance (Blue Cross, Sun Life, Manulife) through a workplace to cover the gaps MSI leaves out.
Scenario 5 · March Break in Florida
You break your leg on a family trip to Florida. You have your Nova Scotia health card, but nobody bought travel insurance.
What happens at the hospital?
1) Your NS health card pays 2) They turn you away 3) They treat you, then send a huge bill
Click to reveal →
THEY TREAT YOU, THEN BILL YOU THOUSANDS.
A US emergency room will stabilize you, but your Nova Scotia health card covers almost nothing outside Canada. An ambulance ($1,000+), X-rays, and surgery can easily hit $5,000 to $20,000+ USD. Always buy travel medical insurance before you go, call their 24/7 line first, and keep every receipt.
