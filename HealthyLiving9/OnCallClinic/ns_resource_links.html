24/7 PHONE & TEXT LINES

Health811GOV
Call 811 · TTY 7-1-1
Free, 24/7. Registered nurses answer general health questions. First call when there is no family doctor. See also About 811.
988 Suicide Crisis HelplineGOV
Call or text 988
Canada-wide, 24/7, free and confidential. For anyone in crisis or thinking about suicide - or worried about a friend.
Kids Help PhoneNGO
Call 1-800-668-6868 · text CONNECT to 686868
Free, confidential, 24/7 counselling for young people. Also has a Resources Around Me finder.
211 Nova ScotiaNGO
Dial or text 2-1-1 · outside NS 1-855-466-4994
Free, confidential navigation to community, non-profit, and government programs - rides, food, housing, supports. VRS available via srvcanadavrs.ca.

NAVIGATING THE HEALTHCARE SYSTEM (NS)

Your Health NS (Nova Scotia Health)GOV
yourhealthns.ca - search “Walk-in Clinics”
Find walk-in clinics near you for non-urgent care; service and location information.
Mental Health & Addictions (provincial intake)GOV
1-855-922-1122 · Mon-Fri 8:30-4:30, Tue/Thu to 8 p.m.
Provincial entry point for mental health and addictions services, including child & adolescent services.
Pharmacist common conditions (PANS)PROF
pans.ns.ca · also pans.ns.ca/minor-ailment-assessment
NS pharmacists can assess and prescribe for a set list of common conditions (e.g., UTI, yeast infection, shingles, Lyme prevention). No appointment. Assessment free; medication costs apply.

WAIT TIMES & REGISTRIES (check before you go)

ER / ED wait timesGOV
yourhealthns.ca → ED wait times · also edwaittimes-app.nshealth.ca · erstat.ca/hospitals/ns
Estimated current wait times for select NS emergency departments. Not sure it's an emergency? Check the wait, then call 811.
Need a Family Practice RegistryGOV
needafamilypractice.nshealth.ca · or call 811 to register
The waitlist for a family doctor (GP). You need your Nova Scotia Health Card. When a doctor accepts new patients, NS Health contacts people on the community list.
Specialist wait timesGOV
waittimes.novascotia.ca/physicians
Wait times for 150+ procedures; shows which locations/specialists have the shortest wait for consult and surgery. You need a referral first.
What MSI covers (and what it doesn't)GOV
novascotia.ca → healthcare services covered by your health card
The "isn't healthcare free?" answer: medically necessary doctor/hospital care, yes. Most dental, retail prescriptions, and some forms/tests, no. (Pharmacare = the provincial program that can lower drug costs.)

YOUTH-SPECIFIC & CONFIDENTIAL

Youth Health Centres in HRCE schoolsGOV
hrce.ca · our school: bic.hrce.ca
School-based Youth Health Centres (e.g., Cobequid) offer confidential health care for students. Ask at the school office or check the page.
IWK Youth Mental HealthGOV
iwk.nshealth.ca/mental-health/youth
Specialized mental health care for children and youth to age 19. Provincial, confidential.
Sex & U (Action Canada for Sexual Health and Rights)NGO
sexandu.ca (formerly sexualityandu.ca)
Canadian, medically accurate sexual health information for teens - consent, STBBIs, contraception. Confidential reading, no account.
Teen Health SourceNGO
teenhealthsource.com
Youth-focused sexual & reproductive health info (Planned Parenthood Toronto - national, not NS-specific).
Caring for Kids (Canadian Paediatric Society)PROF
caringforkids.cps.ca
Reliable, paediatrician-reviewed health handouts for parents and teens - a good “is this source trustworthy?” benchmark.

CROSSING THE BORDER (US TRAVEL)

Medical care outside Canada (Government of Canada)GOV
travel.gc.ca
Provincial plans cover little or none abroad, cap emergency coverage at NS rates, and never pay up front. Hospitals abroad can demand immediate payment. Read before any US trip.
Travel insurance (Government of Canada)GOV
travel.gc.ca
What travel medical insurance should include: coverage limits, evacuation/repatriation, pre-existing conditions, who pays first.
US plan types: HMO / PPO / EPO / POSGOV US
healthcare.gov
How American insurance works: in-network vs out-of-network, referrals, deductibles, copays, coinsurance, out-of-pocket maximums.
EMTALA (US ERs must stabilize anyone)GOV US
cms.gov
EMTALA = the US law saying ERs must screen and stabilize anyone, no matter their ability to pay - then bill them. It is not free care.

THE CANADIAN PUBLIC-vs-PRIVATE DEBATE

Canada Health Act (full text)LAW
laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-6
The 1984 law: five principles (public administration, comprehensiveness, universality, portability, accessibility) and the extra-billing/user-fee deterrence.
Chaoulli v. Quebec, [2005] 2 S.C.R. 791COURT
canlii.org (Supreme Court of Canada)
2005: Quebec's ban on private insurance for medically necessary care struck down (6-3) over long public waits.
Fraser Institute: Waiting Your Turn 2024RESEARCH
Median wait times for treatment after specialist referral - the data behind the “lineups” side of the debate (e.g., orthopaedic ~57 weeks in the 2024 report).
CHA Annual Report 2023-24 (Health Canada)GOV
canada.ca
How the Act is monitored in practice, including extra-billing penalties. (Cambie Surgeries v. BC, 2022 BCCA, upheld the restrictions; SCC denied leave 2023.)

RELIABILITY RED FLAGS (use these on your map's check column)

No author or no date · sells a product in the same post (“link in bio”, discount codes) · claims cures or “doctors hate this” · percentages with no study · no Health Canada product numbers (NPN/DIN) on natural health products · .com blog with no credentials - any of these means: verify with 811, a pharmacist, or a .ca government/professional source before acting on it.
