The Basics: Pathogens, Bacteria, and Viruses

Two Words to Know
Pathogen & Communicable

Pathogen is the science word for any germ that can make you sick. A communicable disease is catchable from another person or surface (like a cold; a broken arm is not).

Kind 1 · Living Single Cell
Bacteria (e.g. Strep, Salmonella)

Alive, eating, and growing. Most are harmless or helpful (gut, yogurt). A few make you sick. Antibiotics (like penicillin) kill bacteria.

Kind 2 · Instructions in a Shell
Viruses (e.g. Cold, Flu, Norovirus)

Not living cells; they hijack your body's cells to copy themselves. Antibiotics do NOTHING to viruses. Vaccines train your immune system before you meet a germ.

Quick Warm-Up · How Communicable Diseases Spread

Five Ways Germs Travel From Person to Person

The 5 Routes
  • 1. Airborne (Droplets): Tiny wet drops fly when someone coughs, sneezes, or talks (colds, flu, COVID-19).
  • 2. Direct Contact: Skin-to-skin touch, like handshakes or high-fives (pink eye, warts).
  • 3. Indirect Contact (Surfaces / Fomites): Germs wait on doorknobs, desks, keyboards, or shared objects (norovirus, Salmonella).
  • 4. Bodily Fluids: Sharing drinks, water bottles, or saliva (mono).
  • 5. Vectors: Living biting bugs like ticks or mosquitoes (Lyme disease in Nova Scotia).

Prevention Toolkit · Choosing the Right Defence

Soap vs. Sanitizer, and Vaccines vs. Antibiotics

20 Seconds at the Sink
Warm Water + Soap Beats Tough Shells
  • Soap + running water (20s): Lifts oil and dirt, physically flushing germs straight down the drain.
  • Alcohol hand sanitizer: Dissolves flu/COVID fatty coats, but works poorly on tough-shelled stomach viruses and greasy hands.
Before vs. After · Community Shield
Vaccines vs. Antibiotics (and Protecting Others)
  • Vaccines (Before): Train your immune system early so it fights a germ fast.
  • Antibiotics (After): Kill bacteria only; useless against viruses.
  • Community Shield: Staying home and washing up protects vulnerable peers (like a classmate in cancer care).
