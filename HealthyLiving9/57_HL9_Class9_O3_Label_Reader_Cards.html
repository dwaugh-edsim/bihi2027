Label Reader — Exhibit Cards

Healthy Living 9 · O3 Health Literacy · Class 9

O3: Learners will investigate ways to access health resources that support health and well-being. Focus: Investigate ways of increasing health literacy; identify places to access reliable information.

EXHIBITS 1–4 · CUT + POST
EXHIBIT 1
The Cough Syrup Label
COUGH & COLD SYRUP - Cherry Active ingredient: dextromethorphan 10 mg / 5 mL Dose (12+ yrs): 10 mL every 6 hours Max: 4 doses in 24 hours Warning: may cause drowsiness. Do not take with an MAOI antidepressant. Keep out of reach of children. Drug Identification Number (DIN): 02456789
Your task: A 14-year-old weighs 45 kg and has taken 3 doses today. How much more can they take in the next 12 hours? Name one warning that matters for school tomorrow.
EXHIBIT 2
The Granola Bar Wrapper
Front of pack: "MADE WITH REAL FRUIT!" Nutrition Facts: Serving size = 1 bar (40 g) Package contains: 2 bars Calories 190 per bar - Added sugars 11 g per bar % Daily Value: Sugar 22% per bar - Fibre 2%
Your task: Someone eats the whole package and says "one serving." How many grams of added sugar is that, and what % of a day's sugar is it? Does the front-of-pack claim match the numbers?
EXHIBIT 3
The Supplement Ad
"IMMUNE BLAST 3000!! Boosts your immune system by 300% in 48 hours! Doctors DON'T want you to know! 100% natural, no chemicals! Order now - 50% off, today only! Paid partnership with @fitlife_guru (2.1M followers)"
Your task: List three red flags in this ad. What is missing that a real Health Canada-reviewed product would show (look for a number on Exhibit 1)?
EXHIBIT 4
The Wellness App Privacy Policy
"...we collect your precise location, contacts, and sleep data. We may share aggregated data with advertising partners. You can opt out in Settings > Privacy (this may limit core features). Last updated: 2019."
Your task: Name two things this app collects that have nothing to do with sleep. What does "aggregated" mean, and why is the 2019 update date a red flag?
THE 4-QUESTION CHECK: Who made this? · What does it actually say? · What is missing? · What do I do with it? — record your answers on the response sheet.

EXHIBITS 5–8 · CUT + POST
EXHIBIT 5
The Blog Post
"5 CURE-ALLS for teen acne that dermatologists HATE! #3 will shock you..." - posted on wellnessblog123.com, no author name, no date, no sources cited. Comments are full of people saying it worked. The post sells a $60 cream.
Your task: Give three reasons this source fails the reliability check. Where could you verify acne advice instead (name one type of source)?
EXHIBIT 6
The Pharmacy Sig Codes
Prescription label: "Amoxicillin 500 mg Take 1 cap PO TID x 7 days Refills: 0"
Your task: Decode the sig: how many capsules per day, for how many days, total capsules needed? What should you do if the label is unclear instead of guessing?
EXHIBIT 7
The Government Page
novascotia.ca page: "Mental Health and Addictions Services" - shows programs, phone numbers, a "Last updated: October 2026" stamp, and links to NS Health sources. Written by the Department of Health and Wellness.
Your task: Name three features that make this a reliable source. Why does the "last updated" date matter for health information?
EXHIBIT 8
The Influencer Video
A 60-second video: a creator drinks a powder, then claims it "detoxes your liver overnight" and "replaces your morning coffee." Caption: "Link in bio - use code TEEN20 for 20% off." No credentials, no studies shown.
Your task: What is the financial motive here? Which claims would you check with 811 or a pharmacist before believing, and why?
EXIT TICKET (on your response sheet): In one sentence — what makes a health source reliable, and what is one red flag that means “do not trust this yet”?
