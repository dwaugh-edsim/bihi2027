1STBBI BIOLOGY

What carries, what does not, what testing can and cannot tell you.
MT-01“You can usually tell if someone has an STI by looking at them.”Many STBBIs cause no symptoms at all, and some cause none you can see. Testing is the only way to know.FALSE
MT-03“You can pick up an STI from a toilet seat.”Transmission requires sexual contact: genital skin-to-skin or body-fluid exchange. A toilet seat does neither.FALSE
MT-07“You can get an STI from oral sex.”Oral sex removes the pregnancy risk but reduces without eliminating STI risk: chlamydia, gonorrhea, herpes and hepatitis B are all possible. Condoms and dental dams reduce it further.TRUE
MT-12“If you're both in a committed, monogamous relationship, there's no reason to get tested.”Testing is about what happened before you got together, not how faithful you are now. People can carry an infection for years without knowing, and faithfulness only protects you if it is mutual.FALSE
MT-13“If someone was tested a month ago and was clear, you don't need to worry.”A month is better than a year, but it is a snapshot of the past: it says nothing about new exposures since then, and routine panels usually leave out herpes and HPV.DEPENDS
MT-14“There is a vaccine for some STIs.”HPV and hepatitis B have vaccines; HIV, chlamydia, gonorrhea and syphilis do not. Vaccines are prevention, not treatment: they do not cure an infection someone already has.TRUE
MT-18“Antibiotics can cure chlamydia and gonorrhea.”Both are bacterial and curable. Viral ones (HIV, herpes) can be managed and treated but not cured. Some are curable, some are manageable.TRUE
MT-19“HIV usually results in death by AIDS.”True before 1996, not now. HIV is a manageable long-term condition, and someone on effective treatment with an undetectable viral load cannot pass HIV on through sex.FALSE
FP-01“Most people with chlamydia never notice a single symptom.”Up to 7 in 10 women and about half of men show nothing at all. The body does not announce it.TRUE
FP-02“A sore that heals on its own means the infection healed too.”A painless sore that appears and heals by itself can be syphilis: the sore goes, the infection stays. It is called “the great imitator” for a reason.FALSE
FP-03“A cold sore can spread herpes somewhere other than a mouth, even when it's healed.”Herpes sheds between sores. Healed does not mean harmless.TRUE
FP-04“A razor that looks clean is safe to share with a housemate.”Looks are not a test at that scale: a used razor can carry traces of blood too small to see, and bloodborne viruses (hepatitis B and C, HIV) can move that way. Razors are single-person items.DEPENDS
FP-07“HIV can pass from one person to another through any body fluid.”Which fluid matters. HIV passes in blood, semen, vaginal and rectal fluids, and breast milk. Not in saliva, sweat, tears or pee.DEPENDS
FP-11“Peeing after sex lowers your chance of catching an STI.”Which infection. Peeing after sex lowers the risk of urinary tract infections, which are not STIs. It does not protect against STIs.DEPENDS

2CONTRACEPTION & PROTECTION

What each method actually does, and the difference between perfect use and real use.
MT-02“It's nearly impossible to get pregnant during your period.”Possible, just less likely: sperm can survive up to five days, ovulation can arrive earlier than expected, and short cycles plus long periods raise the odds.DEPENDS
MT-04“You can't get pregnant if they pull out before they finish.”Pre-ejaculate can contain live sperm, and withdrawal gives zero protection against STIs.FALSE
MT-05“Condoms are 98% effective at preventing pregnancy.”98% is perfect use; typical real-world use is about 87%. Fit, timing and expiry all matter. The exam question is: 98% of what, under what conditions?DEPENDS
MT-08“Wearing two condoms at once gives you extra protection.”It gives less protection: friction between layers makes them tear, slip or bunch. One condom, used correctly, beats two.FALSE
MT-09“The birth control pill protects you from STIs.”The pill works against pregnancy only, with no STI protection at all. Condom plus another method is dual protection: they cover different risks.FALSE
MT-10“Birth control makes you gain weight.”Which method? No strong link for the pill or patch; the Depo injection is the exception. It also depends on the person and what else is changing at the same time.DEPENDS
MT-16“If the morning-after pill doesn't work within a few hours, it's not going to work at all.”It does still work later, but works best within 24 hours and fades from there. A copper IUD placed within 7 days is the most effective option. Sooner is better does not mean point of no return.DEPENDS
FP-05“You remember protection. Your partner pulls a condom from his wallet. You're good.”A wallet is the worst place a condom lives: body heat, friction and time weaken latex. Check the expiry date, and keep spares somewhere cool, flat and unused.FALSE
FP-19“In Nova Scotia a 15-year-old can get birth control confidentially, without involving family.”The mature-minor rule: once a provider judges that you understand the treatment, you consent yourself, and the care stays confidential.TRUE

3CONSENT

Active, affirmative, ongoing, reversible, sober. The definitions Quiz 2 asks for.
MT-06“If your partner freezes and doesn't say no, that's still consent.”Freezing is not agreeing. Consent has to be active and affirmative, and it can be withdrawn at any moment, for any reason, with no explanation owed. Silence, going along with it, and being unable to respond are the absence of consent.FALSE
MT-17“Sexual assaults usually happen when a stranger attacks someone walking alone at night.”The stranger-danger myth. Statistics Canada found about three in four people who were sexually assaulted knew the person: a friend, acquaintance, partner or family member. This is about awareness, not blame.FALSE
MT-20“Someone who is drunk can't give consent.”Read it as written: the blanket can't is what makes it false, because it depends on how impaired the person is. Consent must be informed, sober, ongoing and freely given, and the more impaired someone is, the less able they are to give it. When a person is too drunk to know what they are agreeing to, there is no consent, and the sober person is the one responsible for checking.FALSE
FP-20“A grade 10 student, 15, has a 20-year-old girlfriend at DAL. If anything sexual happens, she could get into legal trouble.”The age of consent in Canada is 16, with a close-in-age exemption: for 14- and 15-year-olds, the partner must be less than five years older. Whether this pair falls inside that depends on how far apart their birthdays are. Law aside: a gap that wide at 15 also raises the power-imbalance question this unit talks about.DEPENDS

4ACCESS & THE LAW IN NOVA SCOTIA

What you can arrange yourself, and what you have to ask for by name.
MT-11“If you're under 16, you need your parents' permission to get tested for an STI in Nova Scotia.”In NS there is no parent permission and no minimum age to get tested for an STI, get birth control, or get a pregnancy test. A Youth Health Centre, a public health unit, a family doctor, or 811 can arrange it.FALSE
FP-08“You have to ask for an STI test by name; it does not come included with a routine check-up.”STI testing is opt-in. No standard physical sweeps for it automatically. Ask for it by name.TRUE
FP-18“The Halifax Sexual Health Centre mainly just hands out condoms.”They do pills, rings, patches, shots, IUDs, implants and emergency contraception, plus testing and information. Condoms are the smallest part.FALSE

Key terms Quiz 2 can ask for

Asymptomatic
carrying an infection with no symptoms; why “looks healthy” proves nothing
Perfect vs typical use
lab-conditions effectiveness (condoms 98%) vs real-world (about 87%)
Dual protection
condom + another method: pregnancy and STIs are different risks
Curable vs manageable
bacterial STIs can be cured; viral ones are treated, not cured
Undetectable viral load
HIV so well treated it cannot pass on through sex
Affirmative consent
an active yes; silence, freezing or going along is not consent
Mature minor
a young person who understands a treatment consents to it themselves
Close-in-age exemption
for 14- and 15-year-olds, a partner less than five years older
