Name the Condition — examples of a good DEPENDS answer
AName the variable
"Depends on which kind of birth control." · "Depends on which infection." The variable is the thing that changes the answer.
BName the test used
"Depends on what test you took — a routine panel doesn't cover herpes or HPV."
CName the moment
"Depends on when you take it — most effective within 24 hours."
DName the situation
"Depends on how carefully it was used — 98% perfect, about 87% typical."
EName the limit of the test
"Depends on how recently — a month-old result says nothing about what happened since."
FNot a condition
“It depends” · “Sometimes” · “It varies” — none of these count. Name the variable or the tick is wrong.
The test: could a doctor, a textbook, or a law check your condition? If yes, you named it. If not, you guessed.
Six Things To Know IF YOU ONLY REMEMBER SIX
  1. Most STBBIs have no symptoms. Feeling fine isn't a test result. Only a test tells you — and testing is worth doing even when you feel fine.
  2. They pass through more than sex, and more than once. Oral sex and genital skin contact are real routes, and you can catch the same infection twice. Number of partners isn't the issue.
  3. Hormonal birth control prevents pregnancy only. Condoms prevent STBBIs. Risk isn't tied to ejaculation — it's tied to penetration, which is why withdrawal isn't STI protection.
  4. Some are curable, some are manageable. Chlamydia and gonorrhea are curable with antibiotics. HIV and herpes are managed, not cured.
  5. Vaccines exist for some — HPV and hepatitis B. Prevention, not treatment.
  6. You can get care confidentially in Nova Scotia. STI testing and birth control don't require a parent's permission, at any age. 811 and Youth Health Centres can point you to local options.
