Six Things To Know IF YOU ONLY REMEMBER SIX
  1. Most STBBIs have no symptoms. Feeling fine isn't a test result. Only a test tells you — and testing is worth doing even when you feel perfectly fine.
  2. They pass through more than sex, and not just once. Oral sex and genital skin-to-skin contact are real routes, and you can catch the same infection twice. Number of partners isn't the issue.
  3. Hormonal birth control prevents pregnancy only. Condoms prevent STBBIs. Risk isn't tied to ejaculation — it's tied to penetration, which is why withdrawal isn't STI protection.
  4. Some are curable, some are manageable. Chlamydia and gonorrhea are curable with antibiotics. HIV and herpes are managed, not cured.
  5. Vaccines exist for some — HPV and hepatitis B. Prevention, not treatment.
  6. You can get care confidentially in Nova Scotia. STI testing and birth control don't require a parent's permission. 811 and Youth Health Centres can point you to local options.
When Is It Actually "Depends"?
01A real condition
"Depends" counts only when you can name it: which method, which infection, which kind of consent, what moment in time.
02Partial truth
True for some, false for others. The most dangerous kind — because it sounds certain.
03Real uncertainty
The evidence is genuinely still moving. Rare — and naming which is the skill.
04Not a hedge
"Depends" is not a safe middle answer. No nameable condition means it's the wrong tick.
