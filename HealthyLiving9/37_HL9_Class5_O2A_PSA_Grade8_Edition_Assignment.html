The Brief

You walked the walls. Twenty claims at a time, you voted, argued, and found out what actually holds up. Now it's your turn to build the wall. Choose 3 claims from the class verdict keys — at least one must be a DEPENDS — and turn each one into a public service announcement a grade 8 class would understand. Every fact gets verified: I did my best, but you check me anyway. The best work gets shown to a grade 8 audience.

Requirements — all seven, checked

  • Three claims from the walls we walked (the verdict keys). At least one DEPENDS — and its condition named, in one line, on the slide. You also justify the set: why these three are what a grade 8 most needs to know.
  • One fact per slide. Google Slides (3 fact slides + 1 source slide), a Canva poster (3 panels + credit strip), or another format you clear with me first — the shape stays the same. Plan on the organizer first; I initial it before you build.
  • The shape: the myth goes small and struck at the top — the truth is the headline. The question never makes the poster.
  • Verification: each fact slide names a source from the shelf + one line on what that source says about the claim — backs it up, sets it straight, or names the condition. The source slide adds one why-legit line per source.
  • Where to go: every fact slide carries the help line — 811 · Youth Health Centre · clinic (none need a parent's permission).
  • The Grade 8 Test passes: a grade 8 could read it alone and get it — keep the clinical term, add the plain words beside it. No shock, no scare, no personal stories. No anatomy pictures, no symptom photos — words, big type, simple icons.
  • Need-to-know, not trivia: each fact slide carries a one-line WHY IT MATTERS — what changes for a grade 8 who knows this. Audience, age-appropriacy, stakes. If the honest answer is "nothing much," choose a different fact.
Claims, not lives

The PSA is about the claim — never about you, your family, or anyone you know. Same contract as the wall: general facts, attributed sources. Anything personal goes in the anonymous question box instead, and I'll answer it properly.

Two ways to submit

Google Slides: 3 fact slides + 1 source slide — share through the class submission form.  Canva: one poster, 3 panels + credit strip — export as PDF, same drop.  Something else: clear it with me first; the shape stays the same.  Due __________ — before Quiz 2. Building this is reviewing for it.

The gates — automatic, non-negotiable

  • Canon trace: if a fact isn't in a class verdict key or on the shelf, it doesn't go on the slide — it goes to the question box.
  • No anatomy pictures, no symptom photos: Canva will offer you medical clip art — decline it. Words, big type, simple icons.
  • Organizer first: the planning organizer gets my initials before the final build starts.
  • Teacher review before display: anything shown to a grade 8 room gets my read first — and their teacher's sign-off.
  • No names, no stories: claims about bodies, never about people.
