Assignment Overview

Over the past several classes, we evaluated common claims across our three wall activities (Myth Trials, Nobody Told Me, and The Fine Print). Now you will put that knowledge to work for younger students. Working in pairs, choose three claims from our class verdict keys (at least one must be a DEPENDS verdict) and turn each into an accurate, informative public service announcement designed for a grade 8 audience. You will verify each claim with trusted health sources, explain why it matters, and provide real support resources. Selected PSAs will be shared directly with grade 8 health classes.

Project Requirements

  • Three verified claims: Select three claims from the class verdict keys. At least one must be a DEPENDS verdict with its specific condition clearly stated in one sentence. Your project must explain why these three facts are essential for grade 8 students to know.
  • Structured format: Dedicate one slide or panel to each fact. Use Google Slides (3 fact slides plus 1 source slide) or a Canva poster (3 panels plus a sources strip). Any other format must be approved in advance. You must complete your planning organizer and have it initialed by the teacher before you start building.
  • Truth as the headline: Make the verified fact your main, bold headline. If you mention the original myth or question, keep it smaller or crossed out at the top so readers remember the correct information first.
  • Source citations: Each fact slide must name an approved source from the Source Shelf and include one sentence explaining what the source confirms (verifying the fact, correcting a misconception, or specifying a condition). The final source slide or strip must explain why each source is reputable.
  • Where to get help: Every fact slide must identify at least one confidential health resource, such as 811, a school Youth Health Centre, or a community clinic. Mention that youth can contact these services directly and confidentially.
  • Grade 8 audience focus: Information should be clear enough that an eighth grader can read and understand it on their own. Use accurate medical terms paired with plain-language definitions. Keep the tone informative and supportive: no scare tactics, no personal stories, and no anatomy diagrams or symptom photos. Rely on bold text, clear layout, and simple icons.
  • Why it matters: Each fact slide must include a one-sentence statement explaining how this knowledge helps a grade 8 student make safe, informed choices. Focus on practical, relevant health knowledge rather than obscure trivia.
Privacy and Respect

This project is strictly about evaluating health facts and evidence. Do not include personal stories, names, or private situations involving yourself, your family, or your peers. All content must focus on objective facts from credible sources. If you have personal questions or need support, please use the anonymous question box or speak with the teacher directly.
