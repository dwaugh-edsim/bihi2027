How a source gets used

On each fact slide: name the source + one line on what the source says about your claim — backs it up (TRUE), sets it straight (FALSE), or names the condition (DEPENDS). On the source slide: one line per source on why it's legit (the why it counts lines below are your first draft).

811 Nova Scotia
NS Health's registered-nurse line and health advice library. 24/7, confidential, free.
Why it counts: run by nurses; it's the province's own health service.
OPEN 811.NSHEALTH.CA → https://811.nshealth.ca
KidsHealth.org (Teen section)
Nemours Children's Health network: body, health, and growing-up pages written directly for teens.
Why it counts: written and reviewed by pediatric doctors; built for your age group.
OPEN KIDSHEALTH TEENS → https://kidshealth.org/en/teens/
SexandU.ca
The Society of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists of Canada: contraception, consent, bodies, STBBIs.
Why it counts: Canada's OB/GYNs — the doctors who actually deliver this care.
OPEN SEXANDU.CA → https://www.sexandu.ca
Public Health Agency of Canada
The federal sexual-health hub: STBBI facts, prevention, where the national numbers come from.
Why it counts: the government body that tracks disease; where statistics come from.
OPEN CANADA.CA SEXUAL HEALTH → https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/sexual-health.html
IWK Health
The Maritime children's and women's hospital: care guides and clear explanations for our region.
Why it counts: the hospital that treats people our age, here.
OPEN IWK.NSHEALTH.CA → https://iwk.nshealth.ca
Nova Scotia Health: the junior high book
The province's own book for junior high students (PDF). If your claim lives in there, this is the most local source on the shelf.
Why it counts: published by Nova Scotia Health for your exact age group.
OPEN THE NS HEALTH PDF → https://www.nshealth.ca/sites/default/files/documents/11032_2023_11_21.pdf
