Part 1 · Key Vocabulary (Write the Exact Term) 6 MARKS (1 EACH)
Definition Clue Key Term (Write Clearly)
1. Any microscopic organism or agent that enters the body and causes disease (the scientific word for "germ").
2. A single-celled, living microscopic organism that eats, grows, and reproduces on its own.
3. A non-cellular package of genetic instructions inside a protein coat that must hijack a living host cell to make copies.
4. A non-living object (like a shared water bottle, phone, or doorknob) that carries germs from person to person.
5. A biting living creature (such as a blacklegged deer tick or mosquito) that carries and transmits a germ to a host.
6. The time gap between picking up a germ and feeling your very first symptoms of illness.

C) Your fingers carry the germs to portals of entry when you rub your eyes, nose, or mouth.

10. Why is washing with plain soap and running water for 20 seconds more reliable against stomach bugs than alcohol sanitizer?
A) Soap lifts grease and germs off skin so running water rinses them down the drain, tough shell or not.
B) Soap turns cold tap water into an antiseptic acid that melts human skin oils.
C) Soap permanently freezes bacteria inside your hands so they cannot multiply.
D) Alcohol hand sanitizer only kills viruses when mixed with boiling water.
11. A hiker in Nova Scotia gets bitten by a blacklegged deer tick and develops Lyme disease. Which travel route caused this infection?
A) Airborne respiratory droplets floating through the woods.
B) Indirect fomite contact from brushing against tree bark.
C) Contaminated municipal drinking water.
D) Vector-borne transmission from a biting living creature.
12. What is the primary purpose of getting a vaccine (immunization) before you are ever exposed to a dangerous virus?
A) It cures an active infection within two hours after symptoms begin.
B) It coats your throat in a permanent plastic shield so germs bounce off.
C) It trains your immune system to build antibodies in advance so it recognizes the real germ fast.
D) It destroys helpful gut bacteria so viruses have less space to hide.
13. What dangerous public-health problem happens when people overuse antibiotics or quit their prescription halfway through?
A) Tough surviving bacteria multiply and evolve into antibiotic-resistant "superbugs."
B) Respiratory viruses turn into biting insect vectors.
C) White blood cells permanently forget how to fight mild colds.
D) Drinking water becomes resistant to plain soap and friction.

A) Vaccinated classmates breathe protective medicine into the classroom air.
B) Community immunity blocks the virus from finding hosts to spread through, shielding that student.
C) Measles vaccines pass from student to student through shared desks and pencils.
D) Unvaccinated students naturally become immune just by sitting near vaccinated friends.
