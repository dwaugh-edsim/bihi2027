05 How It Works

Three tabs. Three jobs.

Every stop opens three tabs. Read all three before answering: the proof is usually in Tab 2.

1 • Story
What happened

First-hand accounts from the scene: staff reports, student interviews, and morning logs.

LOOK FOR Who was there, when symptoms started, and what they saw.
2 • Evidence
The hard proof

The data behind the outbreak: daily absence counts, symptom tallies, floor plans, and lab tests.

LOOK FOR Patterns, cluster locations, and real numbers to cite.
3 • Science & Words
The biology

Plain-language science notes and a daily Word Bank defining every new medical term.

LOOK FOR How the germ spreads, entry gates, and incubation times.

06 How It Works

The Log is where you work

This is your daily worksheet. Short, sharp answers typed day by day.

Task Type 01
Choose & Explain
Select your hypothesis from a list, then give the reason that convinced you.
Rule: saying "too early to tell" is allowed. Guessing without a reason is not.
Task Type 02
Short Evidence Answers
Write 1 to 2 sentences citing a specific clue from Tab 2: a number, date, room, or symptom.
Rule: no essays. Put the actual number in your sentence. Two sentences is plenty.
Task Type 03
Match & Sequence
Pair germs to body entry gates, and sequence the outbreak infection chain from first to last.
Rule: each step connects to the next as one unbroken cause-and-effect chain.
Everything saves automatically. Look for SYNC: SERVER OK in the top bar. You cannot lose your work.

08 The One Gate

Friday 3:15. Three rules. Then you commit.

Vice-Principal Raman needs a Monday Morning Game Plan, and the buses pull out in ten minutes. For each rule: what people must do, and why it stops this thing.

RULE 1
Who stays home, and for how long
Students who are sick, and students who are getting better. Both matter.
WHATStudents who threw up or had diarrhea must stay home until...
WHYThis stops the spread because recovering people still...
RULE 2
How surfaces get cleaned
Taps, fountains, desks, keyboards, door handles. What, and by whom.
WHATCustodians must disinfect surfaces using...
WHYThis works because plain soap and alcohol...
RULE 3
What students do before they eat
The habit that stops it, spelled out in steps a Grade 7 can follow.
WHATStudents must pause ___ and wash hands with ___ for...
WHYThis breaks the chain because...

09 How You Will Be Marked

What counts

The Log makes expectations clear on every stop. Four keys to full marks:

Expectation 01
Short & Focused
No essays. A clear multiple-choice pick or 1 to 2 sharp sentences is all that is required.
Expectation 02
Cite Real Evidence
Put actual numbers, dates, locations, or quotes from the Evidence tab into your answer.
Expectation 03
Always Explain Why
Most marks live in your reasoning: "Because [fact], which means [conclusion]."
Expectation 04
Update Your Hypothesis
Detectives change their minds as evidence builds. Your early guesses are never penalized.
Good science means updating your call. You are marked on how your thinking evolves as new data arrives.
