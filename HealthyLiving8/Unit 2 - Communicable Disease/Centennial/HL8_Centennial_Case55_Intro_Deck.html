01 The Assignment

You are the school Health Officer

Not a doctor. A detective with a clipboard. For one week, Centennial Junior High hands you the outbreak case and the authority to call it.

01
Read the board. Each day brings new evidence: attendance counts, symptom logs, witness interviews, lab tests.
02
Make the call. You record a judgement every day. Change it as often as you like until you commit. Nobody is watching over your shoulder.
03
Deliver the plan. Friday afternoon you hand Vice-Principal Raman three rules that stop the outbreak before it hits a hundred students.
One week of story. Nine stops. One plan. And one verdict you do not get to see until your plan is on the table.
Your desk. Usually ice packs and bandages. This week it is an outbreak.

05 How It Works

Three tabs. Three jobs.

In the middle of every stop, three tabs open. Read all three before you answer, because the answer is usually sitting in the second one.

1. Story

What happened that day, in the words of the people who were there. Dispatch notes and interviews from staff and students.

EXAMPLE: a witness interview, in their own words
2. Evidence

The numbers. Attendance counts, symptom logs, test results, floor plans, lab files. This is where proof lives.

EXAMPLE: a percentage, a date, a location
3. Science & Words

The science you need today, plus a Word Bank that explains every new term in plain words.

EXAMPLE: what a word means, in one sentence
Two buttons worth knowing: every stop has a Read this tab button that reads the page out loud to you, and a Text A+ / A- button in the top bar if you want bigger type. Use them as often as you like. Nobody is keeping score of who used them.

07 How It Works

You cannot lose your work

No save button to hunt for. No USB stick. No "sorry, it crashed and wiped me out."

Start
One click to start
Sign in with your school Google account. No PIN. Nothing to type. No name to spell.
Save
Saved as you go
Every answer saves to the server a couple of seconds after you type it. Watch the top bar: it changes to SERVER OK.
Resume
Pick up anywhere
Close the tab mid-sentence. Come back on a different Chromebook next week, sign in again, and you land exactly where you stopped.
Why it works that way: a Chromebook wipes its own memory when you close it. So this assignment saves to the server, not to the device. That is also why it still works on a different computer, a different day, or after a long weekend.
08 The One Gate

Friday 3:15. Three rules. Then you commit.

Vice-Principal Raman needs a Monday Morning Game Plan, and the buses pull out in ten minutes. For each rule: what people must do, and why it stops this thing.

RULE 1
Who stays home, and for how long
Students who are sick, and students who are getting better. Both matter.
RULE 2
How surfaces get cleaned
Taps, fountains, desks, keyboards, door handles. What, and by whom.
RULE 3
What students do before they eat
The habit that stops it, spelled out in steps a Grade 7 can follow.
COMMIT MY MONDAY PLAN
Committing locks your Monday to Friday answers so you can compare them honestly with the official findings, and it is what opens Case Closed. There is no way to see the verdict first.
09 How You Will Be Marked

What counts

The Log tells you what a stop needs before you move on, so you are never guessing what the task is.

Workload
Every stop has a task
One judgement or a few short answers. No essays, no long writing. Short and sharp is the target.
Reasoning
Evidence, not guesses
Every answer should point at something specific in the Evidence tab: a number, a date, a quote.
Quality
Say why
Most marks live in the "why" line. A right answer with no reason is only half an answer.
Feedback
Your file at the end
After you commit, Tab 3 (Your File) marks your log against the official Health Unit findings, so you see what you would have missed.
Your Monday guess is never marked wrong. Detectives are supposed to change their minds when the evidence changes. What gets marked is whether you changed your mind, and why.
