The words to know

WordWhat it means (plain words)Example
Germs
science word: PATHOGENS
say: PATH-oh-jenz		Things too small to see that can get into your body and make you sick.Cold germs, strep throat germs
Communicable
say: kuh-MYOO-nih-kul		Catchable. A sickness you can pass to another person.A cold is communicable. A broken arm is not.
Bacteria
say: back-TEER-ee-uh		One tiny cell. Alive. It eats, grows, and multiplies. Most are harmless or helpful; a few kinds make you sick.Strep throat, food poisoning (Salmonella, E. coli)
Viruses
say: VY-russ-ez		Not really alive. Instructions inside a shell. They break into your cells and turn them into virus-copying machines.Colds, flu, COVID-19, chickenpox, measles
DropletsTiny wet drops that fly out when someone coughs, sneezes, or talks. You breathe them in.How colds and flu travel through the air
VectorA biting bug that carries a germ from one person or animal to another. Like a taxi for germs.Ticks (Lyme disease), mosquitoes (West Nile)
Immune system
say: ih-MYOON		Your body's germ-fighting system. Full story in Class 3. Viruses can only be beaten by your immune system.Why you get better from a cold with no medicine

The four travel routes

RouteHow it happensGerms that use it
1. Through the airDroplets fly out when someone coughs, sneezes, or talks. You breathe them in.Colds, flu, COVID-19, measles
2. By touchGerm sits on hands or shared stuff (doorknob, desk, towel). You touch it, then touch your face.Pink eye, warts, norovirus, impetigo
3. In food and waterUndercooked meat, unwashed hands making food, unsafe water. The germ rides down your throat.Salmonella, E. coli, norovirus
4. By biteA biting bug carries the germ, then bites you (a vector).Lyme disease from ticks (real in Nova Scotia), West Nile from mosquitoes
Three lines that answer most quiz questions

1. Stomach-and-skin trouble is usually bacteria. Coughs, sneezes, and spots are usually viruses.  2. Antibiotics kill bacteria, never viruses.  3. Germs travel four ways (air, touch, food and water, bites) and always need a way in: mouth, nose, eyes, or a cut. Your hands do the travelling AND the letting-in.

The outbreak teaser (next class)

A school of 400 kids. By Friday, 55 kids are home sick. You will get the absence numbers and figure out: which route did it use, and where could the chain have been broken? If you know the four routes cold, the puzzle solves itself.

Answers: 1. pathogens   2. communicable   3. not really alive   4. bacteria   5. Salmonella, E. coli (strep throat counts)   6. any two of: colds, flu, COVID-19, chickenpox, measles   7. NO   8. air, touch, food and water, bite   9. vector   10. in: mouth, nose, eyes, or a cut (any two).
