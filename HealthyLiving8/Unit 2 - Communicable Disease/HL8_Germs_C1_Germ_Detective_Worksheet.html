Part A · Sorter: Bacteria or Virus? 7 POINTS (1 PT EACH)
[B] Bacteria One-celled living organism
Complete living cells · can multiply on their own in food or moisture · often cause stomach illness, food poisoning, or throat/skin infections · killed by antibiotics.
[V] Virus Genetic code in a shell
Not cellular · must hijack living host cells to replicate · causes coughs, sneezes, fever, or spotty rashes/blisters · antibiotics do NOT work (immune system/vaccines).
Sickness · Case Notes B or V One clue that gave it away (a few words)
1. Strep throat — Severe throat infection; doctor prescribes 10 days of penicillin antibiotics.
2. Chickenpox — Causes itchy red blister spots across the body; doctor says rest, antibiotics will not work.
3. The flu (Influenza) — Sudden high fever, body chills, and cough; penicillin cannot kill it.
4. Food poisoning (Salmonella) — Germs multiplied on warm, raw chicken; causes sudden stomach cramps.
5. COVID-19 — Airborne respiratory germ that invades lung cells to make copies of itself.
6. Germs that turn milk into yogurt — Helpful, living single cells that thrive and digest dairy.
7. Measles — High fever, cough, and a whole-body spotty rash; prevented by childhood vaccine, not antibiotics.
Part B · Name the Route · The Five Travel Ways 5 POINTS (1 PT EACH)
1. Airborne
Microscopic droplets or dust floating in shared air that get inhaled.
2. Direct Contact
Direct physical, skin-to-skin touch between an infected person and another.
3. Indirect Contact
Pathogens lingering on intermediate objects, shared surfaces, or food.
4. Bodily Fluids
Direct exchange of internal fluids (saliva, blood, mucous membranes).
5. Vectors
Pathogens transmitted through bites from intermediate living creatures.
Story / Scenario Route Why? / Defend your pick (if two routes fit!)
1. A kid coughs without covering up. Three classmates breathe the tiny floating drops.
2. Nobody cleaned the classroom doorknob all week. Germs are sitting on it waiting.
3. After a weekend hike through tall grass near Halifax, a kid finds a tick on their leg.
4. A friend home sick with mono took a sip from your water bottle. You drank from it today.
5. Chicken for dinner was still pink inside.
