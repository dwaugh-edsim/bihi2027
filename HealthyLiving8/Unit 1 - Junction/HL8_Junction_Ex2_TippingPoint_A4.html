7:41 AM. FRIDAY.

Sam's thumb picked the kitchen. The phone went downstairs at 10:39, the charger stayed with it, and the group chat buzzed against a countertop for another twenty minutes with nobody reading it.

That part worked.

The rest of it did not.

Homeroom came and Sam sat with Rae, exactly as promised. It cost what the story said it would cost: forty kids looking at the back of Sam's head all morning, and a squad that has not sent a single message since Thursday. Nobody fought. Nobody said anything. That is the whole punishment.

At second period the teacher hands back the group project sheet. Monday, 8:00 AM. The partner listed next to Sam's name has not been in school since Wednesday. Their section is the one Sam covered during the Thursday fire drill.

Downstairs, the kitchen light was on at 5:15 this morning. It usually isn't.

There was an email at 6:40 from the mortgage company. Sam read the subject line and closed the laptop. That is the whole of what Sam knows about it, and that has been enough to sit in the chest since 6:40 like a stone in a sock. Mom has been picking up extra shifts all week. Sam has been covering the ones that run late, and has told nobody, because telling somebody would mean it was happening to the family and not just to Sam.

Three fires: the chat, the project, the house. Two of them are still burning.

Then the phone buzzes at 7:41 — not the chat, the school email notification, the one that always arrives before first period and never with good news.

📧 SCHOOL EMAIL — MR. HALLETT, MATH
"Hi Sam —
Checking in on the Unit 3 project. I see your partner has been out and I have had no message from you about it, so I wanted to ask early: are you on track to have something to hand in Monday morning?

Let me know today if you need to talk. I can be flexible if there is something going on.

Mr. Hallett
Math & Science"

Nobody at home has asked Sam a single question about school this week. That is not an accident. That is Sam's boundary text, aimed in a different direction, and it is working exactly as well as boundary texts do.

So here is the arithmetic, and it is not dramatic. It is just addition.

The chat is cold and will thaw or freeze on its own, without Sam. The partner is gone and the project is due Monday. At home, something is wrong that Sam is pretending is fine. And now a teacher wants an answer by the end of the day.

Exhibit 1's fire lasted thirty seconds. This one runs until Monday.

Nobody can out-wait a five-day fire. And the smart thing from Exhibit 1 — the thirty-second pause, the phone in the kitchen — still works. It is just nowhere near enough.

A stack does not tip because one thing is enormous. It tips because one more thing arrives after there is nothing left.

The bell rings in ninety seconds.

🔁 THE STUCK ALARM

(Sustained Stress • Allostatic Load • Amygdala.) Exhibit 1's smoke detector was a thirty-second burst — it went off and then it stopped. This one never gets switched off, because the thing causing it is still happening. A body that stays in alarm for days stops being scared of one thing and starts being tired by all of them.

🧯 THE FIRE MARSHAL

(Triage • One Decision • Prefrontal Cortex.) You cannot solve six problems at once, and you cannot out-wait a fire that lasts five days. The pilot is sharpest on a single question at a time. Put one fire out properly, then take the next one.
