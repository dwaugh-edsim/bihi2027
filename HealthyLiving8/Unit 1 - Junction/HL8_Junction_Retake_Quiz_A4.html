Part A · Multiple choice · circle one · 8 marks

  1. In a sudden stressful moment, which part of your brain fires first?
    1. The slow, careful thinking part
    2. The fast, automatic part that acts before you have time to think
    3. The part that stores memories
    4. The part that controls digestion
  2. Which sentence best describes the brain's slow thinking system (the prefrontal cortex)?
    1. It acts instantly, before you notice anything
    2. It takes a while to boot up, but once it is running it makes better choices
    3. It only works when you are alone
    4. It is fully finished growing by age 10
  3. Somewhere around what age is that thinking system considered fully mature?
    1. Age 8
    2. Age 13
    3. Age 16
    4. The mid-twenties
  4. A student sends a message they regret. What is the best first move to stop a small problem from turning into a chain of bigger ones?
    1. Send ten more messages defending themselves
    2. Stop, wait, and let the slow thinking part catch up before acting again
    3. Post the whole argument publicly so people pick a side
    4. Keep scrolling and hope it disappears
  5. Which answer is assertive — clear AND respectful?
    1. Saying nothing and hoping it stops on its own
    2. Yelling “leave me alone!” and slamming the door
    3. “Not doing this tonight. See you tomorrow.”
    4. Going along with it so nobody gets upset
  6. Which of these is a healthy way to cope with stress?
    1. Shutting yourself in your room all weekend
    2. Talking it through with a trusted adult
    3. Yelling at whoever is closest
    4. Staying up all night replaying it in your head
  7. A friend tells you they are in real danger and says “swear you won't tell.” Which statement is true?
    1. A promise always comes first, no matter what
    2. Keeping the secret proves you are loyal
    3. When safety is at stake, you tell a trusted adult, even though it breaks the secret
    4. You should decide by yourself whether the danger is serious enough
  8. Which step belongs in good decision-making?
    1. Act first, think later
    2. List your options and think about the short-term AND long-term results of each
    3. Always do whatever the group is doing
    4. Avoid choosing until someone decides for you
