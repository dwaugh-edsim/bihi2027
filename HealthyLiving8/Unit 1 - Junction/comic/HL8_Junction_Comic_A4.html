Sam's Week — The Junction Comic

A graphic novel for grade 8

One group chat. Fourteen seconds. A stack of fires nobody can see. Sam's Week follows a grade 8 student through the week a single screenshot sets on fire — and the thirty seconds that decide what happens next.

Two questions run through the book: what do you do when staying quiet has a cost? And what do you do when a friend asks you to keep something dangerous quiet?

The Trap

And here is the trap: there are no teachers in a group chat at 10:38 PM. No adult on earth can stop thirty kids from turning their shoulders away in a cafeteria tomorrow.

Walk into Homeroom and sit with Rae, and Sam is radioactive for the rest of grade 8. Walk past Rae, and Sam personally executes their best friend in public.

Friday, 5:15 AM

Nobody at home has asked Sam a single question about school all week. That's not luck. Sam asked them months ago to stay out of school stuff — and they listened completely.

The Project — First

Because it's the only fire with a deadline attached.

Sam writes back before lunch, in complete sentences: here's what's done, here's what the absence put at risk, here's what I still need.

A written plan gets a real answer; a panicked midnight email just spreads the panic. Mr. Hallett turns out to be exactly as flexible as he promised.

Home

At dinner, Sam breaks the wall Sam built.

22

What The Strategist Does

First, listening — mirroring the real emotion without agreeing to the dangerous plan. Rae's hands unclench. Their shoulders drop. Someone finally heard them without judging them.

Then the line, calmly, eyes to eyes.

"Real friendship protects people, not secrets. I am not keeping this quiet. But you are not walking into this alone, either."

Two Rules
EXHIBIT 3

An everyday secret

Protects someone's privacy or feelings — a crush, a bad grade, an embarrassing moment. You keep those private.

A heavy secret

Hides immediate danger to someone's body, health, or safety. You break those immediately.

Two moves start a chain. One move stops one.
The strategist picks the third.
