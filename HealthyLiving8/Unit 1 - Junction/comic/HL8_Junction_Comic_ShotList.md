# Sam's Week — The Junction Comic
### Shot list & panel script · 22 panels

**Cast** (locked via `comic/panels/cast_sheet.jpg`)

| Character | Description |
| :- | :- |
| **Sam** | 13, medium-brown skin, short dark curly hair, heather gray zip hoodie, dark jeans, tan canvas backpack. Deliberately ungendered in the narrative — no pronouns used. |
| **Rae** | 13, light-brown skin, slim, long straight dark hair with heavy bangs, oversized navy hoodie, black leggings, silver hoop earrings. |
| **Mom** | Adult woman, 40s. |
| **Mr. Hallett** | Adult man, 40s, math teacher. |
| **The squad** | Jayden, Chloe, and ~38 background students. |

**Palette (locked)** — deep teal-navy shadows · warm amber key light · muted midtones · one hot alarm red accent.

## ACT ONE — THE TRIGGER
*Exhibits 1 & 2 · Tuesday, 10:38 PM*

**Panel 01 — 10:38 PM**
> Sam's phone buzzes against the math textbook — the one that's supposed to be open.
>
> Once. Then four times in a row. Which means the group chat, not a text.

**Panel 02 — The screenshot**
> Jayden has posted a screenshot. It shows a private message Rae wrote on a bad night — a message about Chloe and Jayden that was never meant for anyone but one person.
>
> Now all forty people have read it.

**Panel 03 — The ultimatum**
> *"u sit next to Rae every single day in Homeroom. Tomorrow morning — you sitting with Rae, or sitting with us?"*

**Panel 04 — The alarm**
> Sam's chest goes tight and hot at the same time. That feeling has a name nobody says out loud: it's the alarm — the fast, automatic part of the brain that reacts before any thinking happens.
>
> It was built for tigers in the woods. It is terrible at 10:38 PM group chats.

**Panel 05 — The trap**
> And here is the trap: there are no teachers in a group chat at 10:38 PM. No adult on earth can stop thirty kids from turning their shoulders away in a cafeteria tomorrow.
>
> Two of those moves start a chain reaction. One of them stops one.

## ACT TWO — THE THIRD OPTION
*Exhibits 2 · Tuesday 10:39 PM → Friday*

**Panel 06 — The floorboard**
> Downstairs, a floorboard creaks — Mom's second lap of the kitchen. Phones after 10:30 is a revoked-charger offense, and Sam's charger is already on probation.
>
> The thumb hovers over the keyboard.

**Panel 07 — The choice**
> Sam picks the move nobody was counting.
>
> No clapback. No speech. No picking a table. The phone goes downstairs to the kitchen instead — face down on the counter, charger and all.

**Panel 08 — The unread buzz**
> For the next twenty minutes the chat buzzes against the countertop, furious and unread. It sounds exactly like it matters.
>
> Nobody is reading it.

**Panel 09 — Homeroom**
> At school, Sam keeps sitting beside Rae in Homeroom. No announcement. No speech. Just showing up in the same seat.

**Panel 10 — The silence**
> The squad doesn't fight. Nobody says a word. They just go quiet.
>
> That silence *is* the message. This is the whole punishment: forty kids looking at the back of Sam's head.

**Panel 11 — 5:15 AM**
> The kitchen light is on at 5:15 in the morning. It usually isn't.

**Panel 12 — The stone**
> At 6:40, an email arrives from the mortgage company. Sam reads only the subject line and closes the laptop.
>
> That single line has been sitting in Sam's chest ever since, like a stone in a sock.

**Panel 13 — The project**
> The teacher hands back the group project sheet. Due Monday, 8:00 AM.
>
> The partner listed next to Sam's name hasn't been in school since Wednesday. Their whole section is the one Sam already started covering alone.

**Panel 14 — The clock**
> The school email chimes. It's Mr. Hallett, Math:
>
> *"Hi Sam — Checking in on the Unit 3 project. I see your partner has been out and I have had no message from you about it, so I wanted to ask early: are you on track to have something to hand in Monday morning? Let me know today if you need to talk. I can be flexible if there is something going on."*
>
> A stack doesn't fall because one thing is enormous. It falls because one more thing lands when there's nothing left to hold it up.

**Panel 15 — Thirty seconds**
> Sam takes thirty seconds first. Not because the problems wait — because the thinking brain needs that long to boot up.
>
> Decisions made on the alarm are always the expensive ones.

**Panel 16 — The reply**
> **The project — first, because it's the only fire with a deadline attached.**
>
> Sam writes back before lunch, in complete sentences: *Here is what's done. Here's what my partner's absence put at risk. Here's what I still need.* A written plan gets a real answer.

**Panel 17 — One true sentence**
> **Home — one true sentence.** At dinner, Sam breaks the wall Sam built:
>
> *"I saw the letter on the laptop. I can take a shift Friday, but I can't do Thursdays — those are my homework nights."*
>
> The stone in the sock doesn't vanish, but it halves in weight the moment someone else is carrying a corner of it.

**Panel 18 — Monday, 8:00 AM**
> **The chat — nothing.** No clapback, no counter-attack, no explaining. On Monday a couple of the squad nod at Sam by the lockers. The shoulders never fully turn away.
>
> The project lands on Mr. Hallett's desk, finished. Sam is tired. But it's the ordinary kind of tired.

## ACT THREE — THE LOYALTY BIND
*Exhibit 3 · Monday, 3:15 PM*

**Panel 19 — The bike racks**
> Monday, 3:15 PM. Outside by the back bike racks, away from the bus loop and the crowded hallways, Rae is waiting.
>
> Hoodie pulled down over their eyes, shoulders hunched inward like someone trying to disappear from the world entirely.

**Panel 20 — The approach**
> *"Sam. You're the only person who didn't abandon me last week. You're the only one who didn't turn your back when Jayden posted that screenshot."*
>
> *"Hey. You made it through Monday. Nobody said anything at lunch."*

**Panel 21 — The promise**
> *"I haven't slept in three days. Every time my phone buzzes, my chest goes tight. I'm meeting some older high school kids by the highway underpass tonight. They said they have stuff that helps you sleep. I'm not going home tonight."*
>
> Rae grabs Sam's jacket sleeve with shaking hands. *"Promise me right now you won't tell anyone. Swear it on everything. You're my best friend — keep my secret."*
>
> Keeping this secret isn't loyalty. It's leaving your best friend alone in the dark.

**Panel 22 — Walking together**
> Sam doesn't argue. First, listening: *"Rae. You haven't slept in three days. You're scared every time your phone buzzes, and you're tired of facing it alone."*
>
> Then the line, calmly, eyes to eyes: *"Real friendship protects people, not secrets. I care about you way too much to let you go to that underpass tonight. I am not keeping this quiet. But you are not walking into this alone, either."*
>
> Two moves start a chain. One move stops one.
>
> Silence wasn't the answer this time. **Courage was.**

## Regeneration notes

* Character identity is held by `comic/panels/cast_sheet.jpg`, passed as `input_urls` on every request. Regenerating a panel means re-uploading the cast sheet and passing the fresh temp URL — uploaded URLs expire after 1 hour, so re-upload before every run.
* The generator returns `.jpg` filenames even when a `.png` name is requested. Use the returned `file_name`.
* **Batch limit is 10 requests per call.** `comic/_work/panels.json` holds 22, so it must be split (this book shipped as 8 + 8 + 6).
* PowerShell execution policy on this machine blocks running `.ps1` files. Run the batch loop inline in the shell rather than as a script file.
* If a panel drifts on character identity, re-run that single request — don't re-run the batch.
* Style is held by the fixed suffix in `comic/_work/panels.json`. Change it there only if you want to re-grade the whole book.

### Known deviations

* **Characters read older than 13.** The bold-ink style hardens facial features; Sam in particular can land around 16–17 in close-ups (panels 21, 01). If age accuracy matters for grade 8, add "soft youthful face, round cheeks, early-teen features" to the style suffix.
* **Panels render with a white page margin.** The HTML crops it via `object-fit: cover`. Harmless.
* **Minor in-image text** appears on props (the "MATHEMATICS" cover on panel 01 is legible and correct). All dialogue and narration is HTML overlay, never generated.

## Print verification

Verified by rendering headless Chromium to PDF and inspecting the result (2026-10-08):

| Check | Result |
| :- | :- |
| Page count | 15 / 15 sheets — PASS |
| Page geometry | every page 595×842 pt = 210×297 mm A4 — PASS |
| Blank / orphan pages | none — PASS |
| Images per spread | 2 — PASS |
| Text layers | present and unclipped on every content page — PASS |

**The pack did not print correctly on the first build.** It came out as **23 pages**, because tall captions pushed past the 297 mm page boundary and fragmented every other spread, leaving stray pages carrying nothing but the corner watermark. Two things fixed it:

1. The `@media print` block sat **above** the base rules. Equal-specificity base declarations win, so `.sheet` kept `margin-bottom: 10mm` and `body` kept its flex centring during print. The print block must be the **last** rule in the stylesheet.
2. `.panel-row` used `flex: 1`, letting tall captions grow the row. It now has a fixed `height: 121mm`, and `.sheet` is 296 mm (1 mm under A4) so sub-pixel rounding cannot push it over.

To re-verify after any edit:

```powershell
# headless Chromium renders honour @page size; pass an isolated profile or it
# attaches to an already-running browser and hangs
$profile = Join-Path $env:TEMP "hl8-print-profile"
msedge --headless=new --no-sandbox --user-data-dir="$profile" `
  --no-pdf-header-footer --print-to-pdf=out.pdf `
  "file:///<path>/HL8_Junction_Comic_A4.html"
```

Then assert `len(PdfReader('out.pdf').pages) == 15`.
