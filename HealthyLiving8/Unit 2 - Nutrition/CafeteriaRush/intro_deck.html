01 · Build the Plate

Canada's Food Guide Plate

Half the Plate · 1/2

Vegetables & Fruit

Apples, berries, grapes, carrots, broccoli, salads, sweet potato — the more colours the better.

Whole fruit beats juice: the fibre slows down sugar absorption.
Quarter · 1/4

Whole Grains

Brown rice, quinoa, oats, whole wheat pita or pasta — brain fuel for steady afternoon focus.

“100% whole grain” must be the FIRST ingredient on the label.
Quarter · 1/4

Protein Foods

Chicken, salmon, eggs, tofu, lentils, beans, yogurt — builds and repairs muscles and keeps you full.

Healthy fats (salmon, avocado, seeds, olive oil) fuel your growing brain.
Drink

Water First

Tap water, plain milk, kefir, or unsweetened soy hydrate without a sugar crash.

Juice, pop, sports drinks, boba and energy drinks are sugar/caffeine traps.

02 · Read Every Nutrition Facts Label

The 5% / 15% DV Rule

5% DV or less A LITTLE
  • Good news when it's sugar, sodium, or saturated fat
  • Always check the serving size first — a bag with 2 servings doubles every number
  • Ingredients are listed by weight, heaviest first
15% DV or more A LOT
  • Great when it's fibre, protein, vitamins — whole grains, beans, fruit, milk
  • Red flag when it's sugar, sodium, or sat fat — one pickle spear can be 23% DV sodium
  • Sugar splitting: glucose-fructose + brown sugar + honey + corn syrup = sugar hiding under 4 names
Quick scan order: 1. Serving size → 2. Calories (energy for your brain and muscles) → 3. % DV on sugar / sodium / sat fat → 4. First 3 ingredients.
03 · Label Traps in the Cafeteria

Looks healthy. Isn't.

“Multigrain” Bread

Multiple refined white flours. Only “100% whole grain” first ingredient counts as a whole grain.

Heavy Syrup & Creamy Dressing

Canned peaches in heavy syrup: 31% DV sugar. Bacon Caesar salad: 39% DV sodium, 45% DV sat fat.

The 30-Second Label Scan

1. Serving size.
2. Sugar / sodium / sat fat % DV.
3. First 3 ingredients.
