Nutrition Basics · Term 1 of 4

Calories: Your Body's Energy Unit

What It Is
Energy Stored in Food
  • Measures fuel, not a "good" or "bad" score.
Why You Need It
Powers Growth & Focus
  • Fuels teen growth, brain focus, and sports.
  • Too few causes fatigue and brain fog.
Examples
Quality Matters Most
  • Steady fuel: chicken wrap, oats, fruit.
  • Empty calories: pop, candy, chips.

Nutrition Basics · Term 2 of 4

Carbs: Fast Crash vs. Slow Fuel

Why You Need Carbs
Main Brain & Muscle Fuel
  • Your #1 energy source for school and sports.
Simple Carbs (Fast Crash)
Quick Spike, Quick Crash
  • Examples: pop, candy, white bread, sugary cereal.
  • Burns fast, leaves you tired 45 minutes later.
Complex Carbs + Fibre (Slow Fuel)
Slow, Steady Energy
  • Examples: oats, brown rice, whole grains, apples, beans.
  • Fibre slows digestion so focus lasts longer.
Visual / Diagram

Clean energy graph: jagged coral spike-and-crash line labelled "Simple Sugar (Pop, Candy)" vs. smooth emerald steady curve labelled "Complex Carbs + Fibre (Oats, Rice, Apples)".

Nutrition Basics · Term 3 of 4

Protein: The Body's Builder

Why You Need Protein
Build, Repair, Stay Full
  • Repairs muscles, builds cells, supports teen growth.
  • Keeps you feeling full longest between meals.
Real Food Examples
Everyday Sources
  • Chicken, eggs, fish, Greek yogurt, cheese.
  • Beans, lentils, tofu, peanut butter, nuts.
Marketing Trap
Watch the Hype
  • Most teens already get enough from meals.
  • Many "protein" bars are candy plus whey.
Visual / Diagram

Split editorial graphic: crisp real protein foods (eggs, chicken, Greek yogurt, beans, almonds) in a glowing emerald frame vs. a flashy neon "20g PROTEIN!" candy bar dripping caramel and sugar cubes.

Nutrition Basics · Term 4 of 4

Fats: Brain Fuel vs. The "Fat-Free" Trap

Why You Need Fat
Brain, Hormones & Vitamins
  • Builds brain cells and helps absorb vitamins.
  • Slows digestion for lasting energy.
Healthy Unsaturated Fats
Real Food Examples
  • Avocado, walnuts, almonds, olive oil, salmon, seeds.
Watch Out
Saturated Fat & "Fat-Free"
  • Limit deep-fried foods and processed meats.
  • "Fat-free" snacks often swap fat for sugar.

Mythbusting

Sugar in Fruit: Is It a Problem?

Whole Food
Whole Apple
  • 19g sugar + 4.4g fibre.
  • Fibre slows sugar absorption down.
Stripped Food
Apple Juice (355 mL)
  • 39g sugar + 0g fibre.
  • Hits your bloodstream like pop.
Key Rule
The Takeaway
  • Fibre is the speed bump.
  • Whole fruit wins every time.
Visual / Diagram

Side-by-side diagram: a crisp red apple behind a glowing emerald gate labelled "FIBRE SPEED BUMP (19g sugar, 4.4g fibre)" vs. an apple juice bottle pouring fast into a coral rush labelled "39g SUGAR, 0g FIBRE".

How Canadian Labels Work

Label Rule 1: Serving Size First

The Math Trap
Shrunken Portions
  • Companies shrink serving sizes on purpose.
  • Front numbers look artificially small.
Real Example
Multiply by What You Eat
  • "140 calories" equals 1/6 of the bag.
  • Whole bag equals 840 calories.
Your Move
Check the Top Line
  • Compare serving size to the full pack.
Visual / Diagram

Zoomed-in Canadian Nutrition Facts header highlighting "Per 30 g (1/6 bag)" in bright amber, beside a chip bag stamped "140 x 6 = 840 CAL".

How Canadian Labels Work

Label Rule 2: The 5% / 15% Rule

Low Number
5% DV or Less = A Little
  • Aim LOW for sugar, sodium, saturated fat.
High Number
15% DV or More = A Lot
  • Aim HIGH for fibre, iron, calcium.
Fast Scan
% Daily Value Column
  • Compare two products in three seconds.
Visual / Diagram

Bold dual gauge graphic: teal badge "5% OR LESS = A LITTLE" and coral badge "15% OR MORE = A LOT" pointing directly at a magnified "% Daily Value" column.

How Canadian Labels Work

Label Rule 3: Ingredient Order & Sugar Splitting

By Weight
Most to Least
  • Listed by weight from #1 to last.
  • First 3 ingredients equal most of the food.
Sneaky Trick
Sugar Splitting
  • Sugar, glucose, honey listed separately.
  • Pushes sugar out of the #1 spot.
Watch Out
Count Every Alias
  • Spot syrups, "-ose" words, juice concentrate.
Visual / Diagram

Funnel diagram showing four separate ingredient tags ("glucose-fructose", "sugar", "brown rice syrup", "honey") combining into one large coral block labelled "REAL #1: SUGAR".

Canada's Food Guide (2019)

Proportions, Not Math

Half Plate
1/2 Vegetables & Fruits
  • Fibre, vitamins, and steady energy.
Two Quarters
1/4 Whole Grains + 1/4 Protein
  • Brown rice, oats, beans, eggs, meat.
Daily Habits
Beyond the Plate
  • Make water your drink of choice.
  • Be aware of food marketing.

Mythbusting & Safety

Fueling vs. Restricting: Two Opposite Traps

Know the Difference
Medical vs. Crash Diets
  • Doctor diets treat allergies or illness.
  • TikTok crash diets harm growing teens.
Both Extremes
Real Health Risks
  • Ultra-processed excess strains heart and blood sugar.
  • Eating disorders harm heart, bones, brain.
Support (NS)
Where Help Lives
  • Weight alone does not show health.
  • Help: Counsellor, 811, Kids Help Phone, NEDIC.
